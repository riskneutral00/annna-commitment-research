## §0. What the engine is

The engine is **the only layer where truth lives**: what is stored, and what deterministically follows from it. Same store state + same query → same answer, always — that determinism is what makes handles trustworthy, scenarios replayable, and the model safely swappable above it.

- **Sole client: the harness.** The engine never talks to the model, a user, or a third party; it never renders and never sends. Anything it cannot answer comes back as *data* (a structured decline, a pending decision) for the harness to route — the engine records questions, it never asks them. One carve, for display only *(founder-ruled 2026-08-06)*: the engine **publishes read-only display projections** — shaped views naming exactly what a screen may see (the guest Shared projection is one; the owner's live board view is another — its selectable set and cross-tenant fence: §1.7b; the FR38 template-bundle projection is a third — a shape-only view of a T2 authoring session's kinds, kind templates, rules, shared and resource shapes, whose selectable set cannot name a counterparty, booking, history, ledger or personal-data field: §1.7a; and the **candidate-shape ghost** is the fourth *(FD-34, ruled 2026-08-21)* — a read-only preview of what an **uninstalled** marketplace shared shape would publish against the owner's real availability, selectable set fenced by construction exactly as §1.7/§1.7a fence theirs, minting nothing — no token digests, no Shared row; `../marketplace/SPEC.md §6` and its D3 are the consumers); and the **effective-policy projection is the fifth** *(2026-08-28, FD-85 pending)* — a read-only view of the complete policy governing one board (grants, covering rules at every scope with their authority level and version-of-record fields, stored answers, elicited thresholds, on-call configuration), fenced by construction like its siblings and consumed by an app read view only: §1.7c) — and the app may subscribe to those *(this carve is one of the app's ruled non-harness runtime edges; the set is listed at its home, `../app/INTERFACES.md §2` — never read the sole-client sentence alone)*. A projection is a **contract, not a copy**: whether it is materialized or computed on read is a substrate decision (`BUILD.md` Step 0). Projections accept no writes and answer no questions; every command still enters through the harness. The point is leak-prevention by construction: what a projection omits was never in the readable set, so no access rule has to be right for it to stay unseen.
- **Poka-yoke stance:** the invariants in §10 hold **by construction of the store and the write path**, not by post-hoc validation. Illegal states are unrepresentable or unconstructable, per case.
- **Determinism is a reliability property, not a preference — and it is why the hard work lives here** *(founder-ruled 2026-08-08, FD-16)*. The engine performs the pass: every candidate, every rule, every constraint, evaluated in one place. The model never does. **The reason is arithmetic, and it is why this rule has no convenient exception:** an agent's per-step success rate compounds, so 95% per step is ~60% over ten steps and ~28% over twenty-five. A week placed across instructors, boats, pools, tanks and gear sets is that many steps and more — run through the model it is a coin flip; run through this layer it is deterministic and replayable. **The four-layer split is therefore a reliability argument, not a tidiness argument about layers.** What binds: **no placement, availability, capacity, quota, buffer, travel or ordering computation may move into the harness or the model — for any case, however small.** A single exception re-prices every scenario above it at the compounding rate, and the exception will always look cheap at the moment it is proposed. Where judgment is genuinely needed the model supplies *inputs* to this layer — intent, parameters, ranked preferences — and this layer decides. *(The research that established the curve is cited from the registry entry, not from here: a `SPEC` is the top authority tier and does not depend on a path outside the repo.)*
- **Never outward:** the `destruction` and `value-transfer` reversibility classes have **no entry point** in this layer (no delete operation exists, §1.10; no operation moves value, §1.9).

## §1. The stored object model (the schema of record)

The shapes below restate `../harness/SPEC.md §3` from the engine's side — the harness defined *what exists*; this section is the stored record plus the engine-owned details the harness deliberately left open.

### §1.1 Principal & party
- A principal = identity + attributes + (for bookable ones) a board. Every stored object carries its owning tenant (`owner_org`); nothing leaks across tenants at the store level.
- **A counterparty's own constraints are not a new object class.** "Harold will never move," "Mrs. Ito only Tuesday mornings" are **rules from the menu (§3)** — `pin` and `location-window` — with `target: party`. They store, version, and evaluate like every other rule; what's special is only their force in `resolve` (§7: filtered before candidates exist).
- **A party may later become a principal — the conversion link (FD-46, 2026-08-22).** When a person who has been a counterparty on other tenants' boards opens their own account, the engine records a **party↔principal link** `{party_refs[], principal, basis: verified-channel claim, when}` — minted only by the security layer's claim act (`../security/SPEC.md §2`), never caller-constructable (I4's discipline applied to identity). The link moves nothing and re-homes nothing: every record keeps its owning tenant. What it grants is exactly the **union of what the linked party's booking-bound tokens could already read** — their own bookings, attestations, and participation history (`../app/SPEC.md §5`'s manage-state projection) — assembled onto the new principal's board as its starting history, **over unrevoked, unexpired tokens only, one linked principal per verified channel** (FD-51/FD-76: the security claim's temporal and identity bounds govern this union; an ambiguous shared channel mints no link). Conversion adds no read power; gated at `../security/SCENARIOS.md` N6. **The mint's seam path, named (2026-08-31):** the claim act lands as an ordinary `commit` — the security layer's verified-channel claim writes the link through the existing write verb under its own write id (the §1.13/§7.1 precedent: engine-minted on an existing verb, caller-unconstructable because the input form is the security claim's own, never a caller-suppliable payload). No new verb, no link-specific entry point. **The basis is the claim's own recorded verification, and this link carries no presented-token discriminator** *(pointer aligned 2026-09-11 with `../security/SPEC.md §2`, whose clarified paragraph owns the rule)*: `basis` resolves to that layer's recorded verified-channel act, so **no member of this shape, and no input the claim path could carry, identifies a party by which token was presented** — a presented token is a credential, never identity evidence. A verified channel mapping to more than one party does not mint: the engine stores no ambiguous, partial or provisional link, and B8 asserts the absence.

### §1.2 Board
- Fields per harness §3: `id`, `owner`, `owner_org` (§1.1 — every stored object), **`capacity | balance`**, `attributes {…}`, **`empty_means: free | unknown`**, `zone`.
- **Capacity math — recorded admission and expansion:** a commitment consumes `quantity` for its recorded occupying interval. Admission and expanding interval edits check that concurrent consumption including the proposed draw does not exceed capacity over the span being admitted or expanded, using the recorded intervals and unresolved draws at the supplied reference instant; bound buffers also constrain placement (§3). The unresolved-draw and per-kind completion definition is solely `../harness/SPEC.md §3.4` (FD-98). Availability uses the same accounting as admission; commit checks it atomically (§6.1), and interval edits follow §6.7. Person = 1, pool = N, fleet = N units.
  Elapsed time alone can make an unresolved predecessor overlap a previously admitted successor: a successor admitted beyond the predecessor's scheduled end and bound buffer remains recorded when the predecessor becomes overdue without a report. This is recorded accounting, not an assertion of physical possession or enforcement. It is neither an all-time capacity guarantee nor a requirement that every write erase historical overlap. Overlap alone authorizes no automatic successor move, cancellation, park or outward effect; independently configured permitted follow-ups retain their ordinary authority and validation requirements (`../harness/SPEC.md §7`).
- **Balance math:** cumulative draw-down over a period (the class-pack: 10 dives / 3 months). A board may carry both; they evaluate independently.
- `empty_means: unknown` (the default for proxy boards) **never counts as free** in any availability computation — availability then comes from confirmation, not assumption.
- **What a proxy board is** *(2026-08-29 — the term was used in the bullet above and turned on by `SCENARIOS.md` P5, and no file in `engine/` said what makes a board one; the non-proxy default was likewise unstated, so a builder could not set `empty_means` for an ordinary board either)*: a **proxy board** is a board whose occupancy the tenant does not author — it stands for a resource or a person whose real schedule lives elsewhere, so its emptiness is an **absence of information** rather than a statement of availability. That is the reading the bullet above already presumes when it says availability comes from confirmation. **Both defaults, stated:** a proxy board defaults to `empty_means: unknown`; a board the tenant authors defaults to `empty_means: free`. `empty_means` is a stored field either way, so the default is a **creation-time value, not a derived property** — a board's proxy-ness is expressed *through* `empty_means` and is not a second stored flag.
- `zone`: patterns store zone-relative times; instances store absolute instants; all intersection math runs in UTC. **The zone authority, stated once:** a board's `zone` is its owner's declared home zone (asked once, at board creation) — the default for new patterns and for owner-facing display projections; guest pages display in the *board's* zone and say so on the page, so a guest never reads a silently-converted time. **A goal's zone, for a goal touching more than one board** *(2026-08-22 — "the goal's zone" and "the board's stored zone" were both singular over goals that are not, and two builders would pick differently)*: it is the zone of the board the goal's `consumes` names **first under the goal's own declared ordering**; for a cross-owner offer (§7.1) it is the **initiator's** board's zone, and the counterparty's page displays in their own board's zone — the guest-page rule generalized. DST at pattern edges: §9.

### §1.3 Commitment
The anatomy of `../harness/SPEC.md §3.4`, stored exactly — plus `owner_org` like every stored object (§1.1): `title` (the only universally required field) · `kind` or `exception{reason, by}` (M2: silent-untyped-on-governed is unrepresentable) · `attributes` per kind schema · time fields with **roles** (`start{value, role}`, `end{value, role}`, `actual_end` free — early return is normal) · `temporal_type` and `completed` **derived**, never written · `consumes[{board_ref, quantity}]` · `depends_on[]` · `party` · `order` · `preconditions[]` carrying **evidence** `{principal, at, evidence}` · `expires_at` · per-field `provenance`.

Engine-owned specifics:
- **`place` — one optional attribute type, general.** A declared address plus at most one resolved geo-reference. Any commitment may carry one; nothing else about location is an object. (No GIS: no polygons or route geometry stored; §5 stores only travel inputs and estimate facts.)
- **Latches are write-once.** The three decision latches `confirmed_at / cancelled_at / declined_at` are each `{by, at}`; **`expired_at` is `{at}`** — the clock's latch, with no author member to store; its attribution is the per-field provenance entry with `author: engine` (§1.3 below), and the one shape's home is `../harness/SPEC.md §3.4` *(aligned 2026-08-31 — this sentence previously printed a second shape against the home)*. The store **rejects any write that clears or rewrites a set latch**. Status follows `../harness/SPEC.md §3.4`'s transition table: `confirmed_at` remains history while authorized cancellation or per-kind completion determines the later status; it does not win against them. Without a terminal outcome, start and overdue explicit completion derive `active` and `review` respectively. Preserve the same computed status in display projections; the app adds no precedence.
- **The park is stored, and only a human clears it.** `needs_human {reason, since, trigger_ref, cleared_by{principal, at}?}` (harness §3.4) stores alongside `status`, never replacing it. The store **rejects a `cleared_by` whose author is `engine` or `llm`** — clearing is a human act or it does not happen. Uncleared parks are queryable as a set, because the app must surface them regardless of what the commitment's status derives to.
- **The bound buffer operand is a stored member** *(2026-08-31 — §3's `buffer` row binds "the gap requirement a committed placement carries" at its own commit, and no member carried it, so `SCENARIOS.md` T5's "both neighbours evaluate the same value" had no value to be the same)*: a committed placement stores the buffer operand in force at its commit — a §2 duration or computed-travel form, engine-attributed provenance — and that stored value is what both neighbours evaluate simultaneously (T5) and what a later operand edit neither shortens nor lengthens (harness F6). The binding time's home stays §1.5/§3; this member is the storage of it.
- **Provenance includes `author: engine`** — computed values (a travel gap, a materialized instance, a resolved geo-ref) are attributed to the engine, distinguishable from customer-, template-, and model-authored values. Needed for override precedence (§5) and replay.

### §1.4 Recurrence — pattern + materialized instances
- **`Pattern { shape, zone-relative time, ends?, horizon_policy }`** stored once; the engine **materializes real Commitment rows** over a rolling horizon. Each instance has its own id, latches, parties, preconditions — cancel one, complete one, move one.
- **Routine is the user-facing name for a Pattern and its identified occurrences**, not a third `temporal_type`, rule type or stored primitive. Each occurrence remains an ordinary Commitment whose Event/Task type derives from its time roles (`../harness/SPEC.md §3.4`). A timed Run has occupying start/end; a due-only preparation Task does not acquire an occupying interval merely from its deadline. Routine confirmation and scope elicitation belong to `../harness/SPEC.md §6`.
- **The `shape` vocabulary is a closed menu, same law as §3's:** `daily` (= all seven weekdays; the form's weekday unticks narrow it) · `weekly {days: [mon…sun]}` · `monthly-by-date {date: 1–31 | last}` — each with optional `interval: N` (every N days/weeks/months; default 1) and optional `except: [dates]` (excluded instances are never materialized). A shape outside the menu is **unstorable**, exactly like an off-menu rule type (§1.5). `ends?` is `{until: date} | {count: N}`; `horizon_policy` is the materialization look-ahead, **default 8 weeks**, per-pattern overridable.
- An instance records its generating pattern and generation version. **A pattern edit is forward-only:** instances **carrying state** — any latch, any attached party, any satisfied-precondition evidence — are never touched; bare materialized drafts are regenerated under the new shape.
- **Occurrence identity survives materialization.** Retain the generating Pattern ref, generation version and original scheduled local date/time in its zone beside the occurrence id; a moved occurrence keeps that original identity. Expansion checks existing instances, terminal history and attributed dated exceptions against the full original slot identity already retained here — the generating Pattern ref, original scheduled local date/time and zone, and any existing occurrence id — never a collapsed `Pattern/date` key; generation/version remains a separate staleness and stateful-instance check. Overlapping windows, retries and a later generation cannot recreate a cancelled occurrence or erase its latch. That cancellation guard is generation-independent: a new generation does not authorize recreating the cancelled original slot. Bare-draft regeneration retains the supersession history below and any accepted dated exception; it cannot turn an explicit user edit into discarded scaffolding. An alternating shape retains its `interval` and stored anchor date in the Pattern's zone-relative schedule; when the anchor is not supplied or established by a covering answer, it is a gap, never the current week by default.
- **Dated and usual edits have separate checked targets.** A dated exception binds the occurrence id, generating Pattern/generation and full date including year and zone; scope is not a weekday label. Verify supplied calendar components through §2 before writing: a weekday/date mismatch, ambiguous year/zone or multiple matching occurrences requires clarification. Revalidate the target generation/version at commit (§6); a stale selection cannot silently select a replacement. Usual destination/travel settings are an ordinary forward-only Pattern edit; a one-date origin/departure/buffer edit is attributed to that occurrence. Saving a named place is separate from choosing which occurrences use it. Such an exception is not the governing-kind `exception{reason, by}` escape in §1.3 and grants no rule bypass.
- **Stop from a date is a checked forward scope**, inclusive of that local date in the Pattern's zone. Show the Pattern boundary and the concrete already-stored future instances and dated exceptions, including moved instances whose original occurrence date falls in scope and records beyond the loaded horizon. Bound future generation with the existing `ends` restriction; do not expand an infinite series to enumerate it. Apply only the user-accepted scope through ordinary authorized diffs, rechecking generation, dependencies and current state under §6. Matching bare drafts withdraw under the disposition below; future instances or exceptions carrying state retain their facts until the authorized user resolves each through its lawful lifecycle. Unresolved instances remain visible with the existing stranded-instance clock registration/park; never report the whole series stopped while those decisions remain. Past and in-flight commitments, their bound terms and history survive. A dated exception cannot silently escape the stop boundary or be deleted to simulate resolution. This does not change the existing forward-only Pattern edit law: resolving a stateful instance is a separate authorized act, never a bulk engine cancellation.
- **Regeneration's disposition for superseded bare drafts, named** *(2026-08-21 — every removal mechanism was previously forbidden: no hard delete (§1.10), no engine-authored `cancelled_at` (§0 never-asks), no leaving them (this bullet))*: a superseded bare draft is **withdrawn** — a generation-versioned, engine-attributed terminal event (`superseded_by_generation`) that releases its capacity draw and removes it from every projection; the row survives as history (§1.10 holds). Withdrawal is legal precisely because a bare draft carries no promise to anyone — it is engine-authored scaffolding, distinguishable by provenance (§1.3), so §0's never-asks law is untouched: no instance carrying state is ever withdrawn. **`except` dates added to an existing pattern withdraw matching bare drafts the same way**; an instance carrying state on a newly-excluded date — the **`except`-stranded** case — **stays, and adding the `except` date over it writes a `TriggerRegistration` (§1.15, the registry's home) at the affected instant** — the same mechanism §9's conflicted draft uses — whose firing surfaces the stranded instance as an ordinary parked question, arriving as the harness's ordinary clock source with no new trigger kind (`SCENARIOS.md` N1) *(registered form 2026-08-29 — the prior next-firing phrasing is the one §9 records as fatal: on a board with no scheduled firings before the horizon edge, the next firing never comes, each law is obeyed, and the outcome is a silent drop)*. The generation-time-only reading of `except` is dead.
- Horizon extension is one **internal engine job with two arming paths**: the rolling clock and §5's beyond-horizon request (availability read or board subscription). Both use the same deterministic, idempotent write path and supplied reference instant (§5). The job has **no new seam surface and no harness-callable verb**; its real preparation effects are declared at §5 and `../harness/INTERFACES.md §1.1`, not invisible to the caller's contract.
- The request arming path of that same job **materializes on demand, bounded to the requested window** (a request names its window, so materialization is always finite). The request/preparation boundary is §5; §9 retains conflict and quota/capacity behavior.

### §1.5 Rule
- Authored kind field definitions follow `../harness/SPEC.md §3.3`: persist stable schema-slot identity and revision separately from labels, supported type, required status, scoped defaults and dependency bindings. Ordinary values/provenance retain their bound slot revision. This is versioned schema data through existing diffs, not a new core object or a change to §1.10’s physical object-shape rule. Rule operands bind those slots by reference, never by label; calculation inputs and current references retain their versions. Existing terms follow the binding-time list below.
- Fields per harness §3.5: `id` · `authority: governing | org | individual` · `owner_org` · `target: board | commitment-kind | audience | party` · `type` · `operand` (the complete rule operand at §3, with §2 typed leaves) · `enabled` · `effective_from / supersedes` · provenance.
- **`type` must come from the closed menu (§3). An off-menu type is *unstorable*** — "I can't enforce that yet" is a storage fact, not agent politeness.
- **Binding time, stated here once for every type** *(the per-row claim this bullet used to make was false for eleven of twelve rows and is withdrawn — this list is the home)*: **bind at booking**, never re-evaluated against later edits — `pricing`, `duration`, the booked shape, the commitment's no-show policy (§1.9), and **`hold`** *(moved here 2026-08-21: the response clock reads its operand as of booking; a later operand edit can neither shorten a running clock nor fire `expired_at` retroactively — §3's row states the placement-change behavior)*. **Re-evaluate on placement change** — `buffer`, `travel-override` (§3's row — it feeds `buffer`'s computed form, so it re-evaluates when `buffer` does), `capacity`, `balance`, `quota`, `location-window`, `attribute-domain`, `admission`/`qualification`, `dependency`/`fallback`, `pin`. **Evaluated at their own point** — `precondition` (status derivation), `min-occupancy` (its clock trigger, reading the **current** operand: it is placement-safety, not a bound term — its trigger registers when the run's first booking commits, at `start − decide_by` read then, and **re-registers when `min` or `decide_by` is edited**, so an edit between booking and trigger has exactly one observable effect: the trigger moves and the current values govern at the instant it fires).
- **The bound quote — conditional rate in, one lesson total out** *(2026-09-11; the operand's shape is §3's `pricing` row, the money encoding and its rounding are §1.9's, and this is the binding-time home for what a booking actually carries)*. A `pricing` operand may state its rate **conditionally on the count of booked participants**: each tier carries an explicit `{currency, amount}` money value (§2) and an explicit **per-hour basis**, and an amount with no confirmed currency or basis is **unstorable** under §2's boundary refusal — the harness elicits both before an hourly amount is read back as settled (`../harness/SPEC.md §6`, the readback home). What binds at booking is the **quote**: the tier the booked participant count selects, plus the **lesson total** derived from it as the tier rate multiplied by the booked duration in hours, in §2's typed arithmetic, rounded half-up **once at the final derived amount** per §1.9. **The count chooses the tier; the total is never multiplied by the count** — one lesson booked for four participants derives one lesson's total at the four-participant tier, not four totals and not one total times four. A quote carries a **revision**, the bound quote is its current revision, and §1.9's base derivation of `owed` reads exactly that.
- **Replacing a bound quote is one explicit validated act — never an edit, and never an inference** *(2026-09-11 — `pricing` bound a booked price "forever", and nothing above said how a booking whose people explicitly re-agree ever changes; the answer is not an exemption for pricing edits but a named act carrying its own checks)*. A commit proposing a new quote on an existing booking carries, together: the **proposed roster, time, rate and total**; the **recorded agreement** covering exactly those affected people and that exact proposed act (ordinary attributed commitment data whose home is `../harness/SPEC.md §3.6`); the acting principal's **current authority**; and the **expected current quote revision**. All four are re-checked inside §6 item 1's single transaction against the store as of now, and any mismatch — a stale revision, an agreement naming a different roster, time, rate or total, an authority revoked or suspended since the agreement was recorded — **refuses the whole candidate** through the ordinary envelope, with no partial roster, term, draw or history change. On success the **previous quote and the source it was replaced from stay readable** on §1.10's append-only version chain, and **one logical booking survives**: the same commitment, its single teaching reservation and its participant draws are revised inside that transaction, never cancelled and re-created (§6's shared-request paragraph carries the same identity rule for the roster half).
- **A prospective template edit and a re-agreement on one booking are different acts — and a report is neither** *(2026-09-11)*. Editing the kind's `pricing` rule governs bookings made after the edit and changes **no** bound quote: the binding-time list above is unchanged, and FD-75's standing-series ask with it. Replacing a bound quote reaches exactly the one booking the agreed act names. **A reported fact cannot reprice.** A reported attendance, a reported `actual_end` (`../harness/SPEC.md §3.4`) and a recorded payment mark (§1.9) are attributed facts, and none of them carries the replacement act's required members — so there is no path from a report to a new quote, an absence by construction rather than a validation that has to be right. What any of those outcomes then costs is settled under **Quote marks and attendance outcomes are separate state** below, this section's home for that distinction; nothing re-derives the quote from who turned up.
- **Quote marks and attendance outcomes are separate state** *(2026-09-11)*. The `priced` mark is the money-bearing mark for the booking's current bound quote revision; an accepted replacement appends a new `{amount, currency, by, at}` `priced` mark with that new revision on the same history chain. The expected current quote revision is handle-typed, so a stale revision refuses as `invalid/expired-handle`. This is a recorded term change, not value movement, and it does not set `paid`, `held` or `settled`. An **unknown attendance** outcome — no attendance fact and no human no-show mark — keeps the ordinary bound-quote derivation unchanged. Only an owner or other authorized human act marking `no-show` activates the separately configured no-show policy bound at booking: its full-rate arm reads the current bound quote/`priced` revision, while custom and deposit-forfeit arms read their own bound policy and stored deposit evidence. A no-show policy never turns an unknown attendance outcome into a no-show.
- **Replacement refusal mapping uses the existing envelope** *(2026-09-11; vocabulary home `../harness/INTERFACES.md §7.1`)*: malformed members are `invalid/schema-mismatch`, incompatible typed leaves are `invalid/type-mismatch`, a stale handle-typed quote revision is `invalid/expired-handle`, an agreement that does not cover the exact proposed act is `refused/no-basis`, and revoked or suspended acting authority is `refused/credential-invalid`. No pricing-specific conflict or authorization reason is added.
- Governing-authority rules cannot be removed or disabled by a lower-authority diff (§6.3).

### §1.6 Grant
- `{ action_class, operation, scope, conditions, expiry, revocable }` — the `../harness/SPEC.md §7` shape, stored exactly, attributed. The engine **stores and matches** — it answers "does a covering, unexpired, unrevoked grant exist for this act?" as a pure lookup. The *decision to act* is the harness floor's, never the engine's.
- Revocation is a latched act (stored, attributed, forward-only), not a delete. `revocable` states whether that path exists for this grant — elicited harness-side; the store only honors it.
- The lookup consumes the canonical act descriptor carried by `../harness/INTERFACES.md §1.3`; operation derivation and the exact covering relation belong to `../harness/SPEC.md §7`. Reuse §§2–3's typed comparisons over the actual interval and party/subject chain. Refuse malformed operation/condition shapes under §2's unknown-member rule; well-formed nonmatches return `null`. Persist the confirmed conditions with the Grant; a mutable rule edit cannot rewrite them. Storage does not add an authorization path or enlarge the exact §7.1 ShareGrant carve.

### §1.7 Shared
- A published projection: `{ content_ref, audience, scope, per-recipient token digest entries }` (plaintext tokens are never stored — `../security/SPEC.md §3`). **A digest entry retains `{digest, bound_to: commitment_ref | null}` and carries the per-recipient binding metadata at `../security/SPEC.md §3`** *(2026-08-22 — the list was flat, so FD-43's booking-bound manage token had no field to bind to and §3's "bound to the booking being changed" rule had nothing to read)*: `bound_to` names the request/booking a scoped manage token reads or governs (`../harness/SPEC.md §3.6`), `null` for entry-class tokens that govern none. A token attributes exactly one return (`on_form_return` carries it). A temporary `single-visitor` digest is separate custody from a Shared's per-recipient or booking-bound manage entries: its record carries `issued_at` and the stored `unused` predicate required by `../security/SPEC.md §3`, while Shared entries retain their declared lifecycles and bindings.
- **The temporary record is not domain history:** security's narrow cleanup may remove only an expired `single-visitor` record whose stored `unused` predicate is still true, in the same authoritative operation that arbitrates first activity, hold creation and booking. It cannot remove a Shared, active hold, required continuation, completed booking, history or booking-bound management access; no general engine deletion operation follows from this exception.
- **The board-blind projection is an engine guarantee:** a Shared's answer set is computed availability only. No blocking commitment, no reason, no third party's name or address can appear in it — enforced by what the projection query is *able* to select, not by filtering afterward.
- **In the grant vocabulary (§7.1, the normative home): a Shared *is* the `availability`-rung grant rendered, and a per-recipient token is that grant held anonymously by its bearer.** Wider visibility is a higher rung the owner grants — never a different mechanism.

The board-blind availability projection above is untouched: it still cannot select a mark, a balance, or anyone's standing.

**Group request custody** follows `../harness/SPEC.md §3.6`. This is storage/validation on the existing paths, not another projection or event. Group metadata is not a group-held ShareGrant; §7.1's per-person/per-token grant rule stands.

### §1.9 Money marks
- Money is a typed value (§2); `priced / owed / paid / held / settled` are **latched marks** `{by, at}`. **A mark carries its money value as the printed member** *(2026-08-31 — the marks had no amount anywhere)*: each mark records `{amount, currency}` in §2's `money` encoding — integer ISO-4217 minor units, never floating point — so the marks aggregate (§5 item 6) reads typed values, never strings. **`owed` is derived** from commitment state, never an independently mutable number — cancel the visit and the record corrects itself (the $140 never owed). **The base derivation, printed (FD-94, 2026-08-31):** `owed` derives **from the booking** — the commitment's bound `pricing` term (§1.5: binds at booking) applied to the booked shape — plus the policy-derived charges a human mark triggers (FD-30's marks-first law below, unchanged); **`paid` records beside the derivation and never mutates it**. **The deposit-forfeit amount's stored source, while `held`'s trigger stays open:** the `no-show` policy's *deposit forfeit* arm derives its amount from the deposit recorded in the `payment` precondition's satisfaction **evidence** (§1.3's evidence member) — a stored source that exists today; when the deposits sitting rules `held` (below), the mark and that evidence must agree, and the sitting owes the reconciliation sentence. **Its rounding, stated here because §2 says it must be** *(2026-08-29 — §2's money row obliges each derivation home to state one and no home did)*: `owed` derives in the currency's **ISO-4217 minor units** (§2's representation rule), and any division — a partial period, a pro-rated cancellation credit — rounds **half-up** **once, at the final derived amount**, never per intermediate term. Rounding per intermediate term is how two builders get different totals from one rule.
- **`priced` follows the current bound quote revision; replacement details live at §1.5** *(2026-09-11)*. Its money value and `{by, at}` attribution are read from the current `priced` mark on the append-only quote-history chain; the replacement act appends that mark without moving value.
- **`held` is the fifth mark** (founder-ruled 2026-08-07, D-B), and it was missing here while three files above this one in authority already carried it — `../harness/SPEC.md §3.8`, `../user-stories/README.md`, `../security/SPEC.md §2`. This line conforms to them; it is not a new decision. **What is still unspecified, deliberately not invented here:** what sets and clears the mark. The obvious pairing is the hold vocabulary (`../security/SPEC.md §3`: hold creation, hold expiry), but no file states it, so no file should be read as if it did — the mark exists, its trigger is an open question for whoever specifies deposits. **And until that ruling exists, no layer may set `held`: a write attempting it is refused** *(2026-08-22 — the poka-yoke that keeps the declared gap from being closed wrongly; the obvious wiring is the natural build and nothing else gated against it)*.
- **`no-show` is always a human mark (founder-ruled 2026-08-07, #5).** The system may surface the fact — scheduled start passed, no check-in — but never declares a no-show itself; the owner marks it, attributed `{by, at}`. What a no-show then costs is the commitment's **no-show policy**, a creator-set field (nothing / deposit forfeit / full rate / custom) that, being money terms, **binds at booking** under §1.5 — edits govern forward only. The ledger records what the bound policy says is owed; collecting it stays outside (money tracked, never moved). **Rounding, per §2's obligation:** `full rate` needs none; `custom` and any percentage form round **half-up** **once, at the final derived amount**.
- **The mark latches the money state (FD-30, ruled 2026-08-21).** Once `no-show` is marked, `owed` derives from **the mark and the bound policy**, and no later state change — a counterparty self-cancel included — erases it: a derived value never un-derives a recorded human judgment. Before this line, K1's "cancel and the record corrects itself" and K3's owner-marked forfeit were both MUST on the same commitment with nothing ordering them; the order is now printed — marks first, then state. K1's correction applies only to records carrying no such mark. **The closed menu gains no cancellation-fee type**: what cancelling late costs is the no-show policy's territory and, beyond it, template configuration (FR37) — never platform law.
- **No operation in this layer moves value.** The `value-transfer` class isn't forbidden here — it is *unconstructable*: no such entry point exists.

### §1.11 Proposal (new object — the reshuffle deliverable)
```
Proposal {
  id, trigger,                       // the event that opened the day (a cancellation, an insertion)
  direction: toward-start | toward-end,
  moves: [{ commitment, from, to, moved_party, confirmation: pending|confirmed|declined }],
  untouched: [{ commitment, reason }],   // e.g. "pin rule", "party window", "governing rule"
  freed: [{ window, decision: pending | keep-blocked | reopen }],
  computed_at: store_version, expires
}
```
- A Proposal is **stored, first-class** — it must survive the round-trip (owner approves → moved parties confirm) and be re-checkable when applied.
- **It never applies itself.** Applying is a `commit` variant (§6.5) that re-validates everything as of *now* and requires each move's stored confirmation. A declined move changes nothing.
- **`freed` windows fail closed:** not bookable until the owner's keep-vs-reopen answer is stored. The engine records the pending decision (the harness's elicitation reads and routes it); reclaimed time cannot be booked out from under the owner by default. `decision` is a **closed set** — an unrecognized member is refused at the boundary under §2's unknown-member rule, never treated as `pending`.

### §1.12 Kind template — the multi-day shape (F7, resolved 2026-08-06 — **Claude-drafted mechanism; per FR37 no founder ruling is owed**: the course content is user-built, and the proof is the founder building real courses as a user — `../RULINGS.md` §Provenance)

*The marketplace backlog's open item (F7, that backlog since **folded** away): "the saved course fills the whole assembly at once" was not representable — `dependency` carries only a board-ref list, and the archive's `composition` mechanism was dropped without a recorded resolution. This is the resolution.*

**The ruling, in one line: the shape is authoring-time vocabulary, and it expands into `order` + `depends_on`. There is still no third mechanism.**

That sentence is the whole design, and §1.8's canonical structure is the reason for it. A saved course is **not** a new way to compose commitments at runtime — it is a **template that writes the composition you would otherwise have authored by hand.** Adding a live composition object would have been the third mechanism §1.8 rules out.

```
KindTemplate {
  id, kind, owner_org,
  sessions: [ {
      label,                              // "Day 1 — pool"
      offset: { days, at? },              // relative to the assembly's anchor day
      duration,
      consumes: [{ board_ref | board_role, quantity }],
      requires: [ session_label ]         // becomes depends_on at expansion
  } ],
  anchor_policy : first-session | named-date
}
```

- **Offsets are relative, never absolute.** A four-day course is `+0, +1, +2, +3` from its anchor. The same template books in March and in November without editing.
- **`board_role` resolves at expansion.** A template says *"a boat"* and *"an instructor,"* not *which* boat — the role binds to a concrete board when the course is placed, which is what lets one template serve a shop whose fleet changes.
- **Expansion is a pure authoring act.** Instantiating a template produces N ordinary Commitments under **one Order** (§1.8), sequenced by `depends_on` per `requires`. After expansion **nothing distinguishes a template-authored course from a hand-authored one** — same objects, same rules, same cancellation semantics, same partial-cancel behaviour. `provenance` records `author: template` (§1.3), and that is the only trace.
- **A template stores no times and no availability.** It cannot: placement is `resolve`'s job (§7's multi-day decomposition), run per day at expansion. A template that carried times would be a second scheduler.
- **Editing a template is forward-only.** Already-expanded courses are untouched — the same law as a recurrence pattern edit (§1.4). A customer's booked course does not change because the shop rewrote its curriculum.
- **A template is not a Recurrence.** A pattern repeats *one* commitment on a cadence; a template lays out *several different* sessions once. They share nothing but a sense of "shape," and conflating them was part of why this item stayed open.
- **Partial placement is expected, not exceptional.** If day 3 has no boat, §7's decomposition returns three placements and one structured decline; the Order carries what placed. The template does not require all-or-nothing — the owner does, or doesn't (§7).

### §1.13 Escalation & on-call — stored, not held in harness memory (engine half; gated by B4)

*The `OnCall` and `Escalation` object **shapes** are the harness's — `../harness/SPEC.md §3.9` is their normative home. This section is only the engine's **storage** of them, because an `Escalation` must survive between the trigger that raises it and the `total_timeout` that parks it (different clock firings), and context is assembled from stored structure, never accumulated conversation (`../harness/SPEC.md §8`). It cannot live in harness memory; the engine holds it like every other stored object.*

- **`OnCall`** — board-owner-scoped configuration (`ranked[]`, `step_timeout`, `total_timeout`, per-rung channels/quiet-hours), stored addressably like any owner setup answer (elicited harness-side, §6 ask-once). An owner with no list is the ordinary single-operator case, stored as an empty ladder — not an error.
- **`Escalation`** — first-class stored object: `commitment`, `reason`, `raised_at`, `ladder_state {rung, notified_at}`, `status: open | answered | timed_out_parked`, `answered_by -> principal?`. **`answered_by` is a reference to a principal, not an inline compound** — the `{who, basis, when}` triple lives on the **resulting act**, which is where the harness defines it (`../harness/SPEC.md §7`, the single normative home; §0 above says this section restates the harness and does not redefine it). Storing the triple here would have been a second, divergent home for an attribution the floor already stamps. The harness's ladder walk advances `ladder_state`; the engine **persists each advance**, so a replay reads the same ladder state (determinism, §0).
- **Terminal status is write-once.** `answered` and `timed_out_parked` latch under §1.3's latch discipline — the store **rejects a write that reverts a set terminal status**, so an escalation cannot be silently reopened. `timed_out_parked` co-occurs with the commitment's `needs_human` park (§1.3), which only a human clears — the loop can never un-park itself.
- **Reached through the existing verbs — no new seam verb.** The harness writes the object, advances the ladder, and latches the terminal status through `commit`; it reads open escalations and the on-call list through `calculate`. There is **no escalation-specific engine entry point** — the same guard the cross-owner share obeys (`../harness/SCENARIOS.md I2`; `../harness/INTERFACES.md §4`). *Notification itself is never authority* — the engine stores who was notified when; a basis is created only by the human's answer, captured the ordinary way (harness §7).

### §1.14 PendingDecision — the question the engine records and never asks

*§0's law says anything the engine cannot answer comes back as data — "a structured decline, a pending decision" — for the harness to route. The structured decline has an object. The pending decision did not, and an instruction with no object is not implementable (`../harness/SPEC.md §3.9`, verbatim precedent). This is that object, built as the **general primitive** rather than the `min-occupancy` case, because §0's law is general.*

```
PendingDecision {
  id, commitment,
  raised_by,                          // the rule type or trigger that raised it (e.g. `min-occupancy`)
  choices: [ … ],                     // CLOSED set, ENGINE-NAMED — the whole menu, no free text
  chosen { choice, by, at }?          // absent until an authenticated human answers; human-authored, never engine- or llm-authored
}
```

- **The engine names the choices and persists the answer; only an authenticated human authors the answer.** The engine names the choices because only it knows which are legal; it never authors or selects one, and there is no default. An authenticated human authors one legal `choice` through the existing stored-object `diff` carrier at `../harness/INTERFACES.md §1.2`; the engine validates its membership in `choices` and persists the human-authored `chosen { choice, by, at }` with the human attribution and answer time. `choices` is a **closed, engine-named set** — a stored choice outside it is refused at the boundary under §2's unknown-member rule, never coerced to the nearest member. `chosen.by` has no `engine` or `llm` member — the same shape that makes a park unclearable by the loop (§1.3). The storage-step wording in `BUILD.md` Step 1 is governed by this authorship distinction: no engine-originated selection, while persisting a valid human-authored answer is required.
- **It co-occurs with the `needs_human` park** (§1.3) and is **cleared only by a stored human choice**. The stored choice is what clears the park; no retry, timeout, or later firing absorbs it.
- **Choice identity is part of the stored record, not inferred.** Two legal answers with the same human and instant remain distinguishable by `choice` after a reader restart. No migration may guess a choice from `by` or `at`; if a legacy row lacks `choice`, it receives an explicit unknown/repair treatment rather than an invented answer.
- **The harness routes it; the engine does not surface it.** Reading it is an ordinary `calculate` read — **no new seam verb** (§0 sole-client, `../harness/INTERFACES.md §1`). Whether acting on the chosen option crosses the customer-facing line is the harness floor's question, never the engine's (`../harness/SPEC.md §7`).
- **`Proposal.freed[].decision` (§1.11) is the pre-existing instance of this shape and stays where it is.** It is a `pending | keep-blocked | reopen` field inside a stored Proposal, gated by a passing pinned scenario (X5), and this section does **not** refactor it. Two instances of one shape is a known, deliberate near-duplicate; unifying them would be a separate scoped change, not a side effect of giving the primitive a name.

### §1.15 The trigger registry — the handle behind every scheduled firing

*Two laws already require **cancelling** a scheduled firing — `min-occupancy` "re-registers when `min` or `decide_by` is edited" (§3), and a conflicted bare draft registers a clock trigger at the affected instant (§9) — and cancelling requires holding the scheduled function's id, which no §1 object carried (2026-08-22, the build-contact review's M2: at Situation-C scale this is hundreds of live scheduled firings per tenant, discovered at Step 6/7 against a schema frozen at Step 1).*

```
TriggerRegistration {
  id, owner_org,
  subject_ref,          // the commitment, rule, or draft the firing is about
  fire_at,              // instant
  kind?: reminder | offer-hold | ask-age-out | escalation-deadline,
  reminder?: { booking_revision, policy_ref, policy_revision, grant_ref }, // required exactly for reminder
  escalation_deadline?: { escalation_ref, deadline_role, rung? }, // exact role-dependent shape: harness §3.13
  registered_by,        // the law that registered it (min-occupancy, conflicted-draft, reminder, …)
  scheduler_handle      // the substrate's cancellation token — opaque here, never interpreted
}
```

- Preparation-created registrations share their occurrence/history chunk transaction under §5 and survive its completed write on stop/crash; neither a request cancellation nor an abandoned draft silently withdraws them. Their eventual firing still follows the ordinary current-state and orphan checks below.
- **Engine-owned bookkeeping, not vocabulary.** Nothing evaluates against a registration; no rule may reference one; it exists so re-registration is *cancel-then-create* instead of a leak. Written and superseded through ordinary attributed writes.
- **The orphan rule:** a firing whose `subject_ref` no longer exists in an active state, or whose registration has been superseded, is a **no-op — not an error**: it records that it fired against nothing and stops. Late cancellation is therefore always safe, and a withdrawn draft's stray firing makes **no business-state write, park, or ask; it appends only its own attributed no-op record**, identified by the firing registration and §1.10's history.
- **The optional `kind` member** *(2026-08-31)*: a harness-written registration MUST carry a member of the closed union; an engine-internal registration omits it — the closed vocabulary is homed at `../harness/SPEC.md §3.13` (the registration-kind law; FR13 — the engine stores and returns the member, the harness owns the meaning), and the surfacing mapping is §2.2's (`INTERFACES.md`).
- **Reminder registrations follow the committed booking and policy revision.** For an eligible policy (`../harness/SPEC.md §3.3`), the existing registry has at most one current registration per `(owner_org, subject_ref, policy_ref)`; `subject_ref` is the booking, not a participant or request draft. The engine owns neither the notification nor the loop: the **harness's trigger loop** reads the park and routes the owner's decision (`../harness/SPEC.md §7`), because cancelling real people's bookings is an across-the-line act. The engine never auto-cancels a run, and never blocks a booking for being early |

**Complete rule-operand encoding.** `Rule.operand` means the existing rule’s entire operand, selected by `Rule.type` from this section’s menu, not a new core type. A single-value operand is a §2 `TypedValue`; a composite operand retains the menu’s named fields/tuple/list structure with §2 typed value leaves (and the declared references/predicates where specified). A composite operand is not itself a `TypedValue`: do not add a `type_tag`, hide it in `value`, discard components or flatten its leaves into separate source operands. The stored Rule (§1.5), its ordinary diff/readback and each corresponding Conflict/Latent operand (§8) carry the same complete shape, with the source rule’s provenance retained. Unknown structural members and wrong leaf types follow §2’s boundary refusal. For `duration`, the complete encoding is exactly the legacy typed time-range leaf **or** the aggregate below, discriminated by `type_tag` versus `range`; mixed shapes are invalid. For a plain `location-window` with no attribute, the complete operand is its typed recurring-window set; the attributed form retains the menu’s attribute/window pair. This does not change any other menu operand or the `typed_value(raw, type_spec) → TypedValue` signature: type each value leaf through that boundary, then validate the complete rule shape at the rule boundary.

**Duration operand.** The existing range operand remains valid without an increment. Its extended form is `{range: TypedValue, step?: TypedValue, anchor?: TypedValue}`: `range` is §2’s range pair with time units; `step` and `anchor` are §2 unit-bearing durations, present together or both absent. This adds no rule type or value type. `typed_value` resolves supplied units before evaluation; unit-incompatible operands (for example seats for a duration step) refuse. Bounds retain their inclusive meaning: `min ≤ max`; when stepped, `step > 0` and **both endpoints lie on the anchor’s lattice**. A malformed or incompatible definition cannot store; the harness elicits the intended endpoints/step/anchor rather than silently rounding or discarding one. An absent step means the existing range-only rule, never an inferred universal increment. The authoring readback names the anchor (for example “from the 30-minute minimum”); ambiguous anchoring requires clarification. This is a **length** restriction: it adds no start-time rounding or granularity; §7’s start-candidate lattice is unchanged. The whole duration operand binds at booking under §1.5.

**Restriction enforcement.** Every supported scheduling restriction must agree across its stored typed rule, availability projection, `calculate`/`resolve` and final `commit` admission, using the same operands and §1.5 binding times. A rule’s presence in a field or a successful `check_coverage` is not enforcement evidence. The harness owns natural-language clarification and stored readback (`../harness/SPEC.md §6`); the engine owns this evaluation agreement and refuses missing required or unrepresentable operands under §§2–3, never claiming the unsupported restriction is active. A kind-scoped overnight exclusion uses `location-window`’s ordinary recurring-window operand, not a global personal-time ban. For Matt’s English template the excluded 23:00–09:00 interval crosses midnight and the allowed lesson window is daily 09:00–23:00 in the board’s zone; it intersects actual commitments and the applicable **before and after** buffer requirements. A five-minute requirement applies on each side, not as five minutes divided between them. The same constant `buffer` rule covers both gaps; zero buffers on a neighbouring Personal event do not erase English’s requirement. Unrelated personal routines retain their own rules. This is configuration of the existing menu, derived from `../user-stories/Situations/Situation-A/story-matt.md` S07/S08a/S11, not an English-specific type.

**Run-scoped minimum occupancy.** A `min-occupancy` registration’s `subject_ref` (§1.15) identifies the run whose first booking armed it. At `start − decide_by`, read that run’s confirmed, still-occupying bookings and their recorded draw in the applicable board’s units as of that instant. Evaluate concurrent draw **over the run’s own interval**, not over the board at the earlier decision instant: at every point in that interval the run’s confirmed draw must meet `min`. Only bookings attributable to this same run contribute; other runs on the same board, including overlapping ones, contribute nothing. Resolve attribution from stored subject/booking references, never from coincident times, a common kind or a display title. Unconverted holds are not confirmed seats. This is a recorded admission fact, never proof of attendance. Re-registration, orphan handling and the current operand follow §1.5/§1.15 unchanged; shortfall still only records the park and human choices in the menu row.

**Precedence:** `governing > org > individual`; among same-level rules on one placement, **most restrictive wins**. Governing rules are non-overridable (recorded exception is the only forward path); own-rule conflicts take the override-with-reason path (§8) — **except unsatisfiable combinations under this section’s clash tests, which no authority overrides** (§8 item 4).

**Clash tests (what `check_consistency` §8 evaluates).** Rules clash only where their math meets — overlapping targets and a shared evaluated quantity. Same-level “most restrictive” means the **effective intersection of jointly applicable constraints**, not selecting an arbitrary source rule; compatible scalar bounds still reduce to their tightest bound. For the table’s attribute-predicate row, compose the co-applicable `admission`/`qualification` and `attribute-domain` predicates over the **same typed attribute and subject domain**, retaining every contributor. Test that effective intersection even when three or more predicates each overlap pairwise: pairwise overlap is not a nonempty joint domain. This is the **only higher-order composition exception**; ordinary cross-type clashes remain the table’s two-rule tests. The closed table is exhaustive only with that scoped normalization, not a claim that same-type predicates cannot clash. Rules on different attributes or disjoint applicability branches are not intersected as one domain. In particular, “Eastside only Mon/Wed” and “Riverside only Tue/Fri” are conditional site windows: each restricts its own site, not a demand that one booking use both sites. They store and project per P3.

The previously rejected `duration + buffer × location-window` joint row stays rejected: one placement alone consumes zero inter-placement buffers, so its smallest failing input reduces to `duration × location-window`; do not restore the over-broad row. A cross-type combination outside the table and the stated attribute composition shares no evaluated quantity and composes. §8 routes each hit: ***unsatisfiable*** is refused at write whatever the authority; *latent* alerts never block; other conflicts route by authority.

**Read each row's test, not its label.** Of the six unsatisfiable rows, **five are unconditional** and **one fires only under a stated condition** — `capacity × dependency` (*only at the minimum*). Treating a conditional row as unconditional is not a small error: it refuses rulesets that work, which is the failure this class was never meant to cause. Two rows have died of exactly that (`dependency × pin` fell with the pin disambiguation; the three-rule row fell to the minimum-case test). The label names the *class*; the sentence after the colon is the *test*, and the test governs.

## §4. Handles

- A `Handle` = an engine-minted opaque reference `{ id, query, computed_at: store_version, expires }` to a value the engine computed and validated. Handles are random, resolvable only inside the engine: the model cannot construct one that resolves, and **a literal appearing in a handle-typed position is a type error** — the structural half of thin-agent.
- **Display facet:** a handle carries an engine-rendered, display-only projection (e.g. `"Tom 2:30 → 10:30"`) so the harness can narrate it. Re-entering displayed values as write literals is rejected by the same type rule.
- **The handle-typed positions are an enumeration of record, and the guarantee is exactly as wide as the list** *(2026-08-22 — "read-back can never become authoring" was a bigger claim than the mechanism supports, and its true strength is this list's completeness)*. **The rule:** every parameter on every seam verb that *references an existing stored object* — a commitment, proposal, offer, order, board, rule, grant, pattern, template, party, or pending-decision/ask — is **handle-typed**; the type error covers all of them. **The free positions, enumerated because they are the residue:** the owner's utterance; a `text` node's authored copy and per-language `label` maps; a decline/ask's free note; raw operand strings entering `typed_value` (which validates them); and form-field values a booking legitimately authors. **The residue, stated in the FD-29 style:** a model that has read a facet can paraphrase its value into one of those free positions — nothing structural catches `"15:00"` typed into a note after reading `"Tom 2:30 → 10:30"`. What bounds that residue is not this section: a free position is never an *authorizing* position (the floor, `../harness/SPEC.md §7`), and attended read-back is the check on meaning. The set-equality gate over this enumeration against the built tool contract lands at harness Step 2 (`../deployment/SPEC.md §7a`).
- **The facet's selectable set is fenced by construction** *(2026-08-21 — §0 enumerates the display projections and fences each by what it is able to select; this one had no fence)*: a facet renders **only from the handle's own computed result fields**, never from the store at large. For a handle whose query crossed the tenant line (a §7.1 offer), the facet can therefore name only what the `availability` grant already exposed — the printed example names a person because that handle belongs to the owner whose own board computed it; a counterparty-side facet has no name in its selectable set to render. I3's walk covers it.
- **Staleness:** a handle is bound to the store version it was computed against. `commit` re-validates on redemption (§6.4): it either revalidates cleanly or returns `{conflict}` — stale math never applies silently.

**The travel seam.** `travel(request) → estimate | unavailable` is answered by an external source behind the engine’s own interface (`INTERFACES.md §2.1`). The request names ordered typed `origin` and `destination`, explicitly selected `mode`, `timing {kind: arrive-by | depart-at, instant, zone}`, and supplied `reference_instant`. `instant` is an absolute dated instant verified under §2 with its declared zone; a bare 06:00 or an unresolved place cannot route. Mode is an explicit provider-adapter input, never inferred or silently substituted. Unsupported mode, dated timing or arrival/departure semantics returns unavailable; a departure-only answer cannot masquerade as arrive-by support. No extra user location is inferred.

An estimate binds the entire canonical request and carries engine-typed duration, departure and arrival instants, source/provider and adapter revision, obtained-at reference instant and valid-until/freshness metadata. Its arrival/departure fields must satisfy the requested semantics; the engine validates/derives these typed values, including subtraction from an arrive-by instant, never the app or model. The cached source fact has `author: engine`; a declared override retains its rule provenance and is distinguished from a provider estimate. Neither is factual proof that anyone travelled or arrived. A provider’s declared support or scripted fixture is not measured provider capability; the serving-provider proof stays at BUILD Step 4.

**Override precedence, exact:** an enabled `travel-override` rule (§3's row is the type's home — *status: drafted at its home §3*; scoped to a route / time-of-day, retaining §3’s existing applicability rather than inventing a mode-specific override) **>** eligible cached computed fact **>** fresh external fetch **>** **fail-closed**. If no value is obtainable, the gap is *unknown* — and **unknown ≠ feasible**: the slot is not offered, the move is not proposed. (Same posture as `empty_means: unknown`, §1.2.)

**The travel envelope (bounded, so the seam cannot become the product's latency).** An external call sits inside the hot path of every placement, so its cost is capped by law, not by hope:

- **Ceiling per `resolve`:** a placement call resolves **at most 8 fresh (uncached) request identities under the cache policy below** against the provider. Beyond that the remaining unknown legs are *unknown*, and unknown fails closed (above) — the search declines rather than stalls. **The unit is the deduplicated request identity (historically called a triple), stated because both other readings break the bound** *(2026-08-21)*: it is not raw legs before dedup (V2's batching collapses those) and not provider requests (a day's triples travel in one batched request, so a request ceiling would bound nothing) — V1 counts triples, V2 counts requests, and they measure different things on purpose. 8 is the starting value of a named constant, tuned at BUILD against a real Situation-D candidate set (Steps 4/7 owe the printed tuning row); a cold cache on a dense day **will** hit it, and hitting it degrades honestly to `travel-unknown` legs — the ceiling is a latency bound, never a coverage promise. **And hitting the ceiling starts the warm-up** *(2026-08-22 — every board's first location-aware placement runs cold by definition, so ceiling-and-stop made Situation D's flagship decline on day one)*: the triples the ceiling cut are resolved by an ordinary background job whose completion is a clock-source firing, and the harness **re-offers** when the legs are known. Fail-closed is untouched — nothing was offered on an unknown gap; what changed is that the unknown is being *worked*, and the decline says so.
- **Batching.** Legs are resolved **per candidate day, in one batched request**, not per candidate instant — the §7 lattice generates many candidates over the same small set of place-pairs. Only identical cache request identities below are deduplicated before any fetch; mode or timing differences remain separate even within one batch.
- **Cache policy.** The identity is `(owner_org, origin value/revision, destination value/revision, mode, timing.kind, timing.instant, timing.zone, provider/adapter revision)`. Values include the declared address and any resolved geo-reference; nickname alone is never a route key. The ordered pair cannot be reversed, a different date/time or mode cannot reuse a wrong answer, and a retired/edited place cannot silently resolve through an old nickname. The existing **30-minute buckets** remain batching/index partitions, not permission to reuse across different meaningful inputs: exact dated instant and timing kind stay in identity within a bucket. Historical “triple” terminology in the envelope means this full identity, including for ceiling counts and warm-up. Cache facts remain tenant-scoped (§1.1), so neither cross-tenant results nor hit/miss observations leak another owner’s route. Eligible facts match that identity and are fresh at the supplied reference instant: before both obtained-at + **30-day TTL** and any earlier source valid-until. At either expiry boundary or before obtained-at the fact is ineligible. The 30-day TTL is an upper bound, never a claim that a provider’s estimate is fresh that long. A changed source/provider revision or expired freshness forces a new eligible fact through calculate/resolve or explicit unavailable; commit only refuses. Bucket/TTL/freshness evaluation uses the supplied `reference_instant`, never wall time; unchanged stored inputs at the same reference instant replay identically. Source facts retain their provenance/history; refreshing cannot silently renew a user’s acceptance of old inputs.
- **Timeout:** **2 seconds** per batched request, one retry, then the legs are unknown. No placement waits on a slow provider.
- **"No times" and "can't compute" are different answers, and the decline says which — and "can't compute" says why** *(the second discriminator added 2026-08-22, by V4's own reasoning applied one level down)*. A structured decline carries either `no-feasible-placement` (the math ran; nothing fits — the honest "your day is full") or `travel-unknown` (the math could not run), and a `travel-unknown`'s `detail` carries its cause, `ceiling | provider-failed` (unsupported provider inputs and unusable/stale source facts with no eligible replacement are `provider-failed`, with an authorized explanation of the limitation, never a false outage assertion; the seam-envelope reason stays `travel-unknown` — the cause rides `detail` exactly as `no-feasible-placement`'s constraint class does, so the closed reason set does not widen, `../harness/INTERFACES.md §7.1`) — because the user's next moves differ again: *ceiling* means "still working it out, the warm-up is running, expect a re-offer" and *provider-failed* describes the actual unavailable estimate/source limitation, such as an outage, unsupported arrive-by mode or stale fact without replacement. The harness narrates all three differently. Collapsing any two into one message is a defect (§9).

**Request preparation versus projection computation.** Servicing a beyond-horizon `calculate` availability request or board subscription may first invoke §1.4's existing horizon job for the requested authorized tenant/window. That preparation performs real, engine-attributed domain writes: missing Commitment rows, their ordinary history and required trigger registrations. It is not cache-only merely because a read caused it. Authorization precedes preparation; the request neither materializes another tenant nor fills the intervening unrequested span. The job uses deterministic, idempotent chunks and the supplied reference instant, preserves occurrence identity and prior cancellations/exceptions (§1.4), and follows §9's conflicted-draft, quota and capacity rules. Partial or unavailable preparation is unknown, never verified empty/free. With unchanged inputs, replay at the same reference instant writes nothing further, including no duplicate instances, history, registrations or cache facts.

**Preparation durability and attribution.** Preparation derives only from already committed, authorized Patterns and their stored generations, accepted exceptions and terminal history; a proposed new Routine, unsaved edit, skip or stop cannot supply preparation inputs. Each chunk atomically writes its missing occurrences (including §9's conflicted bare drafts), their attributed history and required §1.15 registrations, or none of them. History under §1.10 records the engine author, originating clock/request, authorized tenant/window and reference instant, generating Pattern/version and affected occurrence identities; a harness-originated request also correlates to its existing firing/claim. No occurrence or registration may survive without that chunk's history. Recheck authorization, stored generation and cancellation/exception guards in each applying transaction.

A harness-originated request follows `../harness/SPEC.md §3.14(b)`'s durable birth-before-preparation ordering through the existing `commit` and claim context. Each applying chunk checks that birth and current ownership (§1.17); a stopped or retired worker cannot continue preparation. Stopping/crashing after a committed chunk preserves its rows, conflict records, history and registrations; an in-flight chunk either commits whole before the stop fence or makes no writes. No rollback or automatic completion of the remaining request is implied; an incomplete window remains unknown. Rolling-clock and direct board-subscription jobs have no harness firing to birth: they retain the same atomic engine history and registry guarantees, and subscription cancellation cannot undo completed chunks. These are real domain effects of maintaining committed Patterns, never application of the abandoned user's draft, a basis spend or permission to dispatch.

Projection computation runs over the prepared stored state and writes nothing except the compute cache. `calculate` never produces an outward effect; preparation only records the ordinary triggers/questions, never asks, sends, chooses or confirms for a user. No new seam verb follows. The separate commit path remains §6.1: commit never fetches or writes a cache fact, and a failed commit has only §6.6's apply-record exception. `SCENARIOS.md` M4/M7/P10 and V6 keep these boundaries distinct.

The existing §5 item 7 relevant-slice read also carries the selected owner-private group/request records declared in `../harness/INTERFACES.md §1.1`, under `../harness/SPEC.md §3.6`. Resolve stable entry/party refs and current revisions with authorized dependency facts, never a label join or new projection. Foreign/nonexistent selections return the same no-match; a guest token cannot invoke the owner slice.

## §6. `commit` — write semantics

**Shared request transaction (`../harness/SPEC.md §3.6`).** Confirmation redeems one accepted request revision under its existing Commitment id and writes that booking, its Order customer set, booked participants, all applicable participant quota/balance effects, the single teacher placement and its ordinary history/registrations atomically. A second recipient link or a fresh write id cannot confirm the same request again: the already-confirmed identity is read back within that credential's scope, or a changed payload takes ordinary stale/conflict refusal. Replay of the original write id returns its original result under item 6, including an unknown acknowledgement. No participant list position, name or link is a new customer identity. Compute each actual board draw once: teacher capacity and placement travel/buffers once; seat quantities and per-customer rules over the distinct selected parties. Re-evaluate every participant's applicable admission against authoritative current state. Any participant refusal, competing teacher booking, stale proposal, arrangement revocation, expired/disputed report or injected member failure writes no candidate booking, quota, placement, reminder or domain history; the existing apply-result record exception remains item 6.

The same transaction rules apply to an accepted participant edit, rejoin, move or whole-booking cancellation: retain one booking identity and original history, apply the entire resulting participant set and required rule-derived effects, never release then reacquire as separate writes. Bound pricing/duration and participant quota-policy contracts remain §§1.5/3; this carrier does not itself define replacement quote or cancellation-release policy. If a requested result lacks a supported validated term/policy, refuse that candidate with an explained dependency, not a guessed outcome. Group/member retirement serializes its complete live-reference and expected-revision checks with its principal-attribute diff under the §3.6 home; concurrent request/link creation either sees retirement or becomes a dependency that retirement must resolve. Revocation/suspension and applying a pending coordinator act serialize on current authorization/report revisions. A completed write remains history; stale work cannot apply later. Roster changes never cascade to contacts or booked participants, and actual attendance never substitutes for a participant transaction.

For field/rule lifecycle diffs, apply `../harness/SPEC.md §3.3` inside the same transaction as the ordinary checks below: compare the expected target/field revisions, re-read the full current engine reference set, check every dependent member’s authority and validate the resulting schema, defaults and complete typed rules. Independent optional retirement succeeds; a required/unresolved reference or stale candidate refuses without partial schema/value/rule/history mutation. A concurrent new reference and retirement cannot both succeed leaving a dangling binding. Schema/type failures and stale Handles use the existing `invalid` envelope reasons (`../harness/INTERFACES.md` §§1.2/7.1); governing protection remains item 3. Reference history bound to prior revisions remains readable, subject to §1.10 erasure. App document reference serialization stays with the app adapter under `../marketplace/SPEC.md §3`, not an engine transaction across stores.

`commit(commitment | diff | proposal_ref, write_id) → {ok, applied_ref, committed_revision} | Envelope` *(the seam home's printed shape, `../harness/INTERFACES.md §1.2` — the neutral applied-result on success; failures are the closed envelope, a `conflict`'s `alternatives` riding as `next`)*

*Every refusal and decline in this section crosses the seam inside the closed failure envelope — `{kind, reason, detail?, next?}`, six kinds, reasons from `../harness/INTERFACES.md §7.1`'s table (`../harness/INTERFACES.md §1` is the envelope's home; pointer added 2026-08-22).*

1. **Atomicity & the race.** Every commit-time check — capacity, balance, quota, buffer gaps (constant or travel-computed) against both neighbors, latches, preconditions, governing rules — evaluates inside **one atomic transaction**. Two commits racing for the same capacity: at most one succeeds; the loser gets the envelope's `conflict` with `alternatives` as `next` (the honest "that time was just taken," with what's still open). **Commit never fetches** *(2026-08-21 — §5's precedence allowed a fresh fetch that no transaction can perform)*: the travel-computed form reads **cached facts only** inside the transaction; a cache miss at commit is *unknown*, and unknown fails closed — the fresh-fetch rung of §5's precedence belongs to `calculate`/`resolve`, never to a write path. A commit therefore writes no cache facts either, which is what keeps a failed commit side-effect-free (the write-id record of item 6 excepted). **A rule diff runs the consistency check inside the same transaction** *(2026-08-29 — the execution site §8 item 4's class previously lacked: three scenarios depended on "refused at write" and no file said which write path runs the check)*: a commit carrying a rule diff — a create, an enable, or an operand edit on a §1.5 Rule — runs `check_consistency` (§8) over the **resulting** ruleset, the stored set with the diff applied, inside the same atomic transaction as every other item-1 check. An `unsatisfiable` verdict **refuses the write at any authority**, and the refusal carries §8's naming message (*"The refusal names the fix"*); a `governing conflict` verdict refuses; an `own-rule conflict` verdict returns for the harness's override-with-reason path; `latent` does not block — that routing is §8's, restated here and never redefined. The check is a **read inside the write transaction** — consistency classification reads stored rules only — so it obeys this item's *commit never fetches* law by construction. Its refusal's reason member is **the table's own row** (`../harness/INTERFACES.md §7.1` — `unsatisfiable` for the unsatisfiable verdict, `rule-violation` for the refusing conflict verdicts, the rule named in `detail`); the engine invents no reason string outside the table (`INTERFACES.md §1`), and the literal lives at the table, never here.
   **Coordinated rule remedies** use this same diff transaction (§8, “Remedy choice and application”): all member edits are assembled before checking the resulting ruleset, authorized per member and committed together or refused whole. The ordinary attach-point diff carries the set under one write id; neither a member’s invalid intermediate ruleset nor a partial write is exposed.
   **Reminder activation and dependencies are part of that same atomicity:** a live-confirmed Grant mint plus its kind-policy activation (`../harness/SPEC.md §3.3`) applies together or neither applies. Booking/policy/grant changes and the registry replacements or withdrawals they require (§1.15) commit together, as dependent contents of the existing commitment/stored-object diff carrier, not a fourth apply-proposal form. Replays return the original result under item 6 without a second activation or arming.
2. **The latch invariant.** A write that would clear a set latch is rejected. Rewriting its author or timestamp is likewise rejected (§1.3). Apply `../harness/SPEC.md §3.4`'s transition table inside item 1's transaction: an earlier confirmation does not refuse an authorized cancel or valid recorded completion, and survives both unchanged. Only competing terminal outcomes refuse a later conflicting transition; a backdated input cannot replace the first effective terminal outcome or supply retroactive authority. Late precondition evidence against `expired_at` is **recorded** (evidence is never thrown away) but the status never flips — no un-expire, ever. Admissible facts after cancellation likewise remain history without revival. Completion follows the kind declaration at the same §3.4 home, with `actual_end` corrections still subject to item 7; storing a return is no physical verification.
3. **Diff-only application.** Board and rule writes arrive as diffs and apply non-destructively. A diff whose effect would remove or disable a governing-authority rule from a lower authority **does not apply** — the wipe is unconstructable, not audited-after.
4. **Handle redemption.** Correctness-critical fields must arrive as handles or typed values; the engine resolves and **re-validates them at commit time** against the current store version.
5. **Apply-proposal** is a commit variant with three input forms, each named *(the latter two stated 2026-08-21 — both existed as prose elsewhere with no input shape here, which is where a builder looks)*: **(a) a Proposal handle** — requires every move's stored `confirmed`, re-runs every check as of *now* (the board may have moved since §7 computed it), applies all moves in one transaction — or returns `{conflict}` and the proposal goes stale; freed-window decisions store the same way. **(b) an offer ref (§7.1)** — an offer has no `moves[]`, so the confirmed-moves check is vacuous and **the gate is the counterparty's stored accept**: the accept-mode Grant's yes or the live confirmation (§7.1 item 2) must exist as a stored, attributed fact, or the commit refuses — a live unanswered offer cannot be committed by its own initiator (I8). **(c) an Order ref carrying its member day handles** — the all-or-nothing form of §7's decomposition: all member placements apply in one transaction or none do. No fourth input form exists — one arriving is refused at the boundary under §2's unknown-member rule. **A bundled `FiringEvent` part is admitted, and it is not a fourth form** *(2026-08-31 — the one-sentence admission §1.16's co-write depended on)*: a birth or seal riding a host commit's payload is content **inside** whichever form the host write takes — the 5(c) one-id-covers-the-bundle shape, checked against its (firing id, part) identity in the same transaction — and a standalone part is an ordinary bookkeeping write under its derived id, no new form either way.
6. **Idempotency per write id** *(2026-08-21)*. Every commit carries a caller-supplied write id (`../harness/INTERFACES.md §1.2`); re-committing an id already applied returns the **original result**, never a second application — the guest seam's hold-idempotency (`../security/SPEC.md §3`), generalized to every write. It is what makes the harness's one bounded retry safe against a lost response (harness K4), and it doubles as the external surface's continuation handle. **The id's law, printed** *(2026-08-21 — it was the one lookup key in the design with no minting law, and it is also a read key)*: the two lookups the id serves are **two different jobs and carry two different keys** *(2026-08-22 — one key served both, and it contradicted X4's cross-client resume: the record that held an interrupted turn was keyed to the credential that started it, so a second client of the same principal read nothing)*: the **apply record** (what makes a re-commit return the original result) is indexed per `(owner_org, credential)` — replay safety; a replayed foreign id matches nothing and returns nothing, so the "original result" can never be another tenant's or another caller's. The **turn-state read** (`../harness/SCENARIOS.md` X4) is indexed per `(owner_org, issuing principal)`, with the originating credential recorded as an **attribute, not part of the key** — any credential of the same principal resumes or discards the turn (RQ-6's law), and a read of another *principal's* id returns nothing exactly as before. A **known id arriving with a different payload is refused** — the envelope's `conflict`, reason `write-id-reuse` — never silently honored under either payload. A **failed** commit records under its id too — the replay of a failure returns the same failure, deterministically. Minting guidance binds the caller the way §3's token law binds annnä: ≥128 bits of randomness, URL-safe, never derived from guessable content — **the harness mints per write** *(re-cut 2026-08-31: the home's law is "every write carries a caller-supplied write id", and this sentence's per-turn phrasing contradicted it — a turn makes up to two work commits plus bookkeeping writes, each under its own id)*; an external client mints its own and owns the collision risk inside its own scope only. **The firing id's own minting law** *(2026-08-31 — the one id the derived part ids are pure functions of, previously with no minting fact)*: the **harness mints the firing id at claim time**, under this same guidance — ≥128 bits, never derived from guessable content — so `birth_write_id`/`seal_write_id` (§1.16) inherit their unguessability from it, and a standalone derived part id is decidable against this item exactly as any caller-minted id is.
7. **Interval-widening edits re-run every placement check** *(2026-08-21 — `actual_end` is deliberately not a latch, and before this item nothing said a widening diff re-ran §6.1, which made "correct a mistyped return time" a legal path to a double-book once the freed slot was re-let)*. A diff that widens a commitment's occupying interval — an **already-recorded** `actual_end` corrected later, `end` moved later, `start` moved earlier — re-runs the full item-1 check set over the widened span, in the same transaction as the write, within §1.2's recorded admission/expansion scope. If the freed time was re-let, the write returns the envelope's `conflict` (`alternatives` as `next`) and the correction **parks for a human** — the mistyped time is fixed by resolving the conflict, never by silent double-occupancy. Narrowing edits stay ordinary diffs.
   **First reported end of an overdue completable:** a first `actual_end` at or before the supplied reference instant closes its unresolved draw under `../harness/SPEC.md §3.4` (FD-98). Classify that closure as non-expanding: existing historical overlap with a preaccepted successor alone cannot block this record or require a park. This is the approved repair plan's **smallest recommended classification** of an implicit contract, not a new founder ratification or a reproduced implementation refusal. Preserve both original schedules, the successor's reservation, confirmation/latch facts and attributed history; the bound buffer runs from the reported actual end (§3), and the successor's independent reservation still constrains availability. This classification applies only to closing the unresolved draw; any other expansion in the same diff still runs the placement checks above. Ordinary authorization (`../harness/SPEC.md §7`), per-field attribution (§1.3), schema/precondition validation (item 1), terminal/latch checks (item 2) and once-per-write-ID application (item 6) still apply. It neither supplies physical evidence nor mandates an overrun workflow (§1.2).

## §7. `resolve` — placement

`resolve(goal, boards, rules) → Handle` *(a placement, a proposal, or a structured decline — same seam signature as `../harness/INTERFACES.md §1.5`).*

**Place-only core (fully designed):** find a placement of `goal` into genuinely free, rule-satisfying space across every board it touches. Output: a placement handle, or a **structured decline** — "nothing fits" is a first-class answer carrying which constraint class refused (never a silent failure, never a forced fit).

**Reshuffle-as-proposal** (the Situation-D revisit, in scope now): when the goal is *compaction* — a trigger opened a window and the owner wants the day pulled tighter — the **`goal` additionally carries the trigger and a `direction` (toward-start | toward-end), supplied per event by the harness inside `goal`** — no new parameter; the signature is unchanged (`INTERFACES.md §1`, which this sentence previously contradicted). It is never a stored default: the owner's pick governs that event only. Output: a **Proposal object** (§1.11), never an applied change.

**Constraint classes the search honors, in order:**
- (a) **governing rules** — hard bounds on the space;
- (b) **party-owned absolutes** — **filtered before candidate generation**: a `pin`ned party's existing placements are not movable (never in `moves[]` — the never-move reading, §3), and candidates outside a party-targeted `location-window` never generate; an excluded move never exists to be declined;
- (c) **buffer gaps (including computed travel)** across every adjacent pair in the candidate day, including the first leg from the owner's start place;
- (d) **latches and preconditions** of any instance touched;
- (e) **bound terms** — a move never re-prices or re-shapes what was booked (§1.5 binding).

**Candidate generation, printed (what makes the search deterministic and finite):** candidates are start instants on a **5-minute lattice** (a named constant, one place) inside the goal's rule-allowed windows — classes (a)/(b) shape the windows *before* generation, so an excluded candidate never exists to be tested. Candidates are tried **earliest-first** in the goal's zone (compaction goals: in the supplied `direction` instead); the first satisfying (c)–(e) wins — feasibility, not optimality, per the printed bound below. Termination is structural: finite windows × finite lattice, each candidate tested once; exhaustion **is** the structured decline, carrying the tightest refusing constraint class.

**The compaction enumeration, printed — the same treatment the place-only lattice gets** *(2026-08-29 — the section printed compaction's direction and its ≤3-move bound and left the enumeration order over *move sets* unprinted, while X1's "two different proposals, each improving in its stated direction only", X7 and §1.11's re-checkability all depend on that order being fixed)*: move sets are enumerated **smallest-first** — one move, then two, then three — and within a size, in the goal's `direction`; each move's candidate instants sit on the same **5-minute lattice** as the place-only search. The **first feasible set wins**; ties break on the **stable id order** this section already names for contention. Termination is structural for the same reason the place-only search's is: finite move sets × finite lattice, each tested once.

**The v1 bound, printed — it bounds the *reshuffle* search:** single board-owner, single day, **at most 3 moves per proposal**. *(It is a bound on rearranging existing commitments. It is not a claim that `resolve` only ever sees one owner's boards — a §7.1 share offer reads a second owner's **exposed availability** and moves nothing, so it is outside this bound by construction, not by exception.)* The promise is *feasibility and improvement in the chosen direction* — not optimality. (Week-level batching and multi-owner reshuffles are §11 non-goals; this bound is what keeps `resolve` a checker, not a routing optimizer.)

**Multi-day placement — decomposition, printed.** The v1 bound is *per call*, not per booking. A multi-day goal (Situation C's four-day course, `../README.md`) is **N single-day `resolve` calls chained by the caller** — the harness — never one multi-day search. The engine exposes no multi-day solver and never will at v1; the bound above is what keeps `resolve` a checker.

- **Composition root.** The days are held together by an **Order** (§1.8) — the object that already exists for exactly this. `resolve` returns one handle per day; the Order carries the set.
- **Partial success is a real outcome, not a failure.** A decline on day N **does not invalidate days 1…N−1.** Their handles stay valid and independently committable. There is no rollback of earlier days, because nothing was written — placement handles are `calculate`-class reads until `commit`.
- **The proposal reports which days placed.** The caller receives, per day, either a placement handle or a structured decline carrying its refusing constraint class (§7 above). *"Three of four days fit; Thursday has no boat"* is a first-class answer the harness can narrate and elicit against — never a silent partial, never a forced fit on the fourth day.
- **All-or-nothing is the caller's choice, expressed as an Order commit.** If the owner wants the course booked only whole, the harness commits the day set as one Order in **one transaction** (§6.1) — all days or none — through `commit`'s Order-ref input form (§6.5 c; the input shape existed in prose here and nowhere on the seam until 2026-08-21). If the owner will take a partial course, the days commit individually. The engine supplies both; it does not decide which the domain wants.
- **Where the kind-level shape lives:** how a saved course is *stored* — "the saved course fills the whole assembly at once" — is **§1.12** (`KindTemplate`, F7's resolution). This section defines how days are **placed and committed**; §1.12 defines how the template is **stored and expanded**. Two questions, two homes, deliberately.

**Contention:** simultaneous triggers are arbitrated in deterministic order (arrival, then stable id tiebreak); **arrival is recorded on the goal at the instant it enters arbitration, read from the supplied reference instant** *(2026-08-29 — the same clock §2's `type_spec` carries; unstated, "arrival" silently meant wall-clock receipt order)*, and the **recorded value** — never receipt order — is what orders contention, which is what makes P8's *"the identical order on replay"* reproducible rather than incidental; re-solve rounds are bounded — **the bound is a named constant whose starting value is 3, tuned at BUILD against a real Situation-D candidate set** *(2026-08-29 — this was the one bound in the section that was not a printed number, so a builder picked it; 3 mirrors the section's other printed small bound, at most one re-solve round per candidate move-set size. Termination needs only that the bound be finite and printed: the Step-7 measurement, not this starting value, is the binding act — the 8-triple ceiling's precedent exactly, and `BUILD.md` Step 7 owes the tuning row)*; a goal that survives no round parks as a structured decline for the harness loop. No interleaving may violate §10.

### §7.1 The share seam — one commitment across owners

*The mechanism cited by `../harness/SPEC.md §2`, `../harness/SCENARIOS.md` I1, `../harness/BUILD.md` Step 5, and `../security/SPEC.md §9` (the sole class of legal crossing at v1). Defined here, once. The permission vocabulary below is also the normative home the grant system cites.*

> **Provenance: founder-ruled 2026-08-07** (wayfinder tickets #2 and #12; registry `../RULINGS.md` §2026-08-07). This section replaces the earlier drafted-not-ruled bind-handshake proposal, which specified paired commitments reconciled by an atomic pair-write. The founder redrew it: there are no pairs.

**What it is, in one line:** there is always exactly **one commitment** — whoever creates it owns it (ownership does not transfer), and it reaches other people's boards and eyes only through **Grants** the mechanics below mint. Not a message, not a request queue, not a mirrored record.

**The ShareGrant — the permission vocabulary (one home, cited everywhere).** It is a distinct shape from the §1.6 authorization `Grant` `{action_class, operation, scope, conditions, expiry, revocable}` — a `ShareGrant` is `{holder, rung, scope, edit_mode}` and governs *who may see or edit one shared commitment or board*, never *which action class the floor permits*. The two shared the bare name `Grant` and are separated here so a reader never conflates them.

- **Three rungs:** `availability` (see free times — the default exposure, and all a stranger ever sees) · `details` (see what was shared) · `edit`. There is no manage-sharing rung: ownership is non-transferable, so only the owner widens or revokes.
- **Scope:** a grant attaches to **one commitment or one board**, and is held by a **person** — or by a **link token**, which is the same grant held anonymously by its bearer (the §1.7 Shared projection *is* the `availability` grant rendered; a per-recipient token is its bearer form). Grants to groups are not in the menu.
- **Edit semantics:** an `edit` grant carries the owner's per-grant choice — apply directly or apply with the owner's approval — defaulted to direct. Either way the edit walks every §6.1 check; a grant widens who may *ask*, never what the rules *allow*.

**It is NOT the §11 reshuffle handshake.** This seam **places a new commitment into already-published free space**; it never moves, re-times, or re-shapes a commitment that already exists on either board. The banned thing at §11 is a *multi-owner reshuffle* — an optimizer that rearranges two people's existing days to make room. That stays banned, and the §7 v1 bound (single owner, single day, ≤3 moves) is unchanged: **reshuffle is single-owner; a share offer is two-owner; they are different operations.** A reader who conflates them re-derives a conflict that is not there.

**No new read power — the grant is the whole window.** The initiator never queries the counterparty's board. It sees exactly what the counterparty exposes: the `availability` grant (§1.7's Shared projection), with §1.7's leak guarantees intact. *Reachability is not readability.* Richer visibility between trusting parties is the counterparty granting a higher rung — the seam itself never reads more.

**The offer is the commitment itself, in a pre-accepted state.** There is no separate proposal object to reconcile with the commitment later — the one object carries its own lifecycle:

```
Commitment (share-relevant fields)
  owner            -> { tenant, board, principal }   // the creator; never transfers
  grants[]         -> ShareGrant { holder: person | token, rung, scope, edit_mode }   // engine-minted only
  status           : draft | offered | declined | expired | …
  hold             : duration                         // creator-set at share time: default 5 min, 0–24 h
  decline_reason                                      // structured; set only from `declined`
```

**The status vocabulary's home is `../harness/SPEC.md §3.4`**, not here — the four values above are the share-relevant subset of that block, and a value this seam needs but does not print is read from there. Two values this list previously carried have been removed rather than reconciled: `placed` (a committed, accepted offer is `confirmed` as a latch and `active` as a derived condition — nothing needed a third name for it) and `stale` (an offer that ends at a commit-time conflict is not a status; item 5 below records it as a terminal event, and `stale` is reserved throughout this corpus for **computed artifacts** — handles §4, proposals §6/§9 — never for a commitment).

The `grants[]` edge (each entry a `ShareGrant`) is the only **authorization** structure in the store permitted to name a second tenant, and **no caller can construct one** — the engine mints it through this seam or it does not exist. That keeps the `../security/SPEC.md §9` invariant literal ("unconstructable by a caller") while making the crossing possible. **The edge-count claims, restated precisely** *(2026-08-21 — three "sole/only" statements were falsifiable as printed)*: engine-minted **references** may also name a second tenant's principal as data — a Proposal's `moved_party` and its `confirmation`, an Escalation's `answered_by`, an Order's `customers[]`. None of them is an authorization: each grants no read and no write across the line, each is engine-authored (no caller constructs one), and every read of them walks the same fenced projections. "The sole two-tenant edge" here, in `../security/SPEC.md §9` and in its §13 ledger counts **authorization edges**; stored references are a different, larger, equally caller-unconstructable set.

**The seam, exactly — and it is not a new one.** The share adds **no verb** to the engine's surface. The harness contract pins five verbs (`../harness/INTERFACES.md §1`) and the swap law is *zero harness changes*; a sixth verb would break both. **One machine (founder-ruled, #2):** a cross-owner booking is an ordinary booking that needs one more yes, riding the two verbs that already exist:

- **`resolve(goal, boards, rules) → Handle`** — when `boards` includes a counterparty's **published availability ref** rather than a board the caller owns, `resolve` validates the goal against the authorized exposed windows and returns a placement handle, a `calculate`-class read until `commit` (§7): it stores no commitment, mints no ShareGrant, arms no hold and fires no recipient loop. Same signature — *"a placement, a proposal, or a structured decline"* — no new parameter. The initiator's ordinary **commitment-form `commit` of that handle** stores the one creator-owned commitment in `offered`, arms its hold (item 4), and emits the recipient's `offered` event (`../harness/SPEC.md §4`) in that creating transaction. The caller supplies only the authorized published availability ref as the counterparty target, never a caller-authored tenant edge or ShareGrant. At creation the engine rechecks that authority and the placement, then mints the **offer-side ShareGrant** scoped to this offered commitment for its recipient; this exposes only the shared offer, never the counterparty's private board. The creating act retains the initiator's floor basis (`../harness/SPEC.md §7`); neither this ShareGrant nor the availability ref supplies the counterparty's yes. Creation stores only offered/hold bookkeeping, not accepted consumption. It uses §6.6's caller-supplied write id and authenticated `(owner_org, credential)` apply scope: retry after a lost response returns the same offer result, with one commitment, one hold and one recipient firing. No goal-level request identity or new apply-proposal form is introduced.
- **`commit(offer_ref)`** — the existing **apply-proposal variant** (§6.5): re-runs every check as of *now*, applies atomically or returns `{conflict}`. Applying an accepted offer places the one commitment so it stands on the owner's board and, via the accept grant, on the counterparty's — **visible on both or on neither**; a half-shared state cannot exist.

*Why one machine is worth stating out loud:* the temptation is to give a cross-tenant operation its own dedicated entry point, and that instinct is exactly wrong here. A second write path is a second place for the tenant line to leak, a second thing the stub must fake, and a harness change at swap time. One seam, one set of checks — the same code path serves a booking on your own board and one that reaches another owner.

**The five questions, answered:**

1. **What crosses.** Only the goal and the counterparty's exposed windows. Never their other commitments, rules, parties, or money marks — none are readable to construct with, unless the counterparty has separately granted a higher rung.
2. **Consent is the counterparty's floor act, not the engine's.** The creating commit's transition to `status: offered` fires the **counterparty's** trigger loop (`../harness/SPEC.md §4`), never the read-only resolve. Their yes arrives per their stored **accept mode** — auto-accept what fits the schedule, auto-accept per person, or always manual (the first two are ordinary stored Grants, `../harness/SPEC.md §7`; the third is a live confirmation). **Silence is not acceptance** — an unanswered offer expires when its hold runs out; it never binds.
3. **What accepting does.** The counterparty's own stored, attributed yes (the matching accept-mode Grant or live confirmation) authorizes the engine to mint the distinct **accept-side ShareGrant** under FD-32 (`../RULINGS.md`); the offer-side grant is not that authority. `commit(offer_ref)` re-runs **every** §6.1 check on **both** boards as of *now* — capacity, buffers (including travel), latches, preconditions, governing rules — inside **one atomic transaction**. The one commitment then stands on both boards or on neither. **A conflict at this step is bounded the way item 4 bounds the racer** *(extended 2026-08-21 — the bound previously covered one path and the general §6.1 law leaked on the other)*: `reason` is stated no finer than the exposed projection ("that time is taken"), and `alternatives` draw from the **exposed availability only** — which private constraint class refused is the counterparty's information on every cross-tenant path, not just the race.
4. **Contention — the hold is the creator's setting.** The hold arms at the initiator's offer-creating commit, never at `resolve`. While `offered`, the targeted window on the counterparty's board is **held** as a temporary reservation, not accepted consumption, for the offer's `hold` duration — creator-set at share time, **default 5 minutes, range 0–24 hours**; `hold: 0` means no reservation, first commit wins. Expiry frees the window and the offer lapses (cascade behavior: `../harness/SPEC.md §4`). A racer against a zero-hold or expired offer gets the envelope's `conflict` with `alternatives` as `next`, drawn from the **exposed availability**, never the board.
5. **Partial failure and the honest dead end.** Decline, expiry, and commit-time conflict are first-class terminal states that place nothing on either board and return a structured reason — **a decline carries its reason** (rate, distance, timing, or a free note; founder-ruled, #8), so the owner learns why and may re-offer on changed terms; that re-offer *is* the counter, and no negotiation thread exists. Each terminal state is recorded on **both** boards as an attributed event `{who, basis, when}` — `../security/SPEC.md §9` N3 made concrete.

**Off-app counterparties are unaffected.** The form-export path (`../harness/SPEC.md §2`) retains its credential-scoped form and approved request-bound conversation: no guest tenant or cross-owner grant edge. Both consume only the authorized Shared/request or own-booking projection and fenced bookability (`../security/SPEC.md §3`); model assembly receives no private blocking events. Existing engine reads, typed values, current-state checks and atomic writes apply unchanged, including direct-call operation/target checks and the one-request/one-booking binding. Interactive normalization is a harness/model concern, never engine authority. The share seam is the *both-on-app* case only.

**Employed vs. freelance is tenancy, not a flag** *(named 2026-08-22 — the reading was load-bearing across Situation C and stated nowhere)*. An employed staff member's bookable board is an **org-owned resource board** (`owner_org`: the org, §1.2): no outside initiator can reach it absent a grant the org minted — which is N1/N4/I4's existing unconstructability, so *"internal staff are un-poachable"* is structural, not a rule anyone writes. A freelancer is their **own tenant**, reachable exactly because they granted availability (Situation C's Hug↔Matthew shape); own-first routing is then just the org resolving over boards it owns before boards it holds grants from. This paragraph adds no mechanism; it names the reading the scenarios already assert.

**Group availability composition — per-person grants only, and unknown stays unknown** *(added 2026-09-11; the saved group's own shape is `../harness/SPEC.md §3.6`'s, this section owns the permission it does and does not carry)*. A saved group is a roster, never a grant holder — *"Grants to groups are not in the menu"* above is unchanged — so composing a common opening across several members reads **each member's own `ShareGrant`, whose `holder` is the authorized querying principal and whose `scope` is that member's board, with only its valid `rung` (`availability`, `details`, or `edit`) visible to that principal, and nothing else. The granting member is the board owner: the grant's `holder` and `scope` express audience, while its `rung` expresses visibility/detail. Creating an account or joining a roster mints no grant, and no member's grant widens any other's. A member who granted nothing is **unknown, not free** (§1.7's `empty_means` discipline carried across the line), and the composition returns that member as a missing answer rather than intersecting over an assumption. Sharing is withdrawn by the owner's ordinary narrow-or-revoke on that one grant (§1.6's revocation path, the `../harness/SCENARIOS.md` G3 impact walk): a re-query after the revoke recomputes from current grants only, and returns nothing the revoked grant had exposed. **A composed common opening is a placement candidate and nothing more.** It is computed under the initiator's own current rules, binding as of the query (§1.5), and it supplies **neither agreement, nor coordinator authority, nor admission**: the counterparty's floor act (item 2 above) and `commit`'s re-run of every check as of *now* (item 3 above) still stand between a proposed slot and a booking, exactly as they do for a single counterparty. The group metadata sentence at §1.7 and this paragraph say the same thing from the two sides of the seam.

**Referral is a different thing and is deferred.** The share seam reaches a counterparty **the initiator already holds an availability grant from**. Handing a customer to another centre — discovering a stranger, passing their data — is the referral flow, deferred (see `../README.md`). The share seam does not build toward it and must not be read as a partial version of it.

## §8. `check_consistency` / `check_coverage`

*(Scope, stated since FD-97 re-typed `check_coverage` into a request/result union: this section's four-way classification governs `check_consistency` and the coverage union's **`board-structural` kind only**. The `covering-grant` kind is §1.6's pure lookup and never enters this classification.)*

- `check_consistency(rule | ruleset) → {conflicts, latent}` classifies **four** ways. The first three route by **authority**; the fourth routes by **satisfiability** and outranks them.
  1. **Governing conflict** — hard stop, the write is refused.
  2. **Own-rule conflict** — returned for the harness's override-with-reason path.
  3. **Latent** — alert, don't block.
  4. **Unsatisfiable** *(added 2026-08-06)* — **refused at write regardless of authority.** A combination admitted by §3’s clash tests is unsatisfiable when **no input can satisfy all its contributing predicates**: `min-occupancy.min > capacity.N`, `duration.min >` the longest allowed window, `duration.min >` a time-denominated pack. *Why this cannot be an own-rule conflict:* the override-with-reason path exists so an owner can say **"I know something the checker doesn't."** That is meaningful when the checker is being conservative. It is meaningless when the contributing rules are arithmetically incompatible — the owner is not overriding a judgment, they are asking to store a contradiction, and every future placement would simply decline. Storing it buys an unsailable boat that looks bookable. The class is this section's; the refusal **executes at §6.1's commit-time check set** — a commit carrying a rule diff runs this classification inside its own transaction (§6.1 owns the write path).
  - **The refusal names the fix**, so ordering is never a trap while authoring: *"capacity is 4 and you've set a minimum of 6 — raise the capacity or lower the minimum."* (The §6 voice: decline by naming the blocking rule, never a bare refusal.)
  - **The test is always against the *smallest* case, and this is the discipline that keeps the class honest.** A combination is unsatisfiable only when the **minimum** input already fails — `duration.min`, the longest available window, a single instance of a kind. It is **not** unsatisfiable merely because *large* inputs fail: a group of six exceeding a four-seat boat is an ordinary capacity refusal at commit (A1), not a broken ruleset, and refusing it at write would block a shop from storing rules that work fine for small bookings. **Getting this backwards turns a working configuration into an unstorable one**, which is worse than the problem the class was added to solve. **Every** row in §3's table must state its minimum-case test explicitly — not merely every new one. Two rows were narrowed by this discipline and two more were later **killed** by it (`dependency × pin` with the pin disambiguation; the three-rule joint row, whose minimum case reduced to an existing pair), all caught by asking "what is the smallest input that fails?" rather than by reading the row. For §3’s scoped attribute composition, the smallest input is one value from the declared domain: an empty intersection means none exists. Pairwise nonempty intersections do not establish such a witness. A row whose test is not printed has not been checked.

  Because the menu is closed, clash conditions are **enumerable: pairwise except for §3’s scoped effective attribute intersection**; the spec of each row's clash test lives with the menu entries (§3), not in open-ended logic, and §3's table marks which rows are unsatisfiable.

**The two return members, printed** *(2026-08-29 — both sides of this seam printed an ellipsis (`{conflicts, latent}` here, `{ conflicts:[…], latent:[…] }` at `../harness/INTERFACES.md §1.3`) while two MUST scenarios asserted the payload: `SCENARIOS.md` G3 requires "the alert returns naming the row" and G6 now requires all contributing source refs/typed operands and the remedy (the original pair-only assertion required both operands) §2 prints `TypedValue` as a literal struct for exactly this reason — two builders reading it must produce identical encodings.)*

```
Conflict {                            // a member of `conflicts[]`
  class    : governing | own-rule | latent | unsatisfiable,   // §8's four, CLOSED
  rules    : [ rule_ref, … ],        // every contributing source rule, ordered as operands
  operands : [ Rule.operand, … ],    // complete source operands (§3), positional to rules; never flattened leaves
  clash_row: §3-clash-table row id,   // which printed row was hit
  remedy   : ({ subject: rule_ref, direction: raise | lower, toward: TypedValue }
            | { subject: rule_ref, direction: widen, toward: TypedValue }
            | { edits: [{ subject: rule_ref, direction: widen, toward: TypedValue }, …] })[]
}                                     // the naming message as STRUCTURED FIELDS, never a prose blob

Latent {                              // a member of `latent[]`
  class    : latent,
  rules    : [ rule_ref, rule_ref ],
  operands : [ Rule.operand, Rule.operand ], // complete operands (§3), still exactly two
  clash_row: §3-clash-table row id
}                                     // NO remedy member — a latent alert does not block, so there is nothing to fix
```

- **Contributor cardinality:** `rules.length = operands.length`. Ordinary pair conflicts contain exactly two entries in each array; only §3’s scoped attribute composition permits more, with at least two distinct source rules and one corresponding complete original operand per rule (§3; typed leaves preserved). Preserve every contributor’s source reference and operand, even when the derived intersection is empty; do not replace them with a synthetic rule or two selected witnesses. `clash_row` still names the existing attribute-predicate row. `Latent` remains exactly two entries in each array.
- **The set remedy is a typed alternative:** each `direction: widen` edit requires `toward` to be a typed domain of the same attribute type, a strict superset of the named source domain. The existing single-subject alternative is valid when that edit alone restores a nonempty intersection with all other contributors. When several sources must change together, one `remedy` entry carries `edits`, a list of at least two distinct contributing source refs and their typed widenings. It has no outer `subject`, `direction` or `toward`; the single-subject and `edits` shapes are exclusive. Validate the **whole proposed edit set** against every contributor, including unchanged rules: it must restore a nonempty joint domain. Individually ineffective edits must not be presented as separate complete alternatives. No contributor disappears from `rules`/`operands` merely because its source is unchanged or has the same domain as another source. A string such as “relax rules”, an empty edit set, duplicate subjects, foreign/noncontributing refs, or a jointly ineffective set is not a remedy. Numeric `raise`/`lower` alternatives keep their existing shape.
- **Remedy choice and application:** Every remedy is a suggestion, never an applied edit or authority: the user chooses one complete alternative, with ordinary authority/confirmation checked for **every member** and governing protections unchanged. A suggestion cannot mint permission to edit a governing rule or read another tenant’s rules. A chosen coordinated alternative travels as one ordinary attach-point rule diff, under §6 items 1/3/4/6, never as a new verb or apply-proposal form. Revalidate its types, source refs, current authorization and resulting ruleset against current stored rules inside that transaction; stale suggestions confer no right to apply old math or substitute different content. Failure of any member or the joint check refuses the whole diff; no partial intermediate writes, version/provenance changes or member-by-member commits occur (the failed write-id record remains §6.6’s exception). A changed source requiring a different edit returns for renewed choice/confirmation. Success records all chosen rule edits and their ordinary attribution together under one write id; replay follows §6.6 without reapplying members.
- **The remedy is structured, not a sentence.** §8's own example — *"capacity is 4 and you've set a minimum of 6 — raise the capacity or lower the minimum"* — is two `remedy` entries, and the harness narrates them in any language. A build that ships the English string satisfies G6 by accident and fails FR15 the moment the owner's stored language is not `en`.
- **`class` is a closed enumeration**; an unrecognized member is refused under §2's unknown-member rule, never classified to the nearest neighbour.
- `check_coverage(board) → {missing_required}` is **structural only**: required attributes/rules present per the governing kind's schema. Semantic completeness is undecidable and never claimed.

## §9. Failure & edge behavior

- **External travel source down** → the gap is unknown → fail closed (not offered, not proposed) with a structured reason the harness can narrate.
- **Beyond-horizon request** → §5 owns its bounded preparation effects before cache-only projection computation, using §1.4's horizon-job discipline; quota windows spanning the horizon edge compute from pattern + instances (materialization never changes a quota verdict). **The capacity counterpart, previously missing** *(2026-08-21 — the bullet covered quota only, so the rolling horizon job could materialize a pattern instance into a slot a one-off had since taken, and being "invisible to the harness" it could neither park nor ask)*: **materialization never changes a capacity verdict either.** A pattern instance whose slot is already occupied when the horizon reaches it materializes as a **conflicted bare draft** — a draft carrying a structured conflict record, drawing no capacity, invisible to every availability projection, readable through `calculate`'s stored-object member — and the harness's next firing surfaces it as an ordinary parked question (keep the one-off / move the instance / except the date). **Writing a conflicted bare draft registers a clock trigger at the affected instant** *(2026-08-22 — on a board with no scheduled firings before the horizon edge, "the next firing" never came: each law was obeyed and the outcome was a silent drop; `min-occupancy` already registers its own trigger and composes — this is the same mechanism)*: the job still only records, and the trigger is the harness's ordinary clock source firing. **And the draft is visible on the owner's display projection** — marked as conflicted, invisible only to *availability* — so the owner sees their weekly class in trouble rather than a hole. Never a double-book, never a silent drop, and the job itself still asks nothing (§0 holds: it *records* the question).
- **Stale handle / stale proposal at redemption** → the envelope's `conflict` with its reason; fresh recompute required; never partial application.
- **The engine itself unreachable, or a call timed out** → the `unavailable | timeout` members (`../harness/INTERFACES.md §1`, 2026-08-21) — a different answer from `{conflict}` and never collapsed into it; the harness surfaces a gap attended and parks unattended, and recovery runs through the §6.6 write id, never through re-authoring.
- **Clock edges** — DST inside a recurring pattern: the pattern's zone governs expansion; instants convert after. A local time erased by spring-forward materializes at the gap's first existing instant; a local time duplicated by fall-back materializes exactly once, at its earlier-UTC occurrence. No day yields zero or two instances, and a duration spanning the transition keeps its true elapsed length. Stated once, here; tested at M5–M6.
- **A restore (owner-scoped, `../security/SPEC.md §8`) walks the same checks as any write** *(2026-08-21 — the ordinary conflict case was absent from all three of §8's printed limits)*: a restored version that conflicts with writes made since the backup — a freed slot since re-let, a capacity since consumed — **does not apply silently**; the conflict set returns as a parked owner decision, per row. And a **latch present anywhere in the object's history binds the restore**: replaying a pre-cancellation version cannot resurrect a cancelled commitment, because the latch law (§6.2) is checked against the full version chain, never just the version being restored. A restore recovers structure; it never re-runs the past.
- **Long `resolve` computations run inside the deployment, never on the seam call's thread** — the async-era pin behind §1's every-verb-returns-a-promise law (`INTERFACES.md` §1's async row cites this bullet as its home).
- All failure paths return data; none skip a check, none invent a partial result.
