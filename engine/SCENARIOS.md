## A — Atomic commit & races
- **A1 [race, one winner]** Two commits race for the last unit of capacity. Exactly one succeeds; the other returns `{conflict, reason, alternatives}` listing still-open times. *(Sofia's 3:00; Situation-D's last Friday slot; Situation-C's last boat seats.)* **Shared-request composition (`SPEC.md §§1.7/6`, `../harness/SPEC.md §3.6`; Matt S13d/S13e/S14c, MT-45/83/84/85/88, HR-17/30/32):** race a two-participant request against an independent solo request for one teacher interval, with both winner orders. One commitment/Order confirms for the winning request, consuming the teacher once and applying every selected participant's applicable count/time draw together. The loser writes no participant, placement, history or reminder state; its existing apply-result record is the only failure-side write. Retain its inputs, obtain a fresh allowed alternative and confirm separately. Give one of four selected participants a failing quota, then inject a failure after a member is prepared: neither may leave any partial group state. Confirm a valid four-participant fixture and assert one booking/four participant draws, seat-board quantity distinct from teacher quantity, and no multiplied travel/buffer. Move/remove/rejoin with a supplied supported terms/policy fixture: either the whole resulting participant/placement/draw set applies or the original remains. Roster edits and attendance-only reports do not change this set. These controls test transaction composition, not conditional-price or cancellation-policy implementation; those stay at §§1.5/3 and their mapped BUILD gates. **All-participant atomicity against the allowance, with holds** *(added 2026-09-11; `SPEC.md §3`'s participant-quota block)*: give exactly one of the four participants an exhausted monthly count and assert the whole confirmation refuses with no count, minute, placement, reminder or history write for **any** participant; repeat with that participant's time constraint exhausted instead and assert the same, the refusal naming the other rule in neither case. Then hold the contested allowance in an unsettled `hold` and race a concurrent confirmation: the two cannot overshoot one allowance, and the hold's lapse releases its reservation without its ever having been a draw and without reaching any cancellation stage.
- **A2 [all checks, one transaction]** A commit that passes capacity but violates a buffer gap against its *following* neighbor fails atomically — nothing partial is stored.
- **A3 [governing wipe rejected]** A lower-authority diff whose effect would disable a governing rule does not apply; the governing rule remains in force. *(Mirror of harness D-series.)*
- **A4 [write-id idempotency — §6.6]** A commit applies; the identical write id is re-committed: the **original result** returns and nothing applies twice. The same id arrives with a **different payload**: refused (`{conflict, reason: write-id-reuse}`), neither payload honored. A commit **fails**; replaying its id returns the same failure. A **foreign tenant** replays the id: nothing matches, nothing returns — the index is `(owner_org, credential)`-scoped. *(The engine half of harness K4.)* **Participant-quota replay arm** *(added 2026-09-11; `SPEC.md §3`'s participant-quota block)*: a confirmed multi-participant booking replayed under its original write id returns the original result and adds no second draw for any participant; a refused or stale participant selection replayed under its own id returns the same refusal and draws nothing either time. **Contact transaction arm (`../harness/SPEC.md §3.12`; Steps 1/4 supply storage/read prerequisites, Step 5 applies):** new party plus PartyContact is one atomic create and one original applied pair on lost-response replay; one existing Arthur plus three resolved additions yields four identities. A typo/name/channel edit, explicit additional-channel association and directory/Student retirement/reactivation retain the chosen party and its quota/booked references. A same-name stranger stays separate. Resolve concrete current contact-slot, preferred-channel, request/link, roster and future-booking dependencies under authenticated authority; stale revisions, a newly inserted reference or an invalid member refuse whole, with no partial history/term/credential change. Run both serializations of reference creation versus retirement: either the reference is validated against the retired result or retirement rechecks that reference, never a dangling binding. Independent saved contacts survive parent-draft abandonment; no group or booking lifecycle is implemented by a contact transaction. **Request identity and authority arm (`SPEC.md §§1.7/6`; Matt S13d/S13e, MT-82/83/85, CRUD-25/26):** lose a shared confirmation acknowledgement and replay the same id/payload: one original booking/result returns. Submit the same request from another admitted link with a fresh write id: no second booking or teacher/participant draw. A changed payload requires a new accepted same-booking edit, never another confirmation. Race revocation/suspension with apply in both orders: a prior successful commit survives as history; revocation first refuses stale pending work. Race group/member retirement against new request/link binding in both orders: either the binding sees retirement or retirement sees the dependency, never a dangling reference or partial deletion. A separately reviewed retained binding allows retirement without changing contacts or booked participants. Reopening recovers stable group/request identities and current revisions. Step 1 supplies stored legacy/no-authority defaults; Step 5 executes these writes, and real token/admission integration remains the existing swaps. **Bound-quote replacement arm (`SPEC.md §1.5`; Matt S13c/S14c, MT-37/84, HR-32):** walk one group booking's roster as **one commitment** — four to three to two booked participants with the group tier's hourly rate and its one-hour total unchanged at 2000, two to one accepted at 1500 after a recorded agreement, one back to two accepted at 2000 — asserting throughout one commitment identity, one teaching reservation and one participant-draw set, never a cancel-and-rebook pair, and asserting each accepted step's previous quote still readable. Then the refusals, each leaving the stored quote, roster and draws exactly as they were: replaying an accepted replacement's write id returns the **original** result and applies no second replacement; the same id with a changed roster, rate or total refuses under this row's payload-mismatch rule; a candidate whose agreement was recorded against the **prior** quote revision refuses as stale; a candidate whose acting authority was revoked or suspended after that agreement was recorded refuses as unauthorized; and a reported attendance or `actual_end` submitted on its own changes no quote at all, because the replacement act's required members are absent from it.
- **A5 [re-widening cannot double-book — §6.7]** A rental ends early (`actual_end` 14:00), the freed 14:00–17:00 is re-let, then a diff corrects the already-recorded `actual_end` back to 16:00: the write returns `{conflict, reason, alternatives}` and **parks for a human**; refusing this expanding correction adds no overlap and preserves both existing records. - **A8 [the `proposal_ref` form returns the proposal's ref — Q2-003]** An apply-proposal commit returns `applied_ref` naming **the proposal applied** — all-or-nothing per §6.5, the applied-result covering the third input form.

## B — Latches
- **B1 [no un-expire]** A hold lapses (`expired_at` set by the virtual clock); the signature arrives late. Evidence is recorded; status stays `expired`; the unit is never re-held (`../harness/SPEC.md §3.4`, enforced by `SPEC.md §6` item 2). Confirmation/completion/re-hold attempts refuse, including a backdated accept; expiry and provenance stay unchanged. At BUILD Step 1 use store fixtures. At Step 5, this existing latch commit gate also walks B2/B3's valid and refused transitions through `commit`/read: confirm then cancel; confirm then recorded completion; cancelled/declined then attempted revival; completed then conflicting cancellation; each original fact survives. These commit-path controls close here, never from Step 1's storage-only proof. *(Situation-B's lapsed hold.)*
- **B2 [terminal precedence and confirmation history]** Store fixtures from `../harness/SPEC.md §3.4`'s table derive: confirmed future booking → `confirmed`; confirmed plus start reached → `active`; confirmed plus authorized cancellation → `cancelled`; confirmed plus valid recorded completion → `completed`. Retain the same `confirmed_at {by, at}` and provenance throughout. A cancelled-but-otherwise-complete commitment reports `cancelled`, not `completed`; a later admissible return fact cannot revive it or retroactively replace cancellation. A completed fixture refuses a later competing cancellation/decline/expiry, including a backdated input. A declined fixture refuses later acceptance; first-terminal precedence never mistakes confirmation for a terminal outcome. Pending plus blocked yields `blocked` with both conditions retained; clearing one condition leaves the other. Step 1 owns these storage/derivation controls; B1 owns their existing commit-path close at Step 5.
- **B3 [no clearing or rewriting write]** Any write attempting to null a set latch or replace its author/timestamp is rejected by the store (`SPEC.md §1.3`). For each declared latch, read back the original fact/provenance after refusal. Adding an authorized `cancelled_at` after confirmation and recording valid completion without clearing `confirmed_at` are positive controls, not conflicting writes (`../harness/SPEC.md §3.4`). Attempts to revive cancelled/expired/declined state refuse even when every precondition is now met; an unbacked completion assertion never substitutes for the kind's recorded act (`SPEC.md §3` precondition).
- **B4 [escalation stored, terminal write-once]** An `Escalation` (`../harness/SPEC.md §3.9`; stored per SPEC §1.13) is written, its `ladder_state` advanced across two virtual-clock firings (rung 1 → rung 2), then its status latched `timed_out_parked`. A later write attempting to reopen it (revert to `open`) is **rejected by the store**, and the co-occurring `needs_human` park is clearable only by a human (B3's discipline applied to the escalation). Assert the object **persists across firings** (read back from the store, not from harness memory) and that **no escalation-specific entry point** is called — only `commit`/`calculate`. *(The engine half of `../harness/SCENARIOS.md D12–D17; §3.9`.)*

- **B5 [no delete entry point — §1.10]** The engine's callable surface contains **no operation whose effect removes a stored row** — asserted structurally as an absence over the entry-point set (the K2 pattern applied to deletion), plus one behavioral half: cancelling, revoking, expiring, and withdrawing a bare draft (§1.4) each leave the row readable in history.

- **B6 [a historic shape stays readable — §1.10]** *(added 2026-08-29 — §1.10's additive-only law had no scenario, no `§10` row and no build step, in an append-only store where the rows it governs are permanent by law, so a violation is not recoverable by a later fix.)* *Given* rows written under an object shape, *when* the shape gains a field with a declared default, *then* every historic row **reads back through the new shape** with the declared default supplied **on read** — and the stored bytes of those rows are asserted **unchanged**, because *"upcast on read, never rewrite"* is falsified by a migration that quietly succeeds. And a shape change that **removes or re-types** a field is **refused at write as a storage fact** — asserted as refusal, the G8 pattern, not as a validation message and not as a migration that runs. **Authored schema data is distinct (`../harness/SPEC.md §3.3`):** store field id/revision, label, supported type, required status, scoped default and dependencies, with a use bound to that revision. Append a label revision and a supported type revision without changing the old bytes or the old form/value meaning. The current pointer changes only on a validated write; duplicate labels never retarget a rule. Clear an optional value and reopen empty despite a default; restore it as a new attributed edit. Retire an independent optional field and omit it from new uses of that revision while the prior bound use stays readable. Assert encrypted historical content obeys the existing missing-key boundary rather than promising perpetual readable PII; this uses the existing keyed fixture, not live key deletion. Step 1 proves stored read/shape controls, Step 5 proves lifecycle commits and races. **PendingDecision subject arm** *(added 2026-09-12; `SPEC.md §1.14`'s additive subject member)*: *given* a `PendingDecision` row written with `commitment` and no `subject_ref`, *then* it reads back through the successor shape with its subject **upcast from `commitment`** and its stored bytes asserted unchanged; a successor row whose subject is a commitment carries both members with the same ref and reads identically; and a **rule-subject** row carries `subject_ref` alone and reads back with `commitment` **absent** — assert no commitment is synthesized for it, and that a read still declaring `commitment` required **refuses that row whole** rather than returning an invented booking. A row carrying both members with **different** refs, and a row carrying **neither**, each refuse at the boundary as `schema-mismatch` with no member preferred and no default supplied. Step 1 proves these stored read and shape controls; the harness's own typed read, stub and fixture are `../harness/BUILD.md`'s carrier obligation under `../harness/INTERFACES.md §7.2`, and nothing here proves that propagation has been written.

- **B7 [a restore conflicts loudly and cannot resurrect a latch — §9, §6.2]** *(added 2026-08-29 — `§10` carried the restore row as the one ledger entry whose construction site pointed outside the package, while no engine scenario mentioned a restore and no BUILD step named one.)* Two arms, asserted in one run. **Conflict arm:** a version restored over a slot that has since been **re-let** returns the conflict set as a **parked owner decision, per row** (`needs_human`, §1.3) — assert **per-row granularity**, not one merged park, and assert **nothing applied silently**. **Latch arm:** restoring a **pre-cancellation** version of a cancelled commitment is **refused**, asserted against the **full version chain** with the latch set on a version *other than* the one being restored — a check that reads only the restored version passes this scenario wrongly, which is the whole reason the arm is written this way. And assert the boundary §9 draws — *"A restore recovers structure; it never re-runs the past"* — as the absence of any replayed terminal event. *(The restore operation's own home is `../security/SPEC.md §8`; this is the engine law §9 states about it.)*

- **B8 [the conversion link is engine-minted and adds no read power — §1.1]** *(added 2026-08-29 — FD-46/FD-51's party↔principal link was defined in `SPEC.md §1.1` with engine-enforced semantics, absent from Step 1's object roster, and gated only by `../security/SCENARIOS.md` N6 in another package.)* **The structural half (Step 1):** **no caller-constructable entry point mints a link** — asserted as an absence over the entry-point set, the B5 pattern; and a link naming two tenants is **engine-authored or it does not exist** (I4's discipline applied to identity — the mint act itself is the security layer's claim, `../security/SPEC.md §2`). **The read-union half (Step 4):** the link's assembled starting history is **exactly** the union of what the linked party's booking-bound tokens could already read — asserted as **set equality**, never as a sample — and drawn *"over unrevoked, unexpired tokens only"*: a **revoked** token contributes nothing and an **expired** one contributes nothing, each asserted in the same run, so a build that assembles over the whole token history fails here. **And it re-homes nothing:** every carried record keeps its owning tenant after conversion, asserted on each record's stored `owner_org`. **And no presented-token discriminator** *(added 2026-09-11 with `SPEC.md §1.1`'s aligned pointer)*: the stored link's `basis` resolves to the security layer's recorded verified-channel act, asserted on the stored link; and the entry-point walk additionally asserts that **no input mints a link from a presented token** — a run presenting two parties' tokens read from one verified channel mints nothing, asserted as absence of the link, with every record's stored `owner_org` unchanged. *(The engine half of `../security/SCENARIOS.md` N6 — the two must agree or conversion means different things on the two sides of the seam.)*
