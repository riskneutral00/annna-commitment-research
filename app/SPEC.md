# annnä App — SPEC (the one-canvas surface)

*The human-facing layer: it renders surfaces, moves data in and out, and delivers outward messages. It contains **zero harness logic, zero engine math, and zero model calls in any render or write path**. Everything it shows was decided elsewhere; everything it collects is handed to the harness untouched. Technology names live in `INTERFACES.md` (downward), `DESIGN.md` (mechanics), and `BUILD.md` — this file is behavior only.*

---

## §0. What the app is

- **Doors and windows, not rooms.** If you deleted the app, the truth (engine) and the behavior (harness) would survive intact; only the ways in and out would be gone. Nothing is decided here.
- **Deterministic rendering.** The render path is generate-once-render-forever: any generative composition happens at authoring time through the harness; rendering stored structure calls no model, ever. The same payload renders the same surface, always.
- **Two audiences, two shapes.** The **owner** gets the one-canvas app (§1) behind sign-in. The **off-app guest** gets traditional token-URL pages (§5) with no account or installed app — a form and the approved request-bound conversation under `../harness/SPEC.md §2` and `../security/SPEC.md §3`'s credential-derived read/operation limits. There is no third shape.
- **Interfaces are data.** Every surface the app renders — forms, guest pages, generative compositions — is declarative stored structure, never generated code. Amending a surface is a data write through the normal seam.

## §1. The one-canvas model (the owner side)

There are no pages on the owner side. There is **one canvas** — the wallpaper filling the screen — and things that sit, rise, and sink on it:

- **The board** sits on the canvas (~90% of it) and is **home**: opening the app shows your board. Board rendering law: §2.
- **The console** — the agent's mouth and ears — is a movable element that is **always present**: on the board, off the board, and while any riser is open. The owner never "goes to" the agent. **Its position on the canvas and whether it is minimized are session-ephemeral display state, outside §7's closed set** *(stated 2026-08-29 — a draggable, minimizable element whose persistence nobody specified)*: they do not survive a reload and therefore never ride `display_settings`. Persisting either would require it to join §7's enumeration and be boring-stashed by `DESIGN.md`'s by-reference rule, which is the recorded alternative this default preserves. *(drafted, not founder-ratified)* *(This deliberately supersedes the prior build's "parallel doors never compete" rule, which hid the console during editing — reversed 2026-08-05 precisely so the owner can talk to the agent about the thing that's open. Recorded in `DESIGN.md` §Supersessions.)*
- **Islands are retired as chrome** *(FD-39, relocate-never-delete)* — the small floating controls that sat at the canvas edges moved intact into the console's settings pane, and nothing lives at the canvas edge at any width; on mobile the console's bottom bar is the phone's entire chrome. Placement law: `DESIGN.md`.
- **Everything else is a riser.** Tap a commitment block and the board recedes; the commitment's riser rises on the canvas. Edit by hand or by talking to the console; save/exit sinks it and the board returns. **An unsaved riser edit is ephemeral** *(stated 2026-08-29 — the fate of a half-typed edit was unspecified, and a builder would have had to invent either a draft store or a discard)*: closing without saving discards the pending edit; C4’s no-change round-trip is unchanged. An independently accepted saved-place write (§8) and the harness’s proposal bookkeeping (`../harness/SPEC.md` §§3.13/6) keep their own scope; neither persists the abandoned form as applied domain state. This is not an addition to §7’s display settings. *(drafted, not founder-ratified)* Settings, the SOP library, audit views: the same move. *(The Plan view was retired 2026-08-09 with the due chip — tasks are ordinary blocks on the board and have no separate peer surface. `DESIGN.md` §Board rendering.)* The app is flat — board, console, risers. Motion is a sink/rise transition, never a page load.
- **What the commitment riser carries — H2 in full, not a bare form.** Its anchor is **the same form the commitment was created with** — resolved from the commitment's stored `form_ref` (`../harness/SPEC.md §3.4`, FR16), never re-derived from `kind`; a commitment with no `form_ref` is BARE and anchors on its title. And the riser also carries everything that *supports* that commitment: its rules, its SOP attachment, its stakeholder assembly, its availability-sharing. One surface — the harness's per-commitment support surface, whole. A build that renders the creation form alone and scatters the support content elsewhere has mis-read this section.

**Canvas state grounds the harness.** The harness's `render(surface, payload)` seam maps surface → canvas state, not surface → page. **And the board's two content sources have one precedence rule** *(2026-08-22 — a 3 a.m. guest booking produces a projection push and no `render` call, so both sources are always live)*: **`render(board, payload)` sets the surface; the projection sets the contents** — and the app discards any pushed or rendered payload whose `store_version` is older than what it holds (`../engine/INTERFACES.md §2a`). The seam:

| Harness surface | Canvas state |
|---|---|
| `board` (H1) | the board, risen (home) |
| `commitment-page` (H2) | that commitment's riser open — **the riser IS the per-commitment support surface** |
| `console` | the console element (present in every state) |

Which harness instance grounds an interaction (H1 board vs H2 support) is read off what's risen. The app reports the active canvas state with every console utterance — the **view-context** `{surface, visible_range?, selected_ref?, draft_ref?, draft_revision?, field_ref?, scope_ref?, ask_ref?}`: which surface, the visible date range, and the selected card if any — so the harness's standing frame carries the right `surface` **and the referent for a deictic utterance** (`../harness/SPEC.md §8`, `../harness/INTERFACES.md §2.1`). The app doesn't interpret, it reports; resolving "this" against `selected_ref` is the harness's job, not the app's.

**Field and rule rows expose the actual operation** (`../harness/SPEC.md §3.3`, the lifecycle home). Render the selected object/use or definition, supported type, required/optional status, scoped default versus current value, and calculated read-only outputs; retain the stable slot binding and current revision in its action payload. Edit focuses that current row in the shared console/form draft (`../harness/INTERFACES.md §3.1`); a changed label never chooses a different binding. Remove distinguishes a pending row, one value and a saved definition retirement, showing the supplied concrete effects and valid dependency resolutions. An independent optional removal can complete; a required/referenced refusal keeps the current state visible beside the pending change. Required or calculated rows never promise arbitrary deletion/overwrite. Retired slots disappear from new use of the updated definition, while reopening a bound form retains its historical meaning. Explicit empty remains visible and editable on reopen, with a supplied missing joining-detail explanation where relevant; no example URL or default is inserted. Definition changes use the existing Replace existing/Save as new branch, while per-use value edits do not. The app echoes exact operation, binding, revision and scope to the harness; it neither decides dependency validity nor interprets a successful render as a saved or enforced rule.

### §1.1 Loading, empty, and failure states

The canvas is never blank and never lies about progress. Two new laws, five citations — the rest of this list is already law elsewhere and is only gathered here so a builder finds every degraded state in one place:

- **First load** *(law)* — the canvas (wallpaper) renders immediately; the board's blocks appear when the engine projection arrives. The loading skeleton is the board frame itself — never a spinner-as-a-page.
- **Console call in flight / timed out** *(law)* — an utterance in flight shows as pending in the transcript; the give-up point is the **per-firing budget** (`../harness/SPEC.md §4`'s fourth termination condition — a console turn makes at least two model calls plus engine calls, so the single-call `timeout_ms` was never the right bound; corrected 2026-08-21), at which the turn **parks and the console renders the park** — the same parked-card shape trigger firings use (§3), saying plainly that the agent didn't finish, with re-send offered. **Re-send is a fresh firing with fresh write ids and is offered only when the parked turn reached no commit point** — a turn that committed is resumed or abandoned through its turn state (`../harness/SCENARIOS.md` X4's law, which the console rides like any client), never blindly re-fired into a duplicate create. No partial answer is ever rendered as an answer, and nothing is inferred app-side.
- **Empty board** — honest emptiness: the board renders with no blocks, console present, nothing nagging; the way in from here is §8's neutral affordances (FD-102). Emptiness is a state, not an error; §8's starters are how an empty board is offered content, once.
- **Generative render failure** — the rejected-render poka-yoke (§4): off-catalog schemas fail loudly at parse, never silently.
- **Guest page, bad or expired token** — indistinguishable non-recognition (`../security/SPEC.md §3`): bad, revoked, and never-issued tokens all get the same neutral not-active page.
- **Delivery failure** — a recorded result plus the card in the console (§6); the failure lives on the record, never only in a toast. **An `unavailable` or `timeout` from a delivery provider is its own outcome, never collapsed into `delivered-failed`** *(2026-08-21 — the members are seam law, `../harness/INTERFACES.md §3.3`, and were absent from this package: a builder whose provider timed out had only a false semantic claim to record)*: the send's fate is **unknown**, which is a different fact from known-failed; it is emitted as such, and **an unknown-fate send is never retried** — the act parks with `needs_human.reason: unverified` and the owner decides *(2026-08-29 — this clause named a retry discipline the outward seam does not have: the write id is a `commit` parameter, `send` takes none, and an outward send is not idempotent at all)*. Normative home: `../harness/INTERFACES.md §1`, whose own outward-call carve-out says it — *"The outward app calls are excluded from that retry sentence"* — with the seam members at `§3.3`.
- **Projection subscription gone quiet** *(row added 2026-08-29 — the seam mandates this rendering and cites this list as the law's reach, and the list carried no such row: a builder had a MUST with no gathered home)* — a subscription that goes quiet is a **state, never an empty board**: the board renders **stale, reconnecting**, stated plainly, never a silently stale view. Home: `../engine/INTERFACES.md §2a` — *"A subscription that goes quiet is a *state*, never an empty board"*. Gated at C15.
- **Marketplace unreachable** — honest absence on the shelf; faves still served from on-device skins (S6; `INTERFACES.md §2`).

## §2. The board

Behavioral law (visual law: `DESIGN.md` §Board):

- The board shows **placed commitments as blocks** and nothing else at rest. Time is block *data*, never block position or size — the board is calm because the truth is layered, not flattened.
- **A conflicted bare draft renders, marked** *(added 2026-08-29 — the engine mandates the owner see it and this package said only "nothing else at rest", under which a builder would have dropped it)*. When materialization meets an occupied slot the engine writes a **conflicted bare draft** — a draft drawing no capacity and invisible to every availability projection — and it is **selectable in the board payload and renders marked as conflicted, never silently absent**. This is the exception the home already mandates, not a widening of the sentence above: `../engine/SPEC.md §9` — *"the draft is visible on the owner's display projection — marked as conflicted, invisible only to *availability*"* — and the board's payload contract carries it (`../engine/SPEC.md §1.7b`, whose selectable set names **conflicted bare drafts**). The app marks what it is given and decides nothing about the conflict; the owner's answer arrives as an ordinary parked question through the console (§3). Gated at C16.
- **Tasks are blocks, the same component as events** *(founder-ruled 2026-08-09, FD-23 — this reverses "task deadlines are never grid blocks", the due chip and the Plan riser)*. Events pack to the top of their column, tasks to the bottom of the board, and the board scrolls asymmetrically between the two bands. Visual law and the exact packing rules: `DESIGN.md` §Board rendering. Behaviorally nothing changed — a task was always a commitment with different axes set (`../engine/SPEC.md`), and it was only ever the *rendering* that split them.
- **Which blocks are legible at rest is the owner's setting, in four parts** — value, scope, keep-awake mark, keep-awake toggle (`DESIGN.md` §Board rendering, *The wake policy*; requirement: `../PRD.md` RQ-10/RQ-11). All four are **display state**: the board renders differently, and nothing about the commitment, the placement, or any engine value changes. Engaging a block wakes it fully whatever the rule says.
- Recurring commitments display the engine's **materialized instances**. Navigation beyond the frontier uses the subscription window `{from, to}` sideways, never a harness call, at `../engine/INTERFACES.md §2a`; `../engine/SPEC.md §5` owns bounded preparation before projection computation. While that span is unloaded/unavailable, show unknown/loading or stale state under §1.1, never verified empty/free. The app invents no occurrences or scheduling math. Gated at C6.
- **Event, Task and Routine labels** reflect stored meaning: Routine is the Pattern/occurrence mapping at `../engine/SPEC.md §1.4`; Event/Task remain the roles at `../harness/SPEC.md §3.4`. The selected occurrence, full date/year and zone stay attached to the form as the owner changes Day/Week/Month views. Render usual settings beside the dated exception and the engine's checked stop scope/unresolved instances; do not substitute a weekday label for identity. The console's submitted instruction, proposal OK/Edit and final form Save preserve `../harness/SPEC.md §6`'s confirmation and scope. Closing an unsaved draft does not persist it. These are existing catalog/form surfaces, no app-side recurrence classifier or new primitive.
- Everything on a block or chip is a **display projection from an engine handle** (the board's payload contract: `../engine/SPEC.md §1.7b`). The app never computes a time, a gap, or an availability — not even for preview.
- **The owner's own in-flight act may echo, marked as in-flight** *(2026-08-22 — a subscription surface that only ever renders the store makes the owner's own confirmed tap appear to do nothing for a round-trip, and nothing said whether the echo was lawful)*: render only the confirmed act, correlated to its existing write id and submitted draft revision. The commit result binds that echo to `{applied_ref, committed_revision}`; only an authorized projection carrying that exact pair in `applied_revisions[]` (`../engine/SPEC.md §5`, transport `../engine/INTERFACES.md §2a`) acknowledges it. The projection replaces the acknowledged echo; it clears only that applied revision, never a later pending edit to the same object. An unrelated push, object identity alone or a larger `store_version` cannot acknowledge a write. Apply §1's staleness discard to projection/render contents before reconciliation. Consume the correlated owner-act result independently (`../harness/INTERFACES.md §3.1`), even if its accompanying display render is stale; retaining a result never authorizes repainting old contents. If projection and result arrive in reverse order, retain the authorized association until they can be matched; duplicate results/pushes are inert and cannot revive an echo or overwrite a newer draft. Coalesced/reconnected projections use the engine's incorporated association, never a guessed revision order. A refused commit removes only its echo and surfaces the refusal; a missing/unknown result or missing association remains unresolved and follows existing write-id recovery, not a success claim or a fresh write. The echo is display-only: nothing else may read it, and no time, gap, quota, availability, target authority or confirmation derives from it. §10's no-app-computed-values law is untouched.

## §3. The console

- **Text, answers and proposals use distinct controls.** Render the shared form/console draft and its selected object/use, changed field, scope and proposal identity from `../harness/INTERFACES.md §3.1`. Send submits the edited text; a finite choice submits once; proposal OK/Edit, picker Save and final Save follow that contract's separate transitions. Show an accepted proposal as accepted into the draft until a confirmed write result establishes persistence. Stale/refused acceptance keeps the current candidate visible and editable; opening a saved row never silently replaces an unsaved draft.

- **The interpretation posture (FD-103, `../RULINGS.md`)**: a **defensible basis** licenses a reversible internal interpretation **without asking**, and defensible interpretations **apply automatically** — high or low confidence is review *routing* (which pile the owner looks at), never the act/ask boundary, and **no numeric threshold exists**; an **adequate basis** is what outward acts require (the harness floor, unchanged); a **question** is asked only where no defensible basis exists AND the error would be material or irreversible.
- Chat with the owner's agent. **Voice is deferred post-v1 (FD-48, 2026-08-22)** — "text and voice are the same console" stands as design intent, scoped: the v1 console is text, and when voice lands it is *this* console behind *this* seam, with its own ruling, scenarios, and a transcription member the model seam does not yet carry (`../model/INTERFACES.md §2` is pinned at four calls; adding one is a spec change with its own ruling). Nothing in v1 may build on or assume a voice path — until it was scoped, the sentence was a MUST with no scenario, no step, and no seam behind it.
- **Agent-initiated speech is UNGOVERNED, pending design (FR-B, 2026-08-06).** The former rule — *"the console never speaks first"* — was **removed by founder ruling**, deliberately and entirely. It is written here rather than left as an absence, because a deleted prohibition and a granted permission look identical in a diff and are opposite things.
  - **Removing the rule did not grant anything.** There is today **no specified circumstance** in which the agent initiates. Nothing in v1 may be built on the assumption that it can.
  - **What still binds, on its own reasoning:** every outward act to a third party remains floor-gated (`../harness/SPEC.md §7`) — that was never derived from this rule. So "ungoverned" means *ungoverned toward the owner, in the owner's own console* — and nowhere else.
  - **Owner: the founder.** This is a product-voice decision (when, if ever, annnä opens its mouth first), not an architecture gap an implementer can close. Until it is ruled, the honest state is this paragraph.
  - **The interim build posture:** build the console as request-response. An initiating console is a design change with its own scenarios, not a flag someone flips.
- **Trigger results surface as cards — an independent law, not a leftover of the removed rule.** When a trigger fires with no human present (a cancellation, a returned form — `../harness/SPEC.md §4`), the result **surfaces as a card with its cause visible**, and the agent's question may ride that card, sitting there until the owner next looks. **A parked firing takes this same card class** *(pointer resolved 2026-08-29 — §1.1 already claimed "the same parked-card shape trigger firings use (§3)" and §3 named no such class, so the cross-reference resolved to nothing)*: a firing that parks — including a parked console turn (§1.1) — surfaces as a card of this class, its cause visible in the same slot, with §1.1's re-send rule governing what the card offers. This holds because *the owner must be able to see what happened while they were away and why* — it would be true under either ruling, and it survives FR-B unchanged. **A card raised by a `PendingDecision`** (`../engine/SPEC.md §1.14` — a run under its minimum is the first instance) renders the **engine's named choices as its actions**: the app names none of its own, renames and reorders none, and applies none locally. Picking one emits it through the seam and nothing else — which is the same law as everywhere else on this surface (`§10`: no app-computed values, no client-side policy), stated here because a choice list looks like copy and is not. A **restore card** is offered when a board comes back short of its last backup watermark — what is missing, when the last good copy was taken, one confirm, nothing written before it. The law is `../security/SPEC.md §8`; this file only says where it appears.
- Renders `narrate` output verbatim and **proposal cards** (anatomy in `DESIGN.md`): a proposal is shown, confirmed or dismissed, and collapses to its consequence. Nothing applies silently. For an unsaved edit, render the draft/proposal correlation, scope and sourced engine values from `../harness/INTERFACES.md §3.1`; picker changes, proposal OK/Edit and final Save echo the current revision. Only final Save applies the accepted form draft under `../harness/SPEC.md §6`, while §8’s reusable-place acceptance is independent. An invalidated estimate/acceptance cannot remain actionable.
- **What the console shows after a reload, stated because a builder otherwise has to guess** *(added 2026-08-29 — §1.1 specifies a turn that "shows as pending in the transcript" and nothing said whether that transcript survives a reload)*: the console's cards **re-derive from their stored objects** — parks, a `PendingDecision`, a pending ask, a delivery event are all stored structure, and a dismissal is an act through the seam like any other — so a reload rebuilds the card set by reading the store, holding nothing of its own. **Plain chat history — the utterance-and-narration pairs between the cards — does not persist at v1.** That is the honest floor rather than a feature: no layer stores it, and the app holds no log of record (§7’s sole app-owned seam write reaches display settings only; account documents are not a chat log). The revisit condition, stated as readiness and not as a plan: **if** the harness's firing-event stream lands, the transcript becomes its projection and this sentence is re-cut against it. *(drafted, not founder-ratified)* Gated at C18. Pending questions and accepted proposal cards re-derive from `../harness/SPEC.md §3.13` alongside settled scope answers. Retain the existing T2 resume and ephemeral-riser distinction (§1); recovered cards never revive a discarded candidate or make an expired proposal actionable. Clearly disclose separately committed place/contact effects when abandoning the parent, using their supplied outcomes; a pending/unknown write must resolve before discard can be claimed. Clearing unsent text or conversation display does not undo an applied act or erase history.
- **The stage line has no behavioral home, and this records the absence rather than filling it.** `DESIGN.md` names a stage line in three places — the collapsed mobile console's chrome (§Islands & mobile), the motion inventory's *stage-line text replacement* (§Motion & iconography), and the **polite live region** of the live-region law (§Accessibility baseline, a11y A4) — so the surface is designed while the behavior behind it is not: **which stages exist, and when the text changes, is unruled**, and it is the open progressive-state question `NOTES.md` holds. No stage set is defined here, and a build may not infer one from the three visual mentions. **The fence, so Step 1's build does not quietly become the home** *(2026-08-31, m-02)*: Step 1 may build the stage line **only as the accessibility live region a11y A4 names** — an announcement surface with no stage vocabulary, no state machine, and no behavior attached; anything more waits on the sitting. *(drafted, not founder-ratified — pointer only; sits with the progressive-state/streaming sitting)*
- **A standing-policy proposal shows the difference.** When the agent offers to record a repeatedly hand-set value as standing policy (`../harness/SPEC.md §6`), the card's rule lines carry **both readings — what is stored now, and what the proposal would store** — so the owner is choosing between two visible states rather than approving a description. Confirm takes the new one; dismiss keeps the old one; a per-card "don't ask me again" declines the pattern permanently (`../harness/SPEC.md §3.10`). This uses the existing proposal-card anatomy (`DESIGN.md`), not a new surface.
- **Turning self-improvement on is itself an approve-card.** The owner is told the mechanism exists and chooses it explicitly; it is never acquired silently and never defaults on. This is the general rule in `DESIGN.md` — any autonomy change arrives as its own approve-card — applied to this case, and it is why the noticed-pattern offer does not need FR-B reopened: an owner who never approved it never sees it.
- **Outward acts surface as cards, never as silent sends.** A floor-gated outward message (`send`) appears in the console as a card stating the recipient **and the exact address the send will use** (§6) + content + basis; token confirmations returning from moved parties surface the same way. The console is where the floor is *visible*.
- The console carries utterances to the harness and renders what comes back. It holds no policy: no local shortcuts, no client-side command parsing that bypasses the seam.

## §4. Generative UI and the fixed catalog

```
render_generative(schema) -> view
```

- The harness/model emits a **typed schema** — a tree of nodes from the **fixed component catalog**. The app maps node type → vetted component. **The LLM composes; it never invents a widget.**
- **Unknown node type = rejected render, and the rejection is whole-schema** *(aligned with §1.1 and U2, 2026-08-21 — this sentence previously said "renders nothing for that node," which U2 and the fail-loudly-at-parse law both contradict; a partial view of a half-valid schema is a guess wearing a render)*: the app returns a structured error to the harness and renders **nothing for that schema** — never a partial view, never a fallback widget. This is the poka-yoke that makes the catalog *fixed* rather than advisory.
- The schema is **data**: declarative, storable, re-renderable with zero model calls, bounded by a meta-schema. It can rename, arrange, and skin; it can never express executable code, add lifecycle states, or alter engine semantics.
- The **catalog manifest** is a named list — the vetted subset of the design system's components (substrate: `INTERFACES.md §2`). Adding a component to the catalog is a **design act** (a manifest edit, reviewed), never a runtime act.
- The guest form (§5) is the same machinery: the exported form is a stored generative schema rendered on a public page.
- **The live authoring preview is this same render path over the *draft* schema.** While the owner edits Lego-style (`../harness/SPEC.md §5`), the preview renders the in-progress schema through the same `render_generative` — **internal, zero model calls, off-catalog fails loudly at parse**, exactly as a finalized schema does (there is no second preview renderer and no model in the preview loop — U3's "the render path has no model client to call" is universal). Freeze-at-finalization (§5/G6) stops further *editing*; it does not change how the schema renders.

### §4.1 The catalog manifest, v1 *(founder-approved 2026-08-06)* — **the manifest carries a version** *(2026-08-22: a marketplace bundle records the manifest version it was authored against, and the install door compares — `../marketplace/SPEC.md §3`; without it, a bundle published under manifest v2 meets a v1 client with no way to know)*

The closed node-type list. Each maps to exactly one vetted substrate component (binding recorded in the manifest file at build time, `BUILD.md` Step 4). Grouped by role:

**Composition** (the only nodes that may carry `children`):
`group` (labeled vertical section) · `row` (horizontal pairing of small inputs) · `text` (static display copy — never input) · `divider`

**Field nodes** (each captures one typed value; where the value is engine-owned it must validate against the engine's `typed_value` table, `../engine/SPEC.md §2`):
`text-input` · `number` (with unit) · `date` (**serializes as the day's interval against the board zone** — the engine's `instant`/`interval` vocabulary, FD-27, `../engine/SPEC.md §2`; a bare calendar day is not a member of that table, the interval it denotes is) · `time` (**composes with its sibling date bind before entering `typed_value`** — a clock time alone denotes no instant, so the pair is what crosses the seam; a `time` node with no sibling date bind is an authoring-time error) *(both clauses added 2026-08-29 — two field nodes bound engine-owned values with no stated serialization)* · `datetime` · `duration` · `recurrence` (bounded by the engine's pattern menu, `../engine/SPEC.md §1.4`) · `buffer` (before/after duration pair) · `select` (closed options, single) · `multi-select` · `toggle` · `threshold` (comparator + typed operand) · `ranked-list` (orderable options) · `scope-selector` (rule target: board / commitment-kind / audience / party) · `party-picker` (a known principal, by reference) · `place` (declared address per the engine's `place` attribute) · `signature` (guest consent capture in-form)

**Historical/deferred node:** `file-slot` and its former direct `FileInput` binding are outside the active v1 manifest under `../security/SPEC.md §4`. A schema naming that node is rejected whole, including saved/catalog schemas; no fallback attachment control or direct file submission restores it. The original upload stories remain preserved as deferred requirements. Structured fields and the `signature` node remain active.

**Action:** `submit` — the single confirm act of a schema (the archive's "executor"); at most one per schema; its firing is the seam event, never a client-side behavior.

**The meta-schema** bounds every node to: `type` (from the list above — unknown = rejected render, per this section) · `id` (stable slot name) · `label` — **a per-language map keyed by FR15's stored ranked set** *(2026-08-22; one string was the shape, and the corpus required multi-language guest copy with no field to hold it — the owner approves each language through the ordinary authoring seam, with optional ordinary translation drafts under §5; the ranked order is the fallback chain, English behind it; stored rendering makes no model call and adds no verb)* · `bind` (the harness field-path the captured value lands in — data, checked at authoring time) · `props` (per-type, closed per the manifest — unknown prop = rejected render, same poka-yoke as unknown type) · `children` (composition nodes only; field nodes are leaves) · `required?` · `default?`. No node may carry code, event handlers, lifecycle, or free-form styling — appearance comes from the active skin's tokens (`DESIGN.md §Appearance`), never per-node. **The `props` half of the poka-yoke is decidable only if the manifest carries per-type prop lists, so it must** *(2026-08-21 — "closed per the manifest" pointed at a manifest `BUILD.md` Step 4 specified as component bindings only, making unknown-prop rejection an undecidable MUST with no defining artifact)*: the manifest file names, per node type, its **closed prop list** alongside its component binding; a prop outside the list rejects the schema whole (the rule above). Growing a prop list is the same reviewed manifest edit as growing the type list. Gated at U5.

For field/rule rows, `id` remains the stable node slot and `bind` resolves the stable field identity and bound revision under `../harness/SPEC.md §3.3`; it is never a display label or a row index. Authoring validation compares `required?`, `default?`, node type and binding with that schema and its scoped defaults. ## §8. Onboarding & starter content

Before pre-account profile, language/timezone/appearance settings, optional Home or address/map queries are collected or transferred, apply `../security/SPEC.md §12`'s timely versioned notice/choice contract and §4's intake policy. The review summary preserves the actual selections and receipt versions; verified account binding attaches them once to the correct account without reinterpreting choices or restarting retention. Skipping Home infers no location; abandoning intake takes its recorded finite deadline. Account/settings transaction proof remains at the existing BUILD gates; this privacy contract adds no alternative intake API.

- A new owner lands on an **empty board** — and the *app* offers a **neutral way in** *(FD-102, `../RULINGS.md` — the native domain starters are removed; authoring affordances are neutral)*: a plain begin-authoring affordance and a **saved-locations setup** (which survives the removal — it is a fact store, not domain content), as tappable affordances on the canvas. **Onboarding is app-surface-initiated in v1** — tappable affordances, not an agent walkthrough. This is now a *build posture* under §3's ungoverned state, not a prohibition: an agent-led onboarding is a design change with its own scenarios, not an omission to be filled in.
- A starter tap compiles to **ordinary writes through the normal seam** — the same commitment/rule shapes the console would produce, pre-filled and confirmed by the owner. Starters are data (stored templates), never code, and never a bypass.
- Starters are **not** marketplace templates: the marketplace's business-in-a-box bundles (`../marketplace/SPEC.md §1.2`) are a richer, deliberately separate system — same spirit, different artifact, never merged.
- **Provenance (FR38), annotated under FD-102.** No named starter ships — the former set is deleted as domain content the platform must not author. FR38's production-default clause **survives for any future starter**: one ships only after the founder builds it through the app, proving any user could; **Claude/developers never author a starter**, and any starter that exists during development is **declared throwaway test scaffold**, rebuilt through the app before ship. Same authorship law the marketplace states for bundles (`../marketplace/SPEC.md §2`) — starters and bundles stay separate artifacts (above), but neither is developer-written.
- **Saved locations — identity and lifecycle.** These are owner-scoped ordinary facts in the existing principal attributes (`../harness/SPEC.md §3.1`, stored under `../engine/SPEC.md §1.1`), not a new core object or service. Each entry has a store-assigned stable entry id within that owner, revision, nickname, typed `place` (declared address plus optional resolved pin), reusable/retired state and ordinary who/basis/when provenance. References retain owner/entry id and the selected revision/place value, never nickname as identity. A nickname edit keeps the id and address; an address/pin edit creates a new revision. Two entries with the same nickname require selection by address; an unknown name is raw place input for validation/clarification, never a silently resolved address. Owner-authorized reads use the existing relevant-slice stored-answer carrier; writes are ordinary principal-attribute diffs through the harness/engine seam, not `display_settings` or a local app store.
- **Home is optional.** Accepting it in setup stores a reusable place and its optional Home selection. Skipping it stores no inferred location; a route needing an origin asks for one. Home may prefill an editable From field, never assert actual whereabouts. A dated origin selection neither renames nor replaces Home. Choosing Home as a commitment destination reads that selected saved address without importing another kind’s buffers; buffer applicability remains with the selected commitment’s own rules (`../engine/SPEC.md §3`).
- **Picker Save and reusable Save are different acts.** Search/address entry or pan/zoom/pin selection yields a checked address and optional nickname in the current draft. Picker Save changes that draft only. A separate, content-bound accepted saved-location proposal persists it for reuse, with its own write id and readback. Refusing that proposal preserves valid one-occurrence use and creates no reusable entry. Cancelling the later run edit discards its pending domain changes but reports an already accepted saved-place write accurately; it does not undo or conceal it. “Permanent” means reusable under the privacy/erasure law (`../security/SPEC.md §4`), not unerasable forever.
- **Referenced-place changes are checked.** Before editing an address/pin or retiring a reusable entry, read back the concrete affected future Pattern/Commitment references (including dated exceptions beyond the loaded horizon) and separately the retained historical use. Ask for the user’s resolution of each affected future scope; do not silently move a venue/origin, clear a required field, merge entries or retarget history. A nickname-only edit discloses its label effect while retaining the underlying address. Apply only the reviewed diff and resolutions under current authority, reference revisions and ordinary engine checks; a stale, unauthorized or unresolved dependency refuses the applying change with no partial retarget. Retiring removes the reusable choice and optional Home selection, preserves explained historical facts and resolves future references explicitly. Erasing personal address data is a separate scope under security §4/§8, not bookmark removal. No new delete verb follows.

## §9. Non-goals (v1)

- **Calendar data flows in, never out** — founder ruling 2026-08-06. **Inbound is in scope**: the owner connects an outside calendar once, then brings updates in by asking. **annnä never pulls on its own** — no background poll, no watch, no timer. **The import law (FD-103, 2026-08-31):** the **source stays unchanged** — the provider's calendar is read, never written; each imported item's interpretation is **separately editable and reversible**, stored apart from the source fact; a pull spans **one month back, with boundary-crossing items taken whole**, and forward it is **logical-future and finite-adaptive** (as far as the asking turn's purpose needs, never unbounded); **unloaded time is unknown, never free**; provider **labels are evidence, not commands**; and a **defensible interpretation applies automatically** — §3's posture, with the review pile as the routing. **The FR12 reconciliation, printed:** the bounded fetch is caller-initiated inside the asking turn (FD-49), so none of this law re-opens the no-background-poll ruling — the bounds say how much one attended pull reads, never when a pull happens. The stored connection credential is a **held credential, not a token class** — filing *this* credential in the token table at `../security/SPEC.md §3` is the specific error §3.1 forbids: annnä *mints* tokens to let others in, but *holds* this one to get into somewhere else, which reverses both the direction of trust and the blast radius. **That is a bar on this credential, never a bar on the table growing** — the table is extended by ruling and was, at FD-17, to admit a genuinely minted fifth class; §3.1's own list is the one that stays closed, at three members since FD-37. It is vault-resident, **read scopes only**, revocable both ways, expiry shown and never retried (`../security/SPEC.md §3.1`). **Providers and connect UX — founder-ruled 2026-08-07 (wayfinder #10/#11), amended by FD-37 (2026-08-21):** v1 imports from **Google Calendar** (`calendar.readonly` — a Google-"sensitive" scope carrying an app-verification step at build time), **Microsoft Graph** (`Calendars.Read`), and **ICS/secret-link feeds** (the secret URL is a §3.1 held credential, member 3 — read-only by format). **The iCloud/CalDAV route is dropped from v1** *(FD-37: an app-specific password over CalDAV is read-write by construction — no scope mechanism exists to narrow it — which turns FR12's outbound ban from an impossibility into a promise, the exact degradation §3.1's read-only-by-construction rule forbids; iCloud owners import via ICS export until a scoped route exists)*. The connect flow is **the console conversation** — the agent asks where the schedule lives and walks the connection; no settings wizard — and its copy stays at the industry-standard minimum, no disclosure beyond what the connection needs. **The mechanism, named (FD-49, 2026-08-22 — this capability was fully specified here with no seam, no scenario, and no build step in any layer):** the sync ask rides the harness's `import_fetch` tool (`../harness/SPEC.md §5.2`; seam row `../harness/INTERFACES.md §3.3`) — reachable only within a caller-initiated firing, which is what makes *never a background poll* structural rather than promised — and fetched items enter the loop `import`-tagged (`../security/SPEC.md §5`, Q3) as ordinary propose→confirm writes. Build home: `BUILD.md` Step 6a; gated at I1–I4. **Outbound is banned**: no two-way sync, no write-back to an external calendar (`NOTES.md` OR-39, closed — carries Decision 2's split-truth argument; the legal export minimum stays `../security/SPEC.md §8` takeout). Outside commitments that are *not* brought in are still handled by **standing rules**, never a background sync.
- No native app shells (deferred, not rejected); no offline-first operation.
- **No voice input (FD-48)** — the console is text at v1; voice arrives as the same console behind the same seam with its own ruling, scenarios, and transcription seam member (§3), never as a bolt-on.
- No automated SMS / Messenger sending, and none over WeChat or Instagram **while their recorded hard points stand** (v1 = email + **LINE** + **WhatsApp** + the Generate-Link copy button — LINE carved out of this row 2026-08-22 and WhatsApp 2026-08-23, the FD-48 pattern: FD-61/FD-74 rule them into the first release and §6 is the home holding the hard-point records; the stories' depicted auto-sends over the *other* channels are the post-v1 product — `../user-stories/README.md` scope note).
- No in-app payment **rails** — money between users is tracked in the engine, never moved. Store transactions are a different lane, handled entirely by the closed marketplace service and **not specified in this repo** (`../marketplace/SPEC.md §5`); no transaction code, keys, or math enter here.
- No marketplace storefront logic beyond rendering `../marketplace/` catalog payloads (store surfaces are §7-class app-only views).
