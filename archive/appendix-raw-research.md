## Stream 1 — Commitment: philosophy & human relationships

**(1) Synthesized abstract definition.** A commitment is a normative operator that a committer applies to a future-directed content, thereby placing themselves under a standard of answerability to one or more parties (a counterparty, a collective, an institution, or in limit cases no determinate other). It is not the content itself (an event or a task) but the binding relation layered on top of that content: an agent, through some triggering act or condition, becomes liable to be held to a specified performance, over a specified time horizon, discharged by specified means, and exposed to specified normative consequences if the performance fails.

Formal notation: **C = ⟨A, B, X, T, K, N, R, D, M⟩** — Committer, Beneficiary/counterparty, Content, Temporal profile, Triggering/sustaining conditions, Normative force/breach consequences, Revocation/release rules, Discharge condition, Modality (personal/interpersonal/institutional/joint).

**(2) Universal structural components.**
- Committer (agent) — singular, plural, or a "we" constituted by the commitment (Gilbert's plural subject).
- Counterparty/beneficiary — determinate person → group/institution → God or no determinate party (the LEAST universal field).
- Content — future act, omission, policy, project, or relationship-quality.
- Temporal binding — instantaneous, deadline-bound, episodic, continuous, lifelong, renewable.
- Triggering/preconditions — felicitous speech act (Austin/Searle), induced expectation (Scanlon), joint readiness (Gilbert), free choice (Sartre), institutional/sacred procedure.
- Stakes/consequences of breach — moral blame, disappointed assurance, legal liability, perjury, religious sanction, relational injury, inauthenticity.
- Unilateral vs mutual/joint (social arity).
- Revocability/exit logic — promisee consent, joint concurrence, institutional authority, or no clean exit.
- Discharge/fulfillment — performance, excuse, release, dissolution, dispensation, expiry, death.
- Modality of agency — personal/interpersonal/institutional/joint.

**(3) Variation across senses.**
- *Austin/Searle*: conditionality built into felicity conditions; formally unilateral but needs uptake; breach = "abuse" (validly performed, not honored).
- *Scanlon (Fidelity)*: obligation holds "absent special justification," lifted by consent; the wrong is in *inducing* an expectation, not merely non-performing.
- *Gilbert (joint commitment)*: maximal, definitional mutuality; bipolar obligations constituting a plural subject; resists unilateral defeat; breach injures the collective "we."
- *Sartre (existential engagement)*: conditionality dissolves — generated by freedom; often no determinate counterparty; open-ended, identity-constituting; "breach" = bad faith/inauthenticity.
- *Vows/oaths/pledges*: counterparty shifts to institution/God; temporal binding to extremes; breach escalates to perjury/sacrilege/dishonor.
- *Betrayal/fidelity/love*: precondition is an existing relationship + vulnerability, not a discrete act; asymmetrically vulnerable; ongoing; per Baier, betrayal wounds the *relation*, not merely an act — "every betrayal is a breach of trust, but not every breach of trust is a betrayal"; no clean release — ending the relationship doesn't erase the betrayal.

**(4) Is event/task adequate? No.** It only characterizes the content field (X). It ignores directedness, conditionality, temporal extension, normative intensity/modality, social arity, and revocability/exit. Event and task are two values within the temporal/content field of a richer nine-field structure.

## Stream 2 — Commitment: contract law & formal obligation

**(1) Anatomy as a field list.** commitment_id/source · obligor (bearer) · obligee · role_context (office separate from the natural person) · modality (obligation/permission/prohibition/right/power) · act_or_object · trigger/condition · timing (start, deadline, recurrence, window; "time of the essence") · performer_specification (named/role/qualification/delegate) · assignment (who fills a role for an interval) · delegation (subcontract/substitute + who stays liable) · cardinality/liability_mode (single/joint/several/joint-and-several/alternative) · quorum_or_approval · performance_standard (SLA target, license, acceptance test) · evidence · breach/violation · consequence (cure, damages, penalty/credit, termination, escalation, replacement) · discharge · priority_and_exceptions (force majeure, waiver, precedence).

Closest formal vocabulary: **OASIS LegalRuleML** (Actor, Role, Figure = actor filling a role, Bearer, temporal qualifiers, conditions, violations, reparations).

**(2) Conditions/triggers — three independent axes:**
- *Condition precedent / suspensive* — must occur before the duty exists (nothing owed until it fires).
- *Condition subsequent / resolutory* — extinguishes an existing duty (built-in exit).
- *Discharge events* — five ways an obligation ends: performance, mutual agreement, breach, frustration/impossibility, operation of law.
- *"Time is of the essence"* elevates a deadline into a strict condition (slight delay = material breach).
- Caveat: a suspensive condition depending solely on the debtor's will is void.
- Tag each trigger by which axis it belongs to (activates/terminates/discharges).

**(3) Who performs — four patterns:**
- (a) Named party ("key/essential personnel") — substitution forbidden/consented.
- (b) Role/office ("the Landlord," "the on-call engineer") — binds whoever occupies it (LegalRuleML Actor `filledBy` Role).
- (c) Qualification/credential class ("a duly licensed PE," "qualified personnel") — a predicate; performer changes silently while it holds.
- (d) Expressly delegable — transfers performance but not liability (unless novation); personal-service duties are non-delegable.
- Separate `delegated_authority` (decide/give notice) from `delegated_performance` (do the act).
- On-call/coverage = unfilled-but-real duty: attaches to obligor/role, not the current individual; unfilled shift = staffing defect, not lapse. Fields: role, service_window, required_performer (qualification), assignment (or null), status (filled/unfilled/replacement-pending), provider_liability continues. SLA escalation ladders = ranked fallback chains of executors.

**(4) Mutual/joint/quorum — three separate axes:**
- *Quorum* — decision-validity threshold (can the body act at all).
- *Approval threshold* — N-of-M/majority/supermajority/unanimous once quorum is met ("2 of 3" is ambiguous across five structures).
- *Joint / several / joint-and-several liability* — who the obligee can pursue for the full obligation.
- Keep `quorum_requirement`, `approval_threshold`, `obligor_cardinality`, `liability_mode` as four distinct fields.

## Stream 3 — Calendar EVENT data model

**(1) Required to create an event:**
- *RFC 5545 VEVENT* (§3.6.1): `UID` (once), `DTSTAMP` (once), `DTSTART` (required unless VCALENDAR sets METHOD). Nothing else mandatory; `DTEND`/`DURATION` optional & mutually exclusive; `SUMMARY` optional. VALARM: `ACTION`+`TRIGGER` required; DISPLAY needs `DESCRIPTION`; EMAIL needs `DESCRIPTION`+`SUMMARY`+≥1 `ATTENDEE`.
- *Google Calendar API* (`events.insert`): only `start` and `end` (each `date` or `dateTime`+`timeZone`). `summary`/`id`/`iCalUID` optional; id auto.
- *Microsoft Graph* (`POST /events`): `subject`, `start`, `end` in every canonical example; id/timestamps read-only.
- **Disagreement:** RFC's hard requirements (UID, DTSTAMP) are exactly what APIs forbid clients to set (server-generated); DTEND/DURATION is spec-optional but API-required (no duration-only create path); SUMMARY spec-optional but de-facto required in Outlook.

**(2) Common optional fields (ranked):** SUMMARY/subject → LOCATION → DESCRIPTION/body → ATTENDEE[] → ORGANIZER → RRULE/RDATE/EXDATE (Google `recurrence[]`; MS `PatternedRecurrence`) → VALARM/reminders (Google multiple; MS single — a gap) → STATUS → TRANSP/`transparency`/`showAs` → CATEGORIES → CLASS/visibility/sensitivity → GEO/PRIORITY/URL (rare) → ATTACH.

**(3) What defines an event:** a fixed anchor (mandatory start); occupies extent (end/duration); therefore conflicts/overlaps → needs free/busy (`TRANSP`) — a property no other object type needs (separates VEVENT from VTODO/VJOURNAL); recurs via a rule (one record → many occurrences, each with RECURRENCE-ID); stable identity (UID) + revision counter (SEQUENCE). Everything else is descriptive metadata.

**(4) Recurrence/attendees/reminders/transparency:** RFC RRULE+RDATE+EXDATE, modified occurrence = separate VEVENT sharing UID + RECURRENCE-ID; Google collapses to `recurrence[]` strings + `recurringEventId`/`originalStartTime`; MS uses structured `PatternedRecurrence` + `seriesMasterId`/exception collections. ATTENDEE (ROLE/PARTSTAT/RSVP/DELEGATED-TO/FROM) vs separate ORGANIZER. VALARM (multiple, ACTION/TRIGGER) vs Google `reminders.overrides[]` vs MS single `reminderMinutesBeforeStart`. TRANSP binary OPAQUE/TRANSPARENT vs Google 1:1 vs MS `showAs` superset.

**Sources:** datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/rfc5545 §3.6.1; icalendar.org/iCalendar-RFC-5545/3-6-1-event-component.html; developers.google.com/workspace/calendar/api/v3/reference/events(.insert); learn.microsoft.com/en-us/graph/api/resources/event, /calendar-post-events.

## Stream 4 — Task / to-do data model

**(1) Required to create a task:**
- *RFC 5545 VTODO* (§3.6.2): `UID`, `DTSTAMP` only. `SUMMARY`, `DTSTART`, `DUE`/`DURATION` all optional — an undated, untitled to-do is legal ("associated with each successive calendar date until it is completed").
- *Apps* (Todoist `content`, Things, TickTick `title`, OmniFocus, MS To Do, Google Tasks): **title only.**
- Universal answer: a task needs only a name.

**(2) Common optional fields (ranked):** due date (`DUE`) → start/defer date (`DTSTART`; OmniFocus deferDate, Things "When") → priority (RFC 0–9; OmniFocus has none, uses Flagged; Google Tasks none) → project/list → tags/labels/contexts (RFC `CATEGORIES`; GTD contexts) → subtasks/checklist → notes/`DESCRIPTION` → reminders/`VALARM` → recurrence/`RRULE` → assignee (shared context; not in RFC) → status/completion (`STATUS`: needs-action/in-process/completed/cancelled; `COMPLETED`; `PERCENT-COMPLETE`; most apps flatten to a boolean).

**(3) What defines a task vs event:** deadline-oriented (`DUE`, not `DTEND` — mutually exclusive with DURATION); completable (STATUS/COMPLETED/PERCENT-COMPLETE — none meaningful for events); may be undated/floating (a VEVENT with no DTSTART is meaningless); deferrable (DTSTART = "don't act before"); decomposable into subtasks; delegable/assignable (owner can change; distinct from event ATTENDEE/PARTSTAT).

**(4) Decomposition, delegation, the blur:**
- Decomposition tiers: full hierarchical objects (Todoist ≤4 levels, TickTick ≤5, OmniFocus action groups, Google Tasks 1-level) · lightweight checklists (Things, MS To Do "Steps") · contextual only (Google Tasks in Chat/Docs).
- Delegation: no clean split between "assigned to" (ownership) and "waiting for" (blocked-on-another); GTD's answer = a **Waiting For** list + dated follow-up. RFC supports ATTENDEE/ORGANIZER + PERCENT-COMPLETE replies on VTODO; rarely implemented.
- GTD structure: Project (>1 action) · Next Action (single physical step, on context lists) · Context (@errands/@calls — ancestor of tags) · Waiting For (delegation) · Someday/Maybe (undated, tracked) · Deferred/start date (tickler = DTSTART).
- **The blur:** once a task has fixed start + duration/end it behaves exactly like an event (occupies a slot, conflicts) — then `STATUS`/`PERCENT-COMPLETE` are the only distinguishing fields. Todoist/TickTick time-blocking, Google Tasks/Apple Reminders in Calendar. Things 3 deliberately keeps "When" vs "Deadline" separate to resist collapse. Principle: schema difference evaporates exactly when DTSTART+DURATION pins down when the work happens.
