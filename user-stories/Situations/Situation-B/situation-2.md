# Situation 2 — Current reality

*Third-person, what-happens only. The **same busy afternoon as Situation 1** — four travellers wanting bikes off one small fleet — run the way an independent rental shop runs today. There is no app: a paper ledger, a WhatsApp thread, keys on a hook, deposits in an envelope, and the buffer living in Ploy's head. When two people want the same unit there's nothing that blocks it and no shared view to reason over. Ploy does all of it herself, and the concurrent load makes it far worse than any single rental would. Who each player is lives in the setup stories (`resource-bike.md`, `operator-shop.md`) and the README cast. This spells everything out as if it were the only world there is.*

## The afternoon's four bikes

- **Tom** (walk-in) — wants a **Click 125i**, one week (abundant — no contention).
- **Marco** (WhatsApp) — wants an **NMAX**, three days.
- **Priya** (walk-in) — wants an **NMAX**, four days.
- **Anya** (WhatsApp) — wants an **NMAX**, two days.

All four land in the same hour and three of them want an **NMAX**, of which Ploy has only **two** — the exact contention the app would have blocked in a glance. (Tom's Click she has fourteen of; that one's never in doubt.)

## Holds by memory

**Marco messages first**, 13:50: *"Hold an NMAX for me, I'll come by after 4."* Ploy taps back *"ok 👍"* and means to keep one aside. There's no clock and nothing marks the bike — it's a promise she's holding in her head while she runs the counter.

**Tom walks in, 14:00.** He wants a Click for the week. Ploy has fourteen of them — no thought required; she photocopies Tom's passport, has him sign the paper T&C on a clipboard, takes a cash deposit into the envelope, and hands him keys. The one thing that goes smoothly all afternoon is the model with no contention.

**Priya walks in, 14:20.** Wants an NMAX. Ploy has two; one she's *mentally* holding for Marco, one is free. She gives Priya the free one — photocopies, signs, deposits, keys. Now the **only NMAX left is the one she's holding in her head for Marco.**

## The double-book surfaces

**Anya messages, 14:31:** *"Any NMAX free for 2 days?"* Ploy checks — one NMAX left — and replies *"yes, come now,"* forgetting in the moment that that last one is the one she promised Marco at 13:50. She now has **one physical NMAX** and **two people promised it** — Marco and Anya. Nothing anywhere blocked the second promise; she made it herself, blind to her own book.

**Marco arrives, 16:10**, for the NMAX she promised at 13:50. There's one NMAX on the hook and Anya is standing there for it too. Ploy has to talk her way out: she offers Marco a **PCX** instead (a bump he didn't ask for), gives Anya the last NMAX, and hopes neither is annoyed enough to leave a review about it. In Situation 1 this same contest resolved itself the instant the two taps landed; here it's a face-to-face she has to smooth over.

## The buffer, kept in her head

**Marco returns his PCX Saturday night**, a day early. Ploy knows — vaguely — that a returned bike shouldn't go straight back out, but the "6 hours" is a rule she keeps in her head, not on the bike. **Sunday morning** she's busy, forgets when exactly it came in, and rents it out again too soon — or, cautious, leaves it sitting idle longer than it needed and loses a rental. Either way she's guessing, because nothing recorded the **actual** return time.

## The deposit and the documents

Every rental this afternoon left a **passport photocopy** in a drawer and a **cash deposit** in an envelope with a name scrawled on it. When Tom's bike comes back with a **scratch**, there's no signed record tying his consent to a damage charge — just Ploy's word against his over how much of the envelope she keeps. The document and the money exist, but nothing links them cleanly to a rental or to Tom's agreement.

## The point

One afternoon — four travellers, three of them wanting the same two NMAX — and Ploy **double-booked a bike she couldn't see**, promised the same last unit to two people, smoothed a face-to-face contest for it, guessed at a buffer she kept in her head, and had no clean link between a passport, a deposit, and a rental when a scratch turned up. Every hold was a promise she had to remember; every unit's status lived in her head and on a hook; and the one thing that went smoothly was the booking with no contention. That's current reality — a shop run on memory, paper, and hope.
