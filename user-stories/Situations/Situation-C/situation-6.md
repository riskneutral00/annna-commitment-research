# Situation 6 — the second seat (TingTing goes home)

*Third-person, what-happens only. **Provenance: scripted** (added 2026-08-22 from the coverage review's question list). One ordinary Tuesday evening at Hug Ocean, after the week situations 1–5 tell — the first run anywhere in the corpus with **two humans on one board**. Its job is double: show what the second seat must do for the desk to work, and **surface, as story beats, the questions the specs answer only for a singular owner.** Where a beat lands on unruled ground it says so — this is a probe, and an honest question outranks an invented mechanism. Who each player is lives in `operator-dive-center-second-seat.md` and the README cast.*

## The handover that isn't one

At six TingTing logs off and goes home. There is no handover ritual — no notes, no briefing text, no "call me if." Everything she did today is already the board's: the two bookings she took, the gear she marked out for repair, Jun Wang's medical block and when it lifted. **Fon signs in to the same truth**, and the evening desk simply continues where the day desk was. The thing being probed: nothing about the board's behaviour may depend on *which* admin is looking at it.

## An evening walk-in, taken by the second seat

At seven a walk-in couple asks for an Open Water course starting Thursday. Fon picks the course and enters their contacts — the same saved template, the same assembly against the same live inventory, the same intake links going out — and the confirmation moment lands on **Fon, at the console, live**. It works; a course sold at 7 p.m. by the evening admin is indistinguishable to every other party from one sold at noon by TingTing.

**The question this beat surfaces.** The floor's law is written singular: a live, content-bound **owner** confirmation at the console. Tonight the person confirming is Fon — an admin TingTing added, not the account's founding admin, not the owner of Hug Ocean the business. The probe's claim is only this: **for a centre to function, Fon's confirmation must satisfy the floor** — and the specs owe a stated answer for *what makes it satisfy* (a seat under the tenant? a per-seat authority mark?), because "owner" as written names a role one person held in every prior story.

## The form links go out on the day admin's grants

The couple's intake links leave through the same permissions TingTing established at setup. Nobody re-consents at 7 p.m.; the sends fire on the **centre's** standing authorization, on a shift its author isn't working. The probe's claim: standing authorizations are **board-scoped** — a grant minted by admin A must authorize the identical act performed under admin B, or every shift change silently breaks delivery. The record still names who acted: the send attributes to Fon's confirmation, under the grant TingTing authored — **two names, correctly, on one act.**

## Gop's narrower seat

Wednesday morning the boat leaves before the desk opens, and **Gop** checks the day's manifest from the pier — his seat shows him the boat's day: who's aboard, which sites, whose gear. He can tick a diver aboard; he cannot sell a course, change a price, or see the week's money. When a no-show leaves a seat empty at 8:15, Gop can record the no-show — a fact of the deck he is standing on — but the *consequence* (what a no-show costs) already lives in the rules TingTing set, and anything that would message the customer waits for the floor like always. **The beat's claim: seats have shapes.** The probe question beneath it: whether seat shapes are a platform primitive or per-seat configuration is unruled — the story needs only that a deck hand isn't a desk admin.

## The refusal that guards the whole thing

Late in the week Fon tries to add a third seat herself — a cousin who could cover Sundays. The story does not say what happens, because the corpus does not: **an admin who can add an admin can, transitively, hand the centre to anyone.** The beat exists to state the fork out loud — is adding a seat an ordinary act any admin performs, an authorization-class act with its own confirmation, or the account owner's alone? — and to insist only on the poka-yoke floor: whatever the answer, it must be **the same answer at every centre**, decided by the platform, not discovered by each front desk.

## The point

Every prior run had one human per board, so "the owner" was one person by accident of casting. Hug Ocean was never really like that. This run establishes the requirements a second seat is *for* — the desk's truth surviving the shift change, an evening sale indistinguishable from a daytime one, board-scoped rules and grants with per-person attribution, a narrower seat for the deck — and surfaces the four questions the corpus owes stated answers to: what satisfies the singular-written floor when admins are plural, what act class *adding a seat* is, whether grants are board-scoped by law, and what a narrower seat's shape is. The probe's discipline holds: the story invents no mechanism; it leaves the questions standing where a build would trip on them.
