# Operator setup — the second seat at Hug Ocean

*The stub `operator-dive-center.md` left "light on purpose" — TingTing bringing others in under Hug Ocean — now told, because a five-star centre with its own boat, pool, gear, and employed staff manifestly has more than one person at a console, and no v1 probe had two humans on one board. **Provenance: scripted** (added 2026-08-22 from the coverage review's question list — this file and `situation-6.md` are probes that *surface* the second-seat questions as story beats; where the mechanism is genuinely unruled, the beat says so instead of inventing one). Third-person, what-happens only.*

## Who

Two more people at Hug Ocean, both invented like everyone here (see the README's fiction notice):

- **Fon** — the second front-desk admin, hired for evenings and weekends, the hours TingTing doesn't work.
- **Gop** — the boat leader of M.V. Hug Ocean; runs the deck, knows the manifest, occasionally ends up behind the desk when both admins are out.

## Bringing them in

TingTing, as Hug Ocean's administrator, adds Fon **under Hug Ocean** — the same act, from her side, as adding an employed instructor: a person who belongs to the centre's account, not a new tenant. Fon gets her own way in (her own credentials, never a shared password) and lands on **the same board** — Hug's one truth, not a copy. Gop gets a narrower seat: he can see the boat's day and the manifests he sails with; the desk work isn't his.

**What this act is, honestly.** Adding a second admin is the weightiest thing an admin can do — Fon can now do everything TingTing can, including adding a third person. The probe question this file exists to raise, not answer: *is bringing in a seat an ordinary console act, or an authorization-class act with its own confirmation shape?* The corpus rules that a credential must never control who may act as the owner, and that the console is the safe place — but it nowhere says what class of act **adding a console seat** is. `situation-6.md` walks the consequences.

## What the second seat is for

The point of Fon is the point of the whole product, applied inward: **the desk's truth must survive the person leaving.** Before annnä, TingTing going home meant the paper book went dark until morning — a walk-in at 6 p.m. got "come back tomorrow," and the phone tree restarted at 8 a.m. from whatever notes she left. With Fon on the same board, the evening walk-in is just another booking: the saved courses, the inventory, the ranked lists, and every rule TingTing tailored are the **centre's**, not TingTing's, and they work identically for whichever admin is signed in. Nothing lives in anybody's head.

## What stays whose

- **Acts are attributed to the person, always.** A booking Fon takes records that Fon took it; a cancellation TingTing confirms records TingTing. One board, one tenant, two named actors — the record never says only "Hug Ocean did it."
- **The rules are the board's.** The medical gate, the buffers, the prices, the ranked lists — authored by TingTing, but they belong to Hug Ocean and bind Fon's bookings identically. Fon changing a price is the same forward-only act with the same downstream surfacing as TingTing changing it.
- **Standing authorizations are the board's too — this is a probe question.** The message-sending permissions TingTing granted during setup were granted *for Hug Ocean*, and the desk expects them to keep working on Fon's shift — a booking confirmed by Fon at 7 p.m. must send its form link exactly as one confirmed by TingTing at noon. Whether the corpus's authorization objects are truly board-scoped rather than person-scoped is exactly what `situation-6.md` tests.
