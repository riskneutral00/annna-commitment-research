# Situation 3 — Specialties (the match works)

*Third-person, what-happens only. Not a full course this time — **specialty** bookings, which don't stand on a fixed day-shape but on a **specialty-rated instructor**. This shows annnä matching a booking down three axes at once — the right rating, then the right language, then who's actually free — and landing it, **while still preferring Hug's own bench and referring out what Hug can't reach**. Everyone who supplies anything is on annnä. Who each player is lives in the setup stories (`resource-*.md`, `operator-*.md`) and the README cast.*

## The assembly — matching down to the fit, own-and-reachable first

annnä doesn't hunt for "an instructor." For each booking it filters the whole instructor bench by the **rating the specialty requires** first, then by **language**, then by **who's free** — but it also honors **who Hug can actually reach**: its own staff, then freelancers, then a referral for a rating that lives only at another center. Each is a different shape of the same match:

- **James → Nitrox (own).** Nitrox is common, and Hug's **own Ploy Srisuk** holds the rating and writes English. The filter leaves several across the market, but Hug's own qualified instructor is free — so it lands on **Ploy**, in house, before any freelancer is considered. Own first, no referral, no call.
- **Camille → Sidemount (reachable freelancer).** The rating filter cuts the whole market to two, **Bear** and **Lena** — and Hug reaches only one of them: **Bear** is a freelancer on the open market, while **Lena** is employed at Scuba Quest and can't be pulled to a Hug booking. Bear also **speaks French**, so the language axis lands cleanly, and he's free. It lands on Bear. *(Had Bear been busy, Camille couldn't fall to Lena directly — she'd be **referred** to Scuba Quest the way Drysuit is below; sidemount has a fallback, but only through the center that employs it.)*
- **Yuki → Drysuit (referred out).** The rating filter cuts the whole market to exactly one person: **Kenji**, the only drysuit-rated instructor — and he's **employed at Hobo-ya**, unreachable directly by Hug. He also writes Japanese, matching Yuki perfectly. So annnä **refers Yuki through the agent to Hobo-ya**, where the match resolves and Kenji, free, takes it. There is no fallback behind him anywhere in the market — this time none is needed.

Every specialty lands on a board, confirmed, no calls — one in house, one on a freelancer, one referred to the center that employs the only person who can teach it. The matching did the narrowing a human would have done by memory and phone — "who's even rated for that? which of them speaks the language? which is free — and can we even reach them, or do we send this diver elsewhere?" — in one pass.

## The point

A specialty isn't matched the way a course is. A course needs *an* instructor who fits the language; a specialty needs one who holds a **specific rating** — and ratings are scarce, and scattered across centers that don't share staff. When the rating is common *and* in-house (Nitrox → Ploy) the match stays inside Hug. When it's rare but a reachable freelancer holds it (Sidemount → Bear) the match works on the open market. When it's held by exactly one person employed at another center (Drysuit → Kenji@Hobo-ya) the match still works — **but only as a referral, and only because that one person is free**. Situation 4 is the same drysuit booking on a day he isn't.
