# Resource setup — the instructor

*How an instructor sets themselves up on annnä, told once through **one** person (Matthew). Every instructor's setup is the same shape over the same admin-made **instructor template**; only the fields differ (rating, languages, specialties, free/busy). The market has many instructors — see the roster in the README — but their stories would read identically, so this is the one. Third-person, what-happens only. **annnä is the app and the agent both.***

## Who (the lens)

**Matthew Lee**, 37, **freelance** PADI instructor out of Rawai. **PADI Instructor #550453.** Reads/writes **English + Traditional Chinese**; **speaks Mandarin** but can't type it — usually the thing that gets him picked. All he sells is his time and his qualification.

## Setting up

**Signing up.** Matthew creates his annnä account through the browser — email, password, app language (English). The app runs in that one language for him.

**Landing.** He lands on an empty board and tells the console he wants to set up as an instructor. annnä already has a generic **instructor template**, made by the admin — the same one every instructor starts from — and the page fills with it, console on top. It offers to pull in what he already has: he connects his calendar, and his blocked days and existing bookings import as real commitments, so he's never staring at nothing.

**What's already there.** The **template** is the generic instructor scaffold. The **agency standards** are separate supporting documents the admin loaded for everyone: PADI's standards say what an Instructor rating may teach (Open Water, Advanced, the specialties) and what it may not. His choices sit on top of the template and are **bounded by those standards** — he can't grant himself a course his rating doesn't cover.

**Declaring his qualification.** He enters his rating — PADI Instructor, #550453. annnä checks it against the standards and opens exactly the courses that rating teaches; the rest stay closed.

**Declaring his languages.** He sets his written languages — English and Traditional Chinese — then separately marks **Mandarin as speech-only** (he teaches in it, can't type it). annnä keeps the two axes apart: written for the app's messages, spoken for the teaching. This is the field that later lets a Mandarin diver land on him even though he writes a different script.

**Declaring his specialties.** From the specialty list the standards define, he ticks the ones he's rated for — **Deep, Nitrox** — and leaves the rest off. He isn't rated for Drysuit or Sidemount, so annnä won't offer him a job that needs them.

**How work reaches him.** He sets acceptance to **Review** — a job arrives as a request he says yes or no to, never dropped straight onto his calendar. **An offer is not forever:** it carries a response window authored by the operator sending it (Hug sets one on its ranked lists, the way the rental shop sets an hour on a hold — not a rule of the app). Unanswered past the window, the offer **lapses and cascades** to the next name on the list, and a late yes doesn't revive it — so a booking never sits in limbo because an instructor was underwater all day. *(The same tentative-with-a-clock primitive as Situation B's hold, applied to a person's answer instead of a bike.)*

**His availability and rate.** On top of the imported calendar he marks the stretches he'll take work; his real free time is what shows. He states his day rate and currency; annnä never converts it.

## Freelance vs. employed (the one real variation)

The only setup difference worth telling twice is **who the instructor works for**:

- **Freelance** (Matthew, Bear): a standalone account, reachable by *any* center on the open market. Acceptance is theirs (Review).
- **Employed** (e.g. Kenji at a Japanese center, Lena at a German one, Dmitry at a Russian one, plus Hug Ocean's own staff): the instructor is **exclusive to one dive center** — added *under* that center the same way the center adds its boat or pool, and reachable **only** through that center's bookings, never the open market. The template and standards are identical; what changes is the account it hangs under and who can see it.

That distinction is why the market fragments by language: a center reaches its own staff + freelancers, and a customer whose language its staff can't teach gets **referred out** to a center whose employed instructor can (see `operator-agent.md`).

## Set up

Matthew now exists as a resource: an Instructor rated for Open Water/Advanced + Deep/Nitrox, English/Traditional written and Mandarin spoken, freelance, on Review, with a live calendar and a rate. He took the admin's instructor template, bounded by the PADI standards, and tailored it to himself. From here on he only sees jobs appear on his board — the whole thing already spelled out (course, days, times, pool, boat, diver, gear) — and decides. He never sees the ranking that put him there, or who was ahead of him.

## Divemasters are the same story, narrowed

A **divemaster** sets up over this *same* template — the only difference is the rating entered. Enter a **Divemaster** rating and the agency standards open fewer permissions: no certifying, fun divers and course-assisting only. See `resource-divemaster.md`.
