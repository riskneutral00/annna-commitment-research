# Resource setup — the air (compressor)

*How a fill station sets up on annnä, told once through one person (Prawit). Every station's setup is the same shape over the same admin-made **air template**; only the gas mixes and capacity differ, so station A and station B would read identically. Third-person, what-happens only. **annnä is the app and the agent both.***

## Who (the lens)

**Prawit Suksawat**, who runs **Scuba Market Thailand** — a fill station and nothing else. No center, no boat; he supplies gas.

## Setting up

**Signing up.** Prawit creates his account through the browser, app language Thai.

**Landing.** He lands on an empty board and tells the console he wants to add his fill station. annnä already has a generic **air template**, made by the admin — the shape of a gas supplier — and the page fills with it, console on top.

**Declaring the gas.** He sets the **mixes he offers** — **air** and **nitrox 32** — and his fill capacity. The rule that matters: a station can only be *placed* for a mix it has actually configured. A station that never sets a mix can't be assigned a fill, however many tanks it owns.

**How work reaches him.** He sets acceptance to **Auto** — a request just shows how many tanks a day needs and in which mix, and he fills them. He notes he can deliver.

## Set up

Scuba Market now exists as a resource: air + nitrox 32, on Auto, with delivery. Prawit tailored the admin's air template to his station.

## What differs between stations (only the mixes)

Every station sets up this way; the difference is which mixes are configured. A station with none can't be placed at all — that gap is why one is a first choice and another the fallback. Note: only a **pool day** needs an outside fill in the first place — the open-water days are on boats with on-board compressors, so no external air is placed there. The air roster is in the README.
