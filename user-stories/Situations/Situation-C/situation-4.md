# Situation 4 — Specialty, no match (the honest dead-end)

*Third-person, what-happens only. The same **Drysuit** specialty as Situation 3 — but on dates the one instructor who can teach it is already booked. There is no fallback, so the booking can't be made, and annnä says so plainly instead of promising something it can't supply. This is the case the industry hides with "let me check and get back to you" and then silence; annnä just tells the truth at submit. Who each player is lives in the setup stories (`resource-*.md`, `operator-*.md`) and the README cast.*

## The booking

A diver wants a **Drysuit** specialty on a specific week. He picks it on the intake link and fills the short form.

## The assembly — and the wall

annnä filters the instructor bench by the **rating the specialty requires**. Drysuit cuts the whole market down to exactly one person: **Kenji** — who is employed at **Hobo-ya**, so Hug can only reach him the way Situation 3 did, as a **referral** to his center. annnä routes the request there and checks his availability for the requested dates — and he's **booked** (teaching Yuki's drysuit from Situation 3, or any other job; it doesn't matter which). There is no second drysuit-rated instructor anywhere in the market to fall to, and no other center to refer to. The filter has emptied.

So the booking **cannot assemble**. There is no green to show, no provisional to hold, nothing to promise. When the diver hits **submit**, it comes straight back:

> **No instructor available for these dates.**

That's it. It happens sometimes. The single person who can teach the thing is taken, and no amount of assembling changes that.

## What annnä does *not* do

- It doesn't quietly book him anyway and leave TingTing to sort it out later.
- It doesn't invent a fallback that isn't rated for drysuit.
- It doesn't promise a date it can't staff.

## What annnä *can* offer (still honest)

Because it knows the one rated instructor and his real calendar, it can show the diver the **next dates Kenji is actually free** — a true opening, not a maybe — and let him take one or walk away. It stores his answer, so he's asked once. If he wants *this* week and no other, the answer stays **no**, and that's the correct answer. (And in the extreme — every drysuit-rated instructor in the market booked solid — there simply is no opening to offer, and the dead-end is flat.)

## The point

Scarcity is real, and a booking system either tells the truth about it or lies. A rating held by one person is a single point of failure; when that person is busy, the honest output is **nothing available** — delivered at submit, in one screen, instead of a phone tree that ends in the same no a week later. Situation 1's ceiling is "everything lands." This is the floor of the *inventory itself*: sometimes the market can't do it, and the best thing the app can do is say so immediately.
