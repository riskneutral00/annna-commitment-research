# Situation 5 — The realistic middle (minimal network, and things go wrong anyway)

*Third-person, what-happens only. The **same busy week as Situations 1 and 2** — four parties, three courses, the club trip, the boat clash, the sick instructor — run in the world annnä actually launches into: **only a minimal network is on the app.** Hug Ocean is on annnä, and with it everything added under Hug — its staff (Ploy Srisuk, Wang Fang, Nok), its boat, pool, and gear — plus three freelancers who signed up on their own: **Matthew, Bear, and Aroon**. **Everyone else is off-app**: Kittisak and his Mandarin Queens, the Matchanu, the outside pools and gear shops and fill stations, every other dive center. Situation 1 is the ceiling and Situation 2 the floor; this is the middle — annnä coordinates everything it can reach silently, **prepares** every call it can't make, writes down what the calls decide, and turns the pain of each call into the next invitation. Who each player is lives in the setup stories (`resource-*.md`, `operator-*.md`) and the README cast.*

## The network

On annnä: **Hug Ocean** (TingTing), its own **Wang Fang, Ploy Srisuk, Nok**, its own boat, pool, and gear, and the freelancers **Matthew**, **Bear**, and **Aroon**. Off-app: **everyone else** — the backup boats, the other centers, the rest of the freelancer bench. When a booking needs someone off-app, annnä can't place them; it can only tell TingTing exactly who to call and what to ask, and hold whatever the call decides.

## The week assembles — as far as the network reaches

The four bookings come in and annnä places everything the network covers, silently, the same way Situation 1 does:

- **Jun Wang (Mandarin, O+A)** → Hug's **own Wang Fang**. Internal, no call.
- **James + 2 (English, Open Water)** → **own Ploy Srisuk** + **own Nok**. Internal, no call.
- **Mei-Ling (Mandarin, Advanced)** → own Mandarin is taken, so the offer goes to the open market — and here the market is just the on-app freelancers. The offer lands on **Matthew's** board with a **clock on it**: Hug's setup gives an offer a set window to be answered, so a booking never waits on silence. He taps accept with hours to spare. One freelancer placed, no call.
- **Pool, gear, air** — all Hug's own. Held silently.
- **Lukas (German, Advanced)** — Hug reaches no German instructor on or off its bench, and the center that employs one (**Scuba Quest**) is **off-app**. annnä can't refer him silently; instead it surfaces the referral as a prepared hand-off — the center to call, the course and dates to state — TingTing makes the one call, Scuba Quest takes him, and she records where he went. The referral happened the way it always has, by phone; the difference is that annnä drafted it and the record of it exists.

## The boat clash — seen weeks ahead, solved by two prepared calls

annnä knows its **own** boat completely — the manifest, the 40-strong club trip, the Racha route. So the Day-2 collision that ambushed TingTing at 07:00 in Situation 2 shows up **at assembly time, weeks early**: the house boat will be **full** (the club trip plus Jun Wang leaves no seats for James's five) and **committed** (Racha, while Mei-Ling's finish needs Phi Phi). In Situation 1 two backup boats absorbed this silently. Here the backup boats are **off-app** — so annnä does the next honest thing: it puts two **prepared calls** in front of TingTing, weeks out. One card: *Matchanu (Aloha) — five seats, Racha, Day 2.* One card: *Mandarin Queen (Kittisak) — one diver + instructor, Phi Phi, Day 2.* She makes the two calls in ten minutes, types in what was agreed — seats, meet times, price — and the manifests rebuild around the answers. Mei-Ling rides Kittisak's deck with **Matthew** aboard: an off-app boat under an on-app instructor, and the course record never notices whose boat it is. The calls still happened — but planned, calm, and **written down where everyone on-app can see them**, not scrambled at the pier.

## The failure — Wang Fang drops, and the record does the briefing

Day 2 evening, **Wang Fang gets an ear infection**. She marks herself out on her own board — she's on annnä, so the fact lands instantly, not whenever she'd have reached the desk by phone. *(An employed staff member writing on Hug's board — the beat rides the unruled seat question situation-6 surfaces, registered as OR-42.)* Jun Wang's remaining days need a Mandarin instructor, and annnä re-solves against what it can reach:

- Own bench: Wang Fang out, no second own Mandarin. **Own isn't enough.**
- The offer goes to the top on-app Mandarin freelancer — **Matthew**, free now that Mei-Ling certified. But Matthew is out on a fun-dive day, phone dry in a boat locker. His offer's **clock runs out unanswered**, and the offer **lapses and cascades** — no human chased him, and the booking never sat in limbo.
- Next name: **Bear**. He taps accept inside his window. When Matthew surfaces at five and taps the dead offer, he's told plainly it lapsed and went to another instructor — a **late yes doesn't revive a lapsed offer**, exactly as a late document doesn't revive an expired hold in the rental.

Then the part Situation 2 needed a phone briefing for: **Bear opens the job and the whole course is already in it** — which confined skills Jun Wang has passed, which dives are logged, that he's behind on mask clearing and equalizing. Wang Fang briefs nobody from her sickbed. The shared record **is** the handover. Jun Wang meets a new face on Day 3 and never learns anything else changed.

## The fifth day — one call, prepared

Jun Wang's slipped skills push him to a **fifth day**, finishing at Phi Phi. Bear confirms the extra day on his board (on-app, silent); Hug's own gear simply stays tagged to Jun Wang a day longer (own, silent). The boat seat is the one piece off-app — so annnä puts up one more prepared card: *Kittisak — one diver + instructor, PP, Day 5.* One call, one recorded answer, done. Four calls all week where Situation 2 took close to two dozen — and every one of them was prepared before it was dialed and written down after.

## The money — recorded, never moved

No money moves through annnä anywhere in this week — and every baht of it is **tracked**. Jun Wang pays at the desk the way he always would; TingTing marks the booking **paid**, and the mark is on the record both of them can point to. On the other side, the week writes Hug's ledger as it runs: what Hug owes **Matthew** and **Bear** at their stated day rates sits on their boards and on Hug's, entry by entry; the amounts agreed with **Kittisak** and **Aloha** over the phone go in by TingTing's hand when she records each call. When Hug settles up — cash, transfer, however they do it, outside the app — each entry is marked **settled**. The paying still happens in the world; the *knowing who owes whom what, and whether it's been paid*, finally lives in one place.

## The conversion — the phone calls are the prospect list

At the end of the week the pattern is sitting right there: **every call TingTing made was a person annnä couldn't reach.** Kittisak alone was two of them — the Day-2 Phi Phi seats and the fifth day. So annnä offers TingTing one tap: **send Kittisak an invite.** If he joins, every one of this week's calls to him becomes next week's silent placement — his rotations visible, his seats bookable, his ledger entries writing themselves. The suppliers who cost the most calls are exactly the ones worth converting first, and the app can see who they are because it prepared every call. That is how the network grows: not all at once, but one painful phone number at a time — while the customers convert from the other side (Jun Wang leaves with an account and a logbook already in it).

## The point

Same week, three worlds. In Situation 1 everything landed silently; in Situation 2 everything was close to two dozen phone calls; here — the world the product actually launches into — annnä placed everything its minimal network could reach (own staff, own boat-pool-gear, three freelancers), surfaced the Day-2 boat clash **weeks early** instead of morning-of, survived a mid-course sick call by cascading a **clocked offer** past a silent freelancer to one who answered, briefed the replacement **from the record** instead of by phone, and reduced the off-app world to **four prepared, recorded calls**. Money never moved through the app and was never untracked. And the calls themselves became the map of who to convert next. The middle is not the ceiling — it's the wedge that gets there.
