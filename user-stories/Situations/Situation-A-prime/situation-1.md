# Situation 1 — the install run

*Third-person, what-happens only. One run covering the checklist in the README: suggestion, ghost preview, blanked-parameter install, first booking, uninstall, owner-publish of an authored shape, one extract-refusal.*

## The need, and the suggestion

Sofia has her life on the board — meals, runs, yoga, the cycle from Flow — but her teaching is still in her head. She tells the agent she wants students to book lessons without the message tag. **She does not go looking for a store; the agent brings it to her:** *"there's a template for bookable teaching hours — want to see it against your own week?"* Browsing exists, but it appears here only as the shelf the agent's suggestion links into, never as the opening move.

## The preview writes nothing

The template card shows the proposal-card anatomy she'll later live in — tag chip, title, rule lines in plain language, the blanked parameters listed as "yours to set" — beside a **ghost render of the guest page her own real availability would publish**: her actual free time, re-doored as a booking page, watermarked as preview. Her runs and meals shape the ghost without appearing in it. Nothing has been written; closing the card leaves no trace.

## Install is a conversation, not a form

Installing walks the blanked parameters as **ordinary proposals**: lesson min/max duration, her buffers, her teachable windows, her price — *her* numbers, each confirmed before it lands, nothing written without her yes. The installed shape is exactly what `story-sofia.md` shows her operating; this run is how that setup could have begun.

## The shape goes to work

She publishes; each student gets their own link; a first booking lands in genuinely free time, pre-labelled. The store's part as *installer* is over — from here, Situation A's three runs are the life of the thing.

## Months later, the uninstall

When she uninstalls the template, **her booked lessons stand** — the commitments are hers, not the template's; only the authoring vocabulary leaves. Nothing on her board dissolves because a store artifact went away.

## She publishes a shape

Proud of the *kind of work* the template made possible, she tells the agent she wants other teachers to be able to do this. She describes what the template needs to be able to do; customizes; **saves it for herself**; iterates; and at some point **presses publish**. The listing is public, with detailed information on what it is and what it does. Anybody can install it. This is a saved, blanked bundle — not her live week.

## The refusal

She then asks the agent to *"publish my setup"* meaning the populated board — her real students, her real hours as data. **Refused, plainly:** nothing authored on a live board is extracted into the catalog. The agent names what she *can* do: the shape she already saved and published.

## The point

The store touched her life as installer and as publisher — a suggestion, a ghost, a confirmed install, a clean uninstall, a published shape — and at no point did it write without her yes, show a stranger her life, or pull her populated week out of her board. That is the whole open-half contract of the marketplace, exercised by one person.
