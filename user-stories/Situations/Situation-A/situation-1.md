# Situation 1 — the perfect case

*Third-person, what-happens only. **Sofia's whole life and her work on one board, and it just runs.** She sets everything down once by talking; her teaching hours publish; her three students each book through their own link into time she's genuinely free; the lessons land pre-labelled beside her runs and meals; a race resolves silently and a cancellation reshuffles cleanly. She copies nothing and answers no scheduling messages. Who each player is lives in `story-sofia.md`, `customer.md`, and the README cast. This establishes the **ceiling**.*

## Set down once, by talking

Sofia imports her cycle from **Flow**, then talks her week onto the board: breakfast/lunch/dinner, a **M/W/F 6:00 run** (buffer 10 before / 15 after → she's actually unavailable 5:50–7:00), Tue/Thu evening yoga, Saturday strength. Then her work: a "Spanish / English lesson" template — **min 30 / max 120 min**, buffer 5/5, hours **Tue–Thu 2–6pm** and **Mon/Wed 6–9pm**, online or in-person — with Bobby (WeChat), Millie (Messenger), and Brownie (text) named. She hits Save Template; annnä sends each student their **own** link through the app they already use. Every commitment now sits on one board.

## The bookings land themselves

- **Bobby** opens his WeChat link, reads her rhythm (only free teachable time shows — none of her life), and books **Monday 6:00–6:30, online**. It lands on her board labelled *Bobby · English*.
- **Brownie** books **Thursday 3:00, in-person**.
- **Millie** opens her Messenger link, taps **Thursday 3:00** — but Brownie's in-person slot and her request collide; annnä shows her **"that time was just taken,"** offers what's still open, and she takes **4:00–6:00, online**. Two students aimed at the same afternoon; exactly one got each slot, silently.

Over two days Sofia's week fills in on its own — each lesson pre-labelled with who, which language, online or at her place — and she gets a small note per booking. She copies nothing and answers no messages.

## A cancellation, reshuffled cleanly

Sofia will be away Monday, so she tells the agent **"cancel Bobby's Monday lesson."** annnä knows it's their third session — a **standing** thing — and asks *"cancel it, and invite Bobby to pick another time?"*; it **will not message Bobby without her yes**. She says yes. Bobby gets the cancellation in WeChat **with a rebook link**, the slot clears, and when he picks **Wednesday 6:00–6:30 — this time as a standing weekly slot** — the series simply appears on her board, week after week.

Separately, **Millie moves her own Thursday lesson** from her link. Sofia is **not asked to approve** it — it's Millie's slot to give up. The old slot frees, the new one drops in, and Sofia just gets a note that it changed.

## The month's money — recorded, never moved

Sofia's price sits on the template — **NT$1,000 a month base, NT$100 an hour on top, no more than 10 hours a month per student** — so as the bookings land, each student's thread writes its own ledger without her touching it: the month's base entry, then **NT$50** for Bobby's half-hour, **NT$100** for Brownie's hour, **NT$200** for Millie's two — and every booked hour counts down that student's 10-hour allowance. When Bobby's transfer arrives she tells the agent *"Bobby paid,"* and his entries flip to **settled**; the students pay her exactly the way they always have, outside the app. When she cancelled Bobby's Monday, his NT$50 became a **credit** that simply rode onto the Wednesday rebooking, and the half-hour went back to his allowance. Her board now answers the question her notes file never could: **who owes me what, this month, right now.**

## The point

One week — a whole life and a teaching practice — and Sofia set it down **once, by talking**, then did nothing but get notes. Her runs and meals **blocked** her availability without ever showing themselves; students were offered **only genuinely free time**; a collision resolved to one winner with an honest "just taken"; a standing lesson cancelled **only after she said yes to messaging a real person**; and a student moved her own slot with no approval asked. Everything lived on **one board** instead of four apps that don't talk. That's the ceiling — a life and its work, coordinated on their own.
