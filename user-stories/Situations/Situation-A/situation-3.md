# Situation 3 — the edges, held honestly

*Third-person, what-happens only. The **same board and links**, now stress-tested from the outside — one pointed case at a time. Each shows a seam of Sofia's world doing its job: availability told truthfully, the other party's power over their own time, and annnä's refusal to act *as* Sofia toward a real person without her say-so. Split into what **must work** and what **must be refused / handled**. Who each player is lives in `story-sofia.md`, `customer.md`, and the README cast.*

## Must work

**A life-commitment blocks a booking.** Sofia's **run** is 6:00–6:45 M/W/F with a buffer of 10 before / 15 after, so she's unavailable **5:50–7:00**. A student opening the link on a Wednesday morning is offered nothing before 7:00 — the run **blocks** the slot **without revealing itself**. The student sees only "not free," never "run."

**The other party moves their own slot, no approval asked.** Millie can't make Thursday and **moves her own lesson** from her link. Sofia is **not prompted to approve** anything — it's Millie's time to give up. The old slot frees, the new one drops in, and Sofia gets a note *after* the fact, not a request before it.

**A standing lesson cancels *with* a rebook.** Sofia cancels Bobby's Monday; because it's their third session, annnä treats it as **standing** and pairs the cancellation with a rebook invitation — Bobby is never just dropped.

**A cancellation credits — the money and the hours both come back.** The cancelled Monday was already on Bobby's ledger: NT$50 owed, half an hour against his monthly allowance. When Sofia cancels it, the amount becomes a **credit** on his thread and the half-hour returns to his allowance — and both simply ride onto whatever he rebooks. No refund is processed, because no money ever moved through the app; the *record* is what corrects itself.

**A rule change applies forward, never backward.** Mid-month Sofia tells the agent her hourly rate is now **NT$120**. Every lesson **already booked** stays at NT$100 — the terms it was booked under — and her links show the new price to anyone booking from now on. Same when she shrinks Thursday afternoons: the lessons already sitting in them stand. Changing her rules re-publishes her future; it never rewrites a commitment she already made.

**Cancel one, or cancel the series?** Bobby's Wednesday half-hour is a **standing weekly slot** now. When Sofia says *"cancel Bobby's Wednesday lesson,"* the agent asks which she means — **just this Wednesday, or the standing slot itself** — because an instance and its series are different things to end. One frees a single week; the other ends the arrangement and stops its hours and charges from writing forward.

## Must be refused / handled

**A sub-minimum booking is refused.** A student tries to grab **5 minutes** at 6:00. The end-time picker only offers **6:30 onward** — Sofia's **30-minute minimum** — so the short grab **cannot be made** at all. The floor is enforced at the point of booking, not apologised for afterward.

**A race yields exactly one winner.** Two students tap the **same Thursday 3:00** within moments. Exactly **one** booking is created; the other gets an honest **"that time was just taken"** and the still-open times — never a silent double-book, never two lessons stacked on one slot.

**The eleventh hour can't be booked.** A student who has used their **10 hours this month** opens their link wanting more. The picker simply won't offer a slot that would take them past the cap — enforced at the point of booking, exactly like the 30-minute minimum, with an honest note that their hours reset next month. Sofia never has to be the one who says "that's enough."

**A no-show is Sofia's rule to make — asked once.** Brownie never turns up for his Thursday 3:00. His hour was held, her afternoon was blocked, and NT$100 sits on his ledger. annnä doesn't decide what a no-show costs — that's Sofia's rule, and she's never stated it — so the **first** time it happens, the agent asks her once: *keep the charge and count the hour, or return them both?* Her answer is **stored**, and every later no-show simply follows it without asking. (Whatever she decides, saying anything to Brownie about it is still a message to a real person — gated on her yes.)

**annnä won't message a real person without Sofia's yes.** When Sofia says "cancel Bobby's Monday lesson," annnä **stops and asks** before it sends anything to Bobby — *"cancel it, and invite Bobby to pick another time?"* Messaging a real person is an **across-the-line action**; without Sofia's explicit yes, annnä **does not do it on her behalf**, however obvious the cancellation seems. **The same line holds for money:** when Bobby's transfer is late, annnä can show Sofia the unpaid entry, but a payment reminder is a message to a real person — it goes nowhere until she says yes.

**A student can't see Sofia's life.** However a student probes the link — looking weeks ahead, drilling into blacked-out days — it only ever exposes **free/busy for teaching**. The meals, runs, yoga, and cycle that carve her week are **never** rendered, named, or leaked, even as they do the blocking.

## The point

The seams are honest at every edge: a run blocks a booking without ever showing itself, the student who owns a slot can move it without asking, a standing cancellation always carries a way back — with its money and hours crediting themselves — the minimum duration simply can't be undercut, the monthly allowance can't be overrun, a rate change touches only the future, a series and its instances end separately, a no-show costs exactly what Sofia decided once, a race resolves to one winner out loud, and annnä refuses to speak *as* Sofia to a real person — about a lesson or an unpaid bill — until she says yes. Availability tells the truth while keeping her life private; the other party keeps power over their own time; and the one across-the-line act — messaging a human — stays gated on consent. The board holds a whole life honestly, and it won't overstep on her behalf.
