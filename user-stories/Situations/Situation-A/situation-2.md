# Situation 2 — Current reality

*Third-person, what-happens only. The **same week as Situation 1**, run the way Sofia lived before annnä — across four apps that don't talk to each other: a nutrition app for meals, **Flow** for her cycle, a notes file for her runs and yoga, and a separate calendar for students. Nothing shares a truth, so she hand-copies between them, plays message tag to book a lesson, and double-books herself because one tool never knew what another held. This spells everything out as if it were the only world there is.*

## Four apps, four truths

Sofia's life is spread out: **calories** in a nutrition app, her **cycle** in Flow, **runs and yoga** jotted in a notes file, and **student lessons** in a calendar app. Each is right on its own and blind to the others. Only she holds the whole picture — in her head — and only until she forgets.

## Booking a lesson is message tag

Bobby messages on WeChat: *"can we do a lesson this week?"* There's no link and no shared calendar he can see, so Sofia opens her calendar app, eyeballs it against what she *remembers* of her runs (which live in the notes file, not the calendar), and types back **"how about Monday 6?"** He replies **"can we do 6 to 6:30?"** — fine. She types the lesson into the calendar by hand. Millie, over on Messenger, wants **Thursday afternoon**; Sofia offers 3:00; Brownie (by text) already asked for Thursday 3:00 an hour earlier but Sofia hadn't written it down yet, so she offers Millie the same slot — and only catches the clash when Brownie confirms. She walks Millie back to 4:00 over a few more messages. Every lesson is a small negotiation she drives by hand, in three different chat apps.

## The double-book she couldn't see

Wednesday, Sofia agrees to a make-up lesson at **6:00 pm** — and forgets that her **evening yoga** is Tuesday/Thursday, but her **run** buffer and a dinner sit nearby, and more to the point she'd told a friend she'd swap Wednesday's run to the evening. The run isn't in the calendar (it's in the notes file), so the calendar happily accepts the 6:00 lesson **on top of** the run she moved there. She discovers it the day of, when both are staring at her from two different apps. In Situation 1 the run sat on the same board as the lesson and simply **blocked** the slot; here, the two tools never met.

## The fifth truth — the money

Her price is real — NT$1,000 a month base plus NT$100 an hour, ten hours a month at most — but nothing tracks it except **her**. Who's paid this month's base lives in a notes column; how many hours Millie has used against the cap means scrolling back through a month of Messenger to count bookings; and when Bobby's transfer doesn't show up she has to notice it herself, then write the awkward "hey, just checking…" message herself. She undercounts one student's hours and eats the difference; she double-asks another who already paid, and apologizes. The four apps hold her life and her calendar — the money is a **fifth truth**, and it lives nowhere but her head.

## Copying, forever

Every change means copying: a period-shift prediction in Flow she wants to plan around gets hand-noted into the calendar; a cancelled lesson has to be deleted in the calendar *and* mentioned back to the student in whichever app they use; when Bobby cancels-and-rebooks it's four messages and two manual calendar edits. Nothing is labelled automatically — she writes *"Bobby Eng online"* herself each time — and nothing reconciles, so the one source of truth is her memory.

## The point

One week — and Sofia spent it as her own switchboard: negotiating each lesson by hand across three chat apps, hand-copying between four tools that never share a fact, missing a clash until the day of because a run and a lesson lived in different apps, and re-typing every label and every change herself. Every "reshuffle" that Situation 1 absorbed silently was, here, a string of messages and a manual edit — and the one double-book happened precisely because **no single board held both her life and her work**. That's current reality — a life that only exists, whole, inside her own head.
