# Situation 4 — the fresh start (calendar import and the store install)

*Third-person, what-happens only. **Provenance: scripted** (added 2026-09-15 under FD-106, re-homed from Situation A / A′ — the calendar-import beat of Situation A's getting-started telling and the whole of A′'s install run, retold in Debra's world because they did not fit Matt's practice). The same empty account `story-debra.md` opens on, the week before its bookings, showing how her setup could have begun from what she already keeps and from the store, instead of from scratch. Discovery is **agent-suggested on need**, and this run stays templates-only — no skin beat. Where the prose sketches a bundle, `../../../marketplace/SPEC.md §1.2`'s closed grammar **governs**: the story illustrates, the format decides, and nothing here widens the grammar by a single member. Who each player is lives in `story-debra.md`, `customer.md`, and the README cast.*

## The empty board, and what she already keeps

Debra sets up an account. The first page is empty, and before anything else the agent asks whether there is anything she already keeps somewhere that she'd like brought in, or whether she wants to start from scratch.

Her appointment book is paper, and it stays paper — nothing on paper is brought in. But for the last year she has also put her office days into the calendar on her phone, a Google Calendar she keeps for office sessions — a small invention of this run. She says so; the agent walks the connection in the conversation, no settings wizard, and she connects it once. Her existing office sessions come onto the board as real commitments, at their times and under the names she typed: *Ito 9:30* on Tuesday, *Priya 3:00* on Wednesday, the rest of the fortnight ahead. The phone calendar is only read, never written back to.

Nothing else comes in. Her patient addresses, her cash notes, and her paper book have no import, and the agent does not offer one. What the import buys her is simple: her first look at the board is not a blank page but the weeks she already has.

## The need, and the suggestion

With her life and her places set down *(as `story-debra.md` tells it)*, Debra tells the agent she wants her patients to book themselves in instead of calling. **She does not go looking for a store; the agent brings it to her:** *"there's a template for bookable session hours — want to see it against your own week?"* Browsing exists, but it appears here only as the shelf the suggestion links into, never as the opening move.

## The preview writes nothing

The template card — "Free Time Available" — shows its title, its rule lines in plain language, and the blanked parameters listed as "yours to set," beside a **ghost render of the guest page her own real availability would publish** (`../../../marketplace/SCENARIOS.md` D3): her actual free time, watermarked as a preview. The swim, the book club, and the office sessions she just imported shape the ghost without appearing in it. Nothing has been written; closing the card leaves no trace.

## Install is a conversation, not a form

Installing walks the blanked parameters as **ordinary proposals** (I2): session length **45 minutes**, a **10-minute buffer**, **$90, cash**, and which site the page offers on which day — **Eastside on Monday and Wednesday, Riverside on Tuesday and Friday**. Each is *her* number, each is read back and confirmed before it lands, and nothing is written without her yes. The installed shape is the "Treatment session" `story-debra.md` shows her running; this run is how that setup could have begun.

## The shape goes to work

She publishes. Each regular gets their own link — Mrs. Ito, Harold, Mrs. Gable, Tom, Priya — and the first booking lands a day later: Priya takes a Friday 11:00 at Riverside, in genuinely free time, pre-labelled *Priya · ankle · Riverside* (Z1). The store's part as installer is over; from here, situations 1–3 are the life of the thing.

## Months later, the uninstall

When Debra uninstalls the template, **her booked sessions stand** (I5) — Priya's Friday, Mrs. Ito's Tuesdays, every session already made. The commitments are hers, not the template's; only the authoring vocabulary leaves. Nothing on her board dissolves because a store artifact went away.

## She publishes a shape

At a continuing-education weekend, two other therapists ask how her booking page knows which office she's at. Back home, she tells the agent she wants other therapists to be able to do this. She describes what the shape needs to do — sessions offered at the site the day belongs to — customizes it, **saves it for herself** (P3), iterates on it over a week, and then **presses publish** (P2). The listing is public, with detailed information on what it is and what it does. Anybody can install it. It is a saved, blanked bundle: no addresses, no patients, no prices of hers.

## The refusal

Then she asks the agent to *"publish my setup"* — meaning her populated board: her real patients, her real sites and addresses, her real hours as data. **Refused, plainly** (P4): nothing on a live board is extracted into the catalog. The agent names what she *can* do — the shape she already saved and published.

## The point

A fresh account started from what Debra already had — her office sessions, brought in from the calendar on her phone, and nothing more — so the first board she saw was her own weeks, not an empty page. The store then touched her practice as installer and as publisher: a suggestion on need, a ghost that wrote nothing, an install made of her own confirmed numbers, a clean uninstall that left every booked session standing, a published shape for other therapists — and at no point did it write without her yes, show a stranger her life, or pull her populated week out of her board.
