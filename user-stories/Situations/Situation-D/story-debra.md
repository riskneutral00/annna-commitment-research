# Debra — the protagonist's own telling (immersive)

*The one **first-person** file in this folder, in Debra's own voice, because for a mobile practice the lived experience **is** the requirement — "a schedule that knows where I am." It doubles as the setup story: in a solo world the protagonist is her own resource **and** her own operator. The rest of the folder (`customer.md`, `situation-*.md`) is the standard third-person Situation view. **annnä is the app and the agent both.***

## Bio

My name is Debra. I'm 52, and I'm a physical therapist. Fifteen years in a hospital system was enough; now the practice is mine, and it's **cash only** — no insurance, no billing codes, no waiting ninety days to be paid a third of my rate. That's not a compromise, it's the business: patients pay me when I treat them, and what I sell is my hands and my time.

The catch is that my time drives. I rent a treatment room on the east side two days a week and a studio across the river two other days, and on Thursdays I go to the patients — the ones who can't come to me. So my week isn't just a list of appointments; it's a **route**. A cancellation isn't just a free hour, it's a hole in the middle of a drive I now make for nothing. And everything else — who's paid me, who I should ask for a review, where I'm supposed to be at 10 a.m. — lives in a paper book and my head. What I want is one place that holds all of it and knows the town has distances.

## Getting started

I set up an account. The first page is empty, and the agent asks what I'd like to put down first. I start with my life, because my life is what my patients book around without knowing it: my swim Monday, Wednesday, Friday at 6:30, lunch every day, book club Wednesday evenings, Sundays with my mother. Each one comes up as a little form already half-filled from what I said; I confirm them one by one and they land on my board.

## My places

Then my places, because nothing about my week makes sense without them.

> **Me:** I work out of two locations. Mondays and Wednesdays I'm at the Eastside room, Tuesdays and Fridays I'm at the Riverside studio.

A form for each: name, address, and the days it's mine. I type the real addresses in and they're saved — after this I only ever say "Eastside" or "Riverside." Home it already knows.

> **Me:** Thursdays I do home visits.

It asks how far I'm willing to go. I trace out my answer — the neighborhoods I'll drive to, basically anything this side of the highway — and how home-visit days should work: house calls only, no office that day.

## The sessions

> **Me:** I need my patients to be able to book themselves in.

A form comes up. I title it "Treatment session" — 45 minutes, a 10-minute buffer between patients so I can reset the table and wash my hands, **$90, cash**. Bookable hours 9:00–5:00 on my office days. The part I care about: the booking page follows my week — **on a Monday it offers the Eastside room, on a Tuesday it offers Riverside** — because a patient shouldn't have to know my geography; the link should.

Then the home visit: 60 minutes, **$140, cash**, Thursdays. A home visit isn't picked off a grid — the patient's page asks for their **address** and requests a time, because whether 11:00 works depends on where 9:30 is. I don't want to do that math ever again. That's the point of all this.

At the bottom, **Who is this for?** I enter my regulars by name and contact — Mrs. Ito, Harold, Mrs. Gable, Tom, Priya — and each gets their own link, sent the way I already reach them. If someone new asks, I just share the link myself.

## What I know about my patients

Here's the part no calendar ever held for me. My patients have shapes.

> **Me:** Mrs. Ito can only come Tuesday mornings — her daughter drives her.

> **Me:** Harold never moves. Once his appointment is made, that's it — don't ever ask him.

> **Me:** Priya will take anything, she reschedules herself constantly.

I say these things once, in passing, the way I'd tell a receptionist — and they're **kept**. Mrs. Ito's link only ever shows her Tuesday mornings. And later, when the agent is juggling my day, I find out what "Harold never moves" really bought me: he is simply never in the shuffle. Not "asked politely." Never suggested.

## Thursday — the round

By Wednesday night my Thursday looks like this: **Harold at 9:00** on Maple Street (his standing slot — same time every week), **Mrs. Gable at 11:00** up Oak Hill, **Tom at 2:30** back south. Between them, drives — real blocks on my board, worked out from the addresses. I didn't enter "20 minutes"; it knows where Maple Street is and where Oak Hill is. The one thing I did tell it: the bridge at rush hour is twenty minutes no matter what any map thinks. It uses my number.

## The cancellation — and the question I'd been waiting for

Thursday, 7:40 a.m., I'm finishing coffee. Mrs. Gable's son cancels through her link — she's had a rough night. My board updates: the 11:00 is gone, and my day is now Harold at 9:00 … nothing … Tom at 2:30. A morning appointment, a dead middle, one more drive south in the afternoon. The old me would eat that hole.

The agent asks me one question:

> **annnä:** Your Thursday just opened up in the middle. Want me to pull the day tighter — toward the morning, or toward the evening?

> **Me:** Morning. Give me my afternoon.

> **annnä:** I can move Tom from 2:30 to 10:30 — you'd leave Harold's at 10:00 and the drive is about 25 minutes. Tom has no constraints against it. Want me to ask him?

It's already done the checking — the drive, my hours, who can and can't be moved. Harold isn't mentioned, and I notice that: he's at 9:00, he never moves, and the agent knows better than to even raise it. I say yes — it doesn't touch Tom without my yes, because a promised time is a promise to a real person.

## From Tom's perspective

I'm Tom. My phone buzzes over breakfast: *"Hi Tom — Debra's day has shifted. Could you do 10:30 this morning instead of 2:30?"* Two buttons: **keep my 2:30**, or **take 10:30**. Nothing's been done to me — my 2:30 is still mine until I answer. Honestly, earlier is better; I tap 10:30, and my confirmation updates. That's the whole thing.

## Back to me — the afternoon stays mine

Tom confirms in two minutes. My board redraws: Harold 9:00, drive, Tom 10:30, home by noon. The whole afternoon is mine, and nobody got a phone call — least of all from me.

Then one more question, and it's a good one:

> **annnä:** Your Thursday afternoon is clear now. Keep it blocked for you, or open it for booking?

> **Me:** Mine.

And that's that — the links stop offering Thursday afternoon. Because what would be the point of pulling my day tighter if the empty half just filled up again behind my back? The time I won back is *won*, not re-listed.

## The money

Every visit writes itself to that patient's thread: Harold, $140; Tom, $140; Priya's Wednesday session, $90. When the cash is in my hand I say so — "Harold paid" — and the entry is marked settled. Nothing moves through the app; it just finally keeps the count that used to live in my glovebox. At the end of the week I can see the one thing I could never see before without an hour and a calculator: who's paid, who hasn't, and what the week actually made.

## The rest of the job

I'm not just the therapist; I'm the whole business. So the rest of the job goes on the same board, as mine:

> **Me:** Remind me Friday to ask Mrs. Gable's son for a review when she rebooks. And I need an afternoon next week for flyers at the Riverside noticeboard.

They land as my own tasks and events — nobody books them, annnä doesn't write reviews or do my marketing, and I wouldn't want it to. It just **holds the work**: finding the next patient is a task like buying resistance bands, and it sits on the board next to the swim and the sessions until it's done.

## And that's the point

I set my places, my sessions, and my patients' shapes down once, by talking — and after that the bookings land on the right day at the right site, Thursdays route themselves, and a cancellation comes with a question instead of a hole: *tighter toward morning, or evening?* My schedule finally knows what I've always known — that it happens somewhere — and the first day it proved it, I got an afternoon of my life back.

## NOTES (mechanism asides — not in Debra's mouth, held-out predictions)

- **Location as a first-class commitment attribute**, with the gap between two commitments measured in **travel, not minutes** — implies an external compute source (maps) behind the engine's `calculate`. General primitive, flagged; nothing PT-specific.
- **Per-patient third-party constraints** ("will never move," "Tuesday mornings only") as stored objects the optimizer must honor absolutely — tests whether the fixed rule-type menu covers constraints owned by the *customer*, not the operator.
- **Compaction = reshuffle-as-proposal** — the engine capability deferred to the revisit (`../../../.specs/deep-interview-engine.md` decisions 2/3/6). This story is its justification and its test: propose → owner approves → affected patient confirms; never auto-move.
- **Direction-parameterized optimization** ("toward morning" / "toward evening" / "inside a window") — an optimization *goal* picked per event, not a standing rule.
- **Declared override beats computed value** (the bridge at rush hour) — the general shape: computed defaults, user rules win.
