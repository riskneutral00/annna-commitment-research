# The customer

*Off-app in every version of this story — no account, no calendar, only a link. Told through **Tom** (the easy-going home-visit patient), with Mrs. Ito, Harold, Mrs. Gable, Priya, and Nathan alongside. Third-person, what-happens only. The patient experiences only the outward slice of Debra's board — when and **where** she can treat — and never her life or her route behind it.*

## Who

**Tom**, 44, a shoulder rehab patient Debra sees at his house on Thursdays. Alongside him: **Mrs. Ito** (81, hip — Riverside office, Tuesday mornings only, her daughter drives), **Harold** (67, knee — home visits, never moves an appointment), **Mrs. Gable** (76, post-stroke — home visits, her son works the link), **Priya** (35, ankle — office, books and moves herself constantly), and **Nathan** (58, back — the new patient). None of them has an annnä account.

## What Tom does

Everything through a **tokenized link**, on his phone, no account:
- **He gets his own link.** A text from Debra — *"Hi Tom, book your sessions here."* The link is unique to him, so whatever he books lands on Debra's board already labelled *Tom · shoulder · home visit*.
- **He sees days, not her day.** For a home visit he doesn't pick from a grid of her hours — he confirms his **address** and requests a Thursday time. Whether 10:00 or 2:30 comes back depends on a route he never sees; he only ever gets times that are actually true.
- **He pays cash, and the count keeps itself.** The page shows his terms plainly — **$140 a visit, cash** — and his own thread: visits done, what he's paid, what's owed. The app never asks him for money; Debra marks it settled when he hands it over.
- **A move is a question, not a notice.** Thursday morning his phone buzzes: *"Debra's day has shifted — could you do 10:30 instead of 2:30 today?"* He can say yes or keep his time; his 2:30 stands until **he** confirms. He taps yes, and the new time is his.

## The others, same shape

- **Mrs. Ito's** link shows her **Tuesday mornings at the Riverside studio and nothing else** — not because the page is broken, but because that's the one shape she can use, told to Debra once. Her daughter books it in under a minute.
- **Harold** booked his standing Thursday 9:00 months ago. He has **never once** been asked to move it — no matter what happens to the rest of Debra's day, no message ever comes.
- **Mrs. Gable's son** books and cancels for her from his own phone. When he cancels the 11:00 on a rough morning, it's two taps and done — no apologetic phone call, no voicemail tag; Debra's board just knows.
- **Priya** treats her link like her own calendar — books a Tuesday, moves it to Friday, moves it again. Nobody approves anything; it's her slot to move, and it only ever lands on times that are really free **at that day's site**.
- **Nathan** gets the link from Priya, not from an ad. He opens it, reads what a session is and costs, picks a Wednesday at the Eastside room, and becomes a patient without Debra lifting a finger. (Finding Nathan was never the app's job — Priya was. The link is just where "ask Priya's therapist" turns into a booking.)

**What a patient must never see:** Debra's swim, her book club, her route, her other patients' names or addresses, or *why* a time isn't offered. **What a patient must always get honestly:** only times that are physically real (reachable from wherever she'll be), the price before booking and their own running count, a clear "that time was just taken" when they lose a race, their own power over their own slot — including the power to say **no** to a move — and an honest "outside Debra's area" at submit, not a maybe that dies by phone.
