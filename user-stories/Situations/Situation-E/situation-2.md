# Situation 2 — Current reality

*Third-person, what-happens only. The **same ER as Situation 1** — the same placements, the same bid week, the same sick call — run the way it runs **today**: a full-time human scheduler with a magnetic whiteboard, each room's admission rule carried in their head, an annual bids spreadsheet resolved in a seniority meeting, and a phone tree when someone drops. No autonomy, no shared solve. This spells everything out as if it were the only world there is. (**Not** held out — this is the manual floor annnä is bet to replace.)*

## The whiteboard

The scheduler, **Marta**, holds the floor on a **magnetic whiteboard** — ten room slots, magnets for staff. She knows the admission rules by heart: Trauma rooms for trauma, Peds for children, Isolation for the infectious, the General Bays for stable adults. When cases arrive she places each by memory:
- the feverish **child** onto Peds 1,
- the **road-accident trauma** onto Trauma 1,
- the **infectious** adult onto Isolation,
- three **stable adults** onto General Bays A–C.

It works while the board is simple and she's at the desk — but the rules live only in her head, so a placement is only ever as good as her attention. When two cases arrive at once and the phone rings, a magnet goes on the wrong slot and someone catches it later, or doesn't.

## The bid book

Next year's holiday-week **time-off bids** are a **spreadsheet**. Four staff want the same week; staffing allows two. Resolving it is a **meeting**: Marta and the charge nurse sort the four by seniority, grant the top two, and then personally tell the other two no — a few awkward conversations, and a promise to "remember you for next year" that lives only in the note margin. The bids consumed nothing while they sat; the losers find out they lost when Marta gets around to telling them.

## The sick call — the 3 a.m. phone tree

An **RN calls in sick** at 3 a.m. There is no solve to run — there's Marta, on the phone, in the dark. Her absence cascades: two General Bays and the Peds child lean on that nurse, and a Resident needed her supervision. Marta works the tree:
- she calls the **float pool** — no answer,
- she calls an **off-duty RN** — she'll come in, in an hour,
- she calls an **Attending** to re-cover the Resident's supervision — reached on the third try,
- she re-draws the whiteboard by hand, moving magnets that were already placed, hoping she hasn't left a room uncovered.

The floor is patched, but only because Marta was awake and dialing — and there's no record of the moves except the eraser marks on the board.

## The point

One shift — placements held by memory, a bid contest resolved in a meeting and delivered by awkward phone calls, and a sick-call cascade patched by a person working a 3 a.m. phone tree — and **all of it lived in one scheduler's head and on a whiteboard.** Every admission rule was Marta's to remember, every losing bidder was Marta's to call, every re-cover was Marta's to dial. Nothing was written where two people could reason over it; nothing re-solved itself. That's current reality — the single point of failure annnä is being bet to replace.
