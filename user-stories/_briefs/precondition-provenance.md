# Brief — where a precondition's authority comes from

**Readiness.** Ready when the founder opens a sitting on the gating documents. It spans Situations B and C, so it is one sitting across both.

**The debt this closes.** Two owed beats in `../COVERAGE.md`'s **precondition provenance** cluster — `Situation-B/resource-bike.md`'s *"Sunbird's own uploaded documents"* and `Situation-C/customer.md`'s *"hard block in the app itself"*.

**Why nothing covers it.** `../../harness/SCENARIOS.md D8` asserts a **document-derived basis** for a named late fee — one document, one fee. Nothing asserts the **structural** claim underneath: that some preconditions get their force from the operator's own uploaded terms and others from an outside legal standard, and that **the two gate differently**. Nor does anything assert a document-*type* precondition — an uploaded medical clearance — blocking intake until it is satisfied, which is the document-kind sibling of the engine's conversation-kind precondition park.

## World

Ploy's booking form gates on four things: passport, license, terms, deposit. Two of those are hers — her terms and conditions, her deposit policy, uploaded by her. Two are not — a motorcycle license class is a fact about the outside world and a passport is somebody else's document entirely. In Situation C, a medical clearance note is a third thing again: a document the *customer* supplies, which blocks until it arrives.

**Protagonist:** Ploy, with TingTing as the second case — **to confirm.** **Multi-stakeholder** if taken together; single if the sitting splits them.

## The question that governs the sitting

**When a booking is refused, whose "no" is it?** Ask Ploy directly. If the refusal is hers, she can change it — that is a rule, forward-only, and she owns the consequences. If the refusal comes from a licence class, she cannot change it and would be alarmed to be able to. **The two feel identical to the customer and are completely different objects**, and the corpus has one mechanism for both.

## Rules and their source — to ask

- Which gates are the operator's own choice and which are imposed? Ask her to sort her own list.
- **Versioning.** `../../security/SCENARIOS.md S3` asserts the version of the document a consent was given against. Ask what happens when she uploads new terms with live bookings under the old ones — changing the rules never rewrites the past, and a document is a rule wearing a different coat.
- What makes a document *satisfied*? Uploaded, read, or checked by a person? The medical note especially: does anybody read it, and is that a quarantine question (`../../security/SCENARIOS.md V3`)?

## The floor (today, without the app)

To ask: today, who checks the licence, and when? At the counter, when the traveller is already standing there and has already paid for the taxi. The floor's whole cost here is **lateness** — the refusal arrives after the customer has invested — and the product's claim is that it moves the refusal to the point of booking. Ask whether she believes customers will accept it earlier.

## Situations to cover — the questions, not the answers

**To elicit as must-work.**
- A gate that is hers, changed by her, applying forward only.
- A gate that is not hers, which she cannot change, and what the app says about that.
- A document-type precondition arriving late and unblocking the thing it blocked.

**To elicit as must-be-refused.**
- A booking passing a gate whose document has not arrived. What does the customer see, and can they proceed anyway "just this once"? Ask whether she wants that door — an override she asks for is a finding, not a feature to design in.
- An operator editing an outside-standard gate. Should annnä refuse, or warn?
- The medical note reaching anybody who does not need it.

## Held-out predictions to flag, never design to

1. Whether "provenance of a rule" is expressible in the fixed rule menu at all, or whether it is a new attribute the menu does not have. This is the finding most likely to come out of the sitting, and it should be recorded as a **missing general primitive** — never as a bike-shaped or dive-shaped feature.
2. Whether a document is an object annnä holds or a fact it records having seen. The vault law (`../../security/SCENARIOS.md V1`) implies the first for a passport; the medical note may want the second.
