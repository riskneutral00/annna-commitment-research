# Brief — what a resource declares, and what that admits or refuses

**Readiness.** Ready when the founder opens a sitting on Situation C's qualification model. It is the largest single coverage subject the audit found and it needs no other condition.

**The debt this closes.** Sixteen owed beats in `../COVERAGE.md` — the **qualification and language matching** cluster (12), the **declared capability as a placement limit** cluster (3), and the hand-authored per-language copy beat (FD-56). They share one root question, which is why they share one brief.

**Why it is the largest gap.** `../../engine/SCENARIOS.md P4` refuses on a domain or attribute mismatch and is exemplified **only by address**. Grepping every suite for `language`, `rating`, `divemaster` or `certify` returns nothing. The corpus's whole multi-resource story rests on matching people to work by what they are qualified to do, and no scenario in any layer asserts that matching by name.

## World

Situation C, the Hug Ocean week. A customer needs a course taught in Mandarin. An instructor holds a Deep specialty and a divemaster does not. Somebody speaks a language but does not type it. A gas supplier offers three mixes and not a fourth. A gear shop has every piece but one size. Every one of these is the same shape: **something declared, and a placement that must respect the declaration.**

**Protagonist:** TingTing at Hug Ocean, presumptively — **to confirm.** **Multi-stakeholder**, matching Situation C.

## The question that governs the sitting

**Is a qualification a rule the operator writes, or a fact the platform checks?** The stories read both ways. TingTing declares her instructors' ratings — that is data. But *what a Divemaster rating permits* comes from an agency's standards document, not from her, and the story says the limit is "derived from loaded agency documentation rather than a hardcoded app rule." Ask which of those the founder means, because the answer decides whether this is one rule type or two.

## Actors — to be established

| Actor | Kind | To ask |
|---|---|---|
| TingTing | operator | Which qualifications does she actually track, and which does she carry in her head? |
| Instructors, divemasters | resources | Same object shape with different ratings, or different kinds? The story says the same shape — confirm it with her, not with the spec |
| The agency (PADI and its siblings) | document source, not a party | Whose document is loaded, who loads it, and what happens when it changes? |
| The customer | customer, off-app | Does the customer state a language, or is it inferred? |

## Rules and their source — to ask

- **Language:** the story treats spoken and written as separate axes ("Mandarin as speech-only"). Ask whether that split is real in her business or an artefact of the telling.
- **Ratings:** which rating opens which role — and does a rating ever *close* a role the person could otherwise fill?
- **Declared capability:** ask about the air mixes, the gear sizes, the boat that carries its own compressor. Is a capability the resource declares, or one the operator declares on the resource's behalf?
- **Per-language guest copy (FD-56):** the owner authors each ranked language's copy herself; no model translation call exists. Ask what that costs her and whether she would do it.

## The floor (today, without the app)

To ask: today, how does TingTing avoid putting a German student with an instructor who speaks no German, or a Deep course with somebody unrated for it? Memory? A whiteboard? A phone call to check? The floor is where the cost of getting it wrong shows up.

## Situations to cover — the questions, not the answers

**To elicit as must-work.** For each, ask for the concrete week-moment, not the rule:
- A customer placed with somebody who can actually teach them in their language.
- A course requiring a specialty rating reaching only somebody who holds it.
- A gas mix, a gear pack complete in every size, a boat whose own air satisfies the requirement.
- Somebody who speaks a language but cannot type it, still reached for a spoken need.

**To elicit as must-be-refused.** This half is the poka-yoke and is where the sitting earns its keep:
- What must annnä refuse to place, even when the resource is free? Ask her to name the placement that would frighten her.
- A Divemaster placed as a certifying instructor — refused, and what is the person told?
- A capability never declared, assigned anyway — what is the honest decline?
- **The decline that offers the next opening.** When one instructor holds the scarce rating and is busy, `Situation-C/situation-4.md` has annnä offer the next dates that person is actually free. `../../engine/SCENARIOS.md P2` asserts the honest decline itself and `../../engine/SCENARIOS.md A1` offers alternatives only for a capacity race — neither asserts this. Ask her what a *useful* no looks like when the answer is "not this week, but Thursday": is the alternative part of the refusal, or a second conversation?

## Held-out predictions to flag, never design to

1. Whether "loaded agency standards" is a document the platform reads or a set of rules somebody transcribes — the first is a new seam, the second is existing rule machinery, and the story does not say which.
2. Whether a rating is an attribute on a resource or an object in its own right with a validity window. Certifications expire; `../../app/SCENARIOS.md C11` shows expired certs going red, which hints at the second and specifies neither.
3. Whether language belongs to the *resource* or to the *commitment* — a course taught in Mandarin is a property of the work, not only of the worker.
