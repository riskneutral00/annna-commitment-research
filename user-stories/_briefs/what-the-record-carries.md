**Readiness.** Ready when the founder opens a sitting on what a finished course leaves behind.

**The debt this closes.** Four owed beats in `../COVERAGE.md` — the **certification written on course completion** cluster (2) and the **a cascade inherits the record** cluster (2).

**Why nothing covers it.** Grepping every suite for `certif` returns **nothing**: no layer names a certification write or a derived certification attribute anywhere. And `../../engine/SCENARIOS.md I1` with `../../harness/SCENARIOS.md C8` assert the offer-and-accept step of a cascade; neither asserts that the replacement arrives holding the history.

## World

Two moments in Situation C, and one question under both. A student finishes their required dives and is certified — `situation-1.md` says annnä records it. An instructor's offer lapses, the job cascades to Bear, and `situation-5.md` says Bear "opens the job and the whole course is already in it." In both, **something has accumulated on the commitment that outlives the sitting that made it.**

**Protagonist:** TingTing and her bench — **to confirm.** **Multi-stakeholder**, extending Situation C.

## The question that governs the sitting

**Is a certification a commitment, an attribute, or a document?** Each answer is a different product:
- a **commitment** makes it a thing with a date, a party and a ledger position;
- an **attribute** makes it a fact on a person that other placements then check (which is `qualification-and-language.md`'s subject, and the two briefs meet here);
- a **document** makes it something the agency owns and annnä merely holds a copy of.

Ask the founder which, in his own words. Do not offer the three as a menu of mechanisms — ask what actually happens in the shop when somebody certifies.

## Rules and their source — to ask

- What makes a course "complete"? Dives logged, an instructor's say-so, or a date passing?
- Who may write the certification, and what stops anyone else?
- **On the cascade:** what exactly travels — skills passed, dives logged, the customer's notes, the money marks? Ask what Bear must *not* receive, too; a replacement inheriting the customer's medical note is a quarantine question (`../../security/SCENARIOS.md V3`).

## The floor (today, without the app)

To ask: today, when an instructor hands a half-finished course to somebody else, what is the handover? A phone call? A paper log? Nothing, and the student repeats a dive? The floor is what tells us how much of "the whole course is already in it" is a real relief and how much is a nice sentence.

## Situations to cover — the questions, not the answers

**To elicit as must-work.**
- A course completing and the certification landing — where, visible to whom.
- Bear opening a cascaded job and finding it already populated: what is on the screen.

**To elicit as must-be-refused.**
- A certification written before the work is done — what stops it?
- A replacement seeing something the original instructor could see but they should not. Ask her to name it.
- Changing a completed course's terms afterwards. Changing the rules never rewrites the past, and a certification is the strongest possible "past".

## Held-out predictions to flag, never design to

1. Whether "the whole course is already in it" is an ordinary consequence of one shared record — in which case there is nothing to build and the beat is already true — or a real inheritance step. The corpus's own I1 law (one record, no mirrored copy) suggests the first; the story reads like the second. **This is the highest-value thing the sitting can settle.**
2. Whether a certification that expires makes every past placement retroactively questionable — the forward-only law says no, and nothing tests it against a credential rather than a price.
