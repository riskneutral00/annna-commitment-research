# Brief — what the guest sees before they commit, and never after

**Readiness.** Ready when the founder opens a sitting on the guest's own page. It touches Situations A, B and C, so it is one sitting across three `customer.md` files rather than three sittings.

**The debt this closes.** Four owed beats in `../COVERAGE.md`'s **the guest's pre-commitment view** cluster — from `Situation-A/customer.md`, `Situation-B/customer.md` and `Situation-C/customer.md`.

**Why nothing covers it.** `../../app/SCENARIOS.md G10` asserts the money and quota render on the **manage-state** page — the page a guest reaches *after* booking, holding their own token. Every story says the guest sees the price and their standing **before** they commit. That is a different page, and no scenario asserts it. Symmetrically, all three `customer.md` files carry a "what he must never see" list, and those lists are compound claims no single scenario asserts whole.

## World

Three off-app people, one shape. Bobby opens Sofia's link and sees the price and that he has used four of his ten hours this month. Tom opens Ploy's and sees a fleet with prices. Jun Wang opens the dive booking. In each, **the guest is deciding, and what they are shown is what they decide on.**

**Protagonist:** the guests — but a guest is never a user, so this is elicited from the **owners** about their guests. **To confirm the framing at the sitting.**

## The question that governs the sitting

**What does the owner want their customer to know before saying yes, and what would embarrass them if it leaked?** Both halves are the owner's judgement about their own business, which is why this is a founder-elicitation and not a design decision.

The second half is the sharper one. `Situation-B/customer.md`'s list is four separate claims — the traveller must never learn *which specific unit* he got, *that somebody else lost a race for it*, *the buffer timing after his return*, or *the deposit as anything other than "held"*. Ask which of those the owner actually cares about, because a leak nobody minds is not a refusal worth building.

## Rules and their source — to ask

- Is the price on the guest page the owner's choice to show, or always shown? A price the owner can hide is a different product.
- The running month — hours used, amount owed — is that the *guest's* information or the *owner's*? It is about the guest and it is owned by the owner, and the answer decides who may see it where.
- What is a guest owed *honestly* even when the honest answer is unflattering? Ask about the moment there is nothing available.

## The floor (today, without the app)

To ask: today, what does a customer know before they book? They ask on the phone and are told, or half-told, or told wrong. The floor here is mostly **asymmetry** — the operator knows and the customer does not — and the product's claim is that it closes that. Ask whether the owner *wants* it closed.

## Situations to cover — the questions, not the answers

**To elicit as must-work.**
- The guest's booking page before the booking: price terms, their own standing, what is genuinely available.
- The moment a guest realises they are at a cap. What are they told, and does it read as a refusal or as an insult?

**To elicit as must-be-refused.** The compound lists are the material; take them apart:
- Which specific resource was assigned — ask each owner whether they mind.
- That another customer lost a race for it.
- Anything about what happens after their own slot ends.
- Any substitution the owner made behind the scenes (Situation C's swapped instructor, pool, gear or boat).
- The owner's own life on the board (`../../app/SCENARIOS.md G1`'s wire test already covers the leak; ask what *else* belongs on that list).

## Held-out predictions to flag, never design to

1. Whether the pre-booking page and the manage page are one surface with two states or two surfaces — the corpus has scenarios for one of them and prose for both.
2. Whether "his month" is computable before he is identified. A guest arriving on a fresh link has no standing yet; the beat assumes the page knows who he is, and the token law may or may not grant that.
