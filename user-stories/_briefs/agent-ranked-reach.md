## World

An agent in Phuket sells dives. It owns no boats, no pools, no instructors — it owns customers and relationships. A booking arrives; it must reach a center that can take it, and if that one cannot, the next.

**Protagonist:** the agent (Phuket Travel, in the existing telling) — **to confirm.** **Multi-stakeholder.**

## Rules and their source — to ask

- Who sets the ranking, and does it change per booking? A ranking that changes by request is an optimization goal, not a standing rule — and the corpus has one precedent for that in Debra's direction-picked compaction.
- What makes a center "unable to take it" — capacity, qualification, or its own refusal? Each is a different decline.
- **Commission.** `Situation-C/situation-1.md` names Phuket Travel's commission. Money is tracked, never moved — ask what tracking a commission means when the booking crossed a tenant line, and whether `../../engine/SCENARIOS.md K5`'s one-record-on-both-boards shape covers three parties rather than two.

## The floor (today, without the app)

To ask: today the agent phones centers in order until one says yes. How many calls is a normal booking? What does the customer hear while it happens? The floor here is unusually informative, because the product's whole claim is that the calls it had to *prepare* become the prospects it converts.

## Situations to cover — the questions, not the answers

2. Whether an agent is a degenerate operator — one whose every resource is somebody else's — or a fourth party kind the model does not have.
