# Brief — one form, many kinds of operator

**Readiness.** Ready when the founder opens a sitting on how a dive center's own resources differ from a standalone supplier's — if they differ at all.

**The debt this closes.** Six owed beats in `../COVERAGE.md` — the **resource-form equivalence across operator kinds** cluster (4) and the **employment exclusivity** cluster (2).

**Why it matters more than it looks.** The claim under it is the corpus's central bet: that annnä has **general primitives**, not a dive product. Situation C's setup files say a dive center's owned boat is filled through "the exact same form a standalone operator fills" and produces the same stored object. If that is true, it is the strongest single piece of evidence for the bet — and **no scenario in any layer asserts it.** It is currently a claim the corpus makes about itself and never checks.

## World

Situation C has boats, pools, gear and air arriving two ways: owned by Hug Ocean, or owned by somebody who rents them out. The stories say the form is the same and the object is the same. They also say an employed instructor is "exclusive to one dive center" and never appears in the open freelance market's matching — which is a *difference* sitting right beside the sameness.

**Protagonist:** TingTing, and a standalone supplier — **to confirm.** **Multi-stakeholder.**

## The question that governs the sitting

**Where is the difference between owning a resource and hiring one — and is it a difference in the resource, or only in who holds the board?** The stories imply the second (same object, different owner). Employment exclusivity implies the first (an employed person is a different kind of reachable). Ask which, because they cannot both be the whole truth.

## The limit that binds this brief

**The freelance supplier's own board is out of bounds.** FD-68 holds the founder's own freelance life out as the product's first live test and forbids both eliciting it and drafting a fictional stand-in. This sitting may ask about the *employed* side and about what the center sees; it must not elicit the freelancer's own week. `../COVERAGE.md`'s "Setup-only, no run" table carries that limit and the FD-63 dogfood run is its probe.

## Rules and their source — to ask

- What does an operator declare about a resource, and is any of it different when they own it?
- **Exclusivity:** is "employed here" a rule on the resource, a grant on the board, or a consequence of who created it? Ask what happens the day somebody stops being employed there.
- Does an owned resource ever become reachable to somebody else — and is that the share seam, or something else?

## The floor (today, without the app)

To ask: today, does TingTing keep her own boat and a chartered boat in the same place in her head? Same book, same whiteboard? Or are they simply different problems? If they are different problems today and the same object in annnä, that is a gain worth the story showing.

## Situations to cover — the questions, not the answers

**To elicit as must-work.**
- The same form filled twice, by two kinds of operator, producing the same stored thing — and the moment where that sameness pays.
- An owned pool sitting first in the center's own ranked list: ask whether ownership *is* the ranking, or merely correlates with it.

**To elicit as must-be-refused.**
- An employed instructor surfacing in somebody else's ranked matching. What is the harm, in her words?
- One operator seeing another's inventory through a shared booking (`../../security/SCENARIOS.md N1`, `N2`).
- Ask what she would consider a *betrayal* of ownership. The refusals here are about trust, and trust answers are the ones a spec cannot invent.

## Held-out predictions to flag, never design to

1. Whether "employed" is expressible at all in the vocabulary of the atoms, or whether it is a job title in disguise — the OR-42 proposal already warns that job titles are not platform types, and the same warning applies here.
2. Whether resource-form equivalence, once asserted, is a *test* or a *tautology*: if both paths compile to the same object by construction, a scenario asserting it cannot fail. Ask what would have to be true for it to fail; if nothing would, say so and record the finding instead of writing the scenario.
