**Readiness.** Ready when FD-50's deferral of purchasable skins lifts, **or** when the founder authors the free-skin half. Either condition alone is enough; neither is a date.

## Rules and their source — to ask

- Is appearance the owner's own choice, or does something constrain it? The rest-state measurement (RQ-12) implies a constraint the *platform* holds, not the owner — ask what the founder thinks that constraint is protecting.
- If skins become purchasable, what is being sold — and does the answer change the refusal at the door?

## The floor (today, without the app)

To ask: what does an owner do today when the tool looks wrong to them? Live with it? Choose a different tool? The floor is what tells us whether appearance is a real want or a designed-in one.

## Situations to cover — the questions, not the answers

**To elicit as must-work.**
- The moment Sofia wants a different appearance — what triggers it, and what does she expect to happen?
- Applying a skin and living with it: what does she notice the next morning?

**To elicit as must-be-refused.** This is the half that matters most here, and it is exactly the owed beat:
- **A skin that fails the rest-state measurement is refused at the door (RQ-12).** Ask: what does Sofia see when that happens? Is she told what the skin did wrong? Does she believe the refusal, or does she think the app is broken? A refusal a user does not believe is a refusal that will be routed around.
- What appearance change would the founder want annnä to refuse even if the owner insisted?

## Held-out predictions to flag, never design to

1. Whether the rest state is measurable at install time at all, or only observable after the fact — the difference between a door and a monitor.
2. Whether entitlement revocation is visible to the owner as a *change in appearance* or as a notice; the E-family specifies the mechanism and no story says what it feels like.
